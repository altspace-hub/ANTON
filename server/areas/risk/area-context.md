# Risk Management Area Context

## Domain Landscape

The Risk Management area covers enterprise risk assessment, risk appetite framework design, operational risk management, scenario analysis, and third-party risk management. It serves Chief Risk Officers, risk managers, board risk committees, and advisors supporting risk governance in financial institutions and large corporates.

## Risk Management Frameworks

**Enterprise Risk Management:**
- COSO ERM Framework (2017) — Strategy, Objective-Setting, Performance, Review, Information/Communication
- ISO 31000:2018 — Risk Management Guidelines
- FERMA Risk Management Standard

**Financial Services Specific:**
- Basel Operational Risk Framework (standardised approach under Basel IV)
- EBA Guidelines on Internal Governance (governance of risk function)
- BCBS Principles for the Sound Management of Operational Risk (2011)
- FSB Principles for Effective Risk Data Aggregation and Risk Reporting (BCBS 239)
- SREP / ICAAP — supervisory review of risk management quality
- DORA — digital operational resilience (ICT risk subset of operational risk)

**Scenario Analysis:**
- Bank of England / EBA Climate Stress Testing frameworks
- BCBS stress testing principles
- Reverse stress testing requirements under supervisory frameworks

**Third-Party Risk:**
- EBA Guidelines on Outsourcing Arrangements (EBA/GL/2019/02)
- DORA ICT Third-Party Risk requirements (Articles 28-44)
- ECB Guide to assessment of internal models (for model risk)

## Risk Taxonomy

**Strategic Risk** — Risks arising from business decisions, market position, and competitive dynamics
**Financial Risk** — Credit risk, market risk, liquidity risk, interest rate risk
**Operational Risk** — People, process, systems, external events
**Compliance Risk** — Regulatory breach, enforcement action, fines
**Reputational Risk** — Media, social, stakeholder perception damage
**Emerging Risk** — Climate, geopolitical, technological disruption (AI, crypto), pandemic

## Key Terminology

- **Risk Appetite** — The amount of risk an organisation is willing to accept in pursuit of its objectives (board-set)
- **Risk Tolerance** — The acceptable variation around risk appetite (operational boundaries)
- **Inherent Risk** — Risk level before controls
- **Residual Risk** — Risk level after controls are applied
- **Key Risk Indicator (KRI)** — Leading metric that signals risk is trending toward the limit
- **Risk Register** — Documented inventory of identified risks with assessments and mitigations
- **Three Lines Model** — 1st line owns risk, 2nd line oversees, 3rd line provides independent assurance
- **Concentration Risk** — Excessive exposure to a single counterparty, geography, or sector
- **RCSA** — Risk and Control Self-Assessment (1st line process)

## Analytical Principles

When working in the Risk Management domain:
1. **Interconnected risk thinking** — Risks compound and cascade; the most dangerous risks are the ones that connect across categories
2. **Risk appetite as a constraint, not a target** — Operating at the limit of risk appetite signals poor risk culture
3. **Qualitative and quantitative balance** — Not all risk can be quantified; qualitative assessment is valid and necessary
4. **Living risk register** — A static register reviewed annually is a compliance exercise, not risk management
5. **Board ownership** — Risk appetite is a board responsibility, not a management document
6. **Forward-looking** — Risk management predicts and prevents; it does not just explain what went wrong
7. **Proportionality** — Risk management complexity should match the entity's risk profile and systemic importance
