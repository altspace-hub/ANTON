# Education & Learning Design Area Context

## Domain Landscape

The Education & Learning Design area supports educators, curriculum designers, instructional designers, and learning and development professionals in designing, building, and evaluating high-quality educational experiences. It covers K-12 and higher education settings as well as corporate L&D, spanning curriculum design, lesson planning, assessment development, e-learning production, and student feedback. The common thread is purposeful design: ensuring that learning experiences produce measurable, meaningful outcomes.

## Core Education Domains

**Curriculum Design:**
- Backward design (Understanding by Design — Wiggins & McTighe): start with desired outcomes, then design assessment and instruction
- Scope and sequence development: horizontal and vertical articulation across year groups and subjects
- Standards alignment: national curriculum frameworks, IB, IBDP, Cambridge IGCSE, Common Core (US)
- Cross-curricular and thematic curriculum design
- Competency-based curriculum development for vocational and professional contexts

**Lesson Planning:**
- Lesson structure models: direct instruction, inquiry-based learning, flipped classroom, project-based learning
- Learning objectives using Bloom's Taxonomy (remember, understand, apply, analyse, evaluate, create)
- Differentiation strategies: tiered tasks, scaffolding, extension activities
- Formative checkpoints: exit tickets, think-pair-share, cold calling, mini-whiteboards
- Managing diverse classrooms: SEND provision, EAL learners, gifted and talented

**Assessment Design:**
- Summative assessment: examination papers, coursework, portfolio assessment
- Formative assessment: low-stakes, feedback-rich, diagnostic
- Rubric and mark scheme development: analytical and holistic rubrics
- Assessment for Learning (AfL) principles
- Validity, reliability, and fairness in assessment design
- Moderation and standardisation processes

**E-Learning Design:**
- ADDIE model: Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation
- SAM (Successive Approximation Model) for iterative e-learning development
- Kirkpatrick Four Levels of Evaluation: reaction, learning, behaviour, results
- Storyboarding and script writing for instructional video and e-modules
- SCORM and xAPI standards for LMS integration
- Accessibility: WCAG 2.1, closed captions, screen reader compatibility
- Microlearning, scenario-based learning, gamification principles

**Student Feedback:**
- Feedback principles: specific, actionable, timely, forward-looking (not just evaluative)
- Written feedback on assignments: margin comments, summary feedback, feedforward
- Verbal feedback strategies: Socratic questioning, coaching conversations
- Peer and self-assessment design
- Using data to inform teaching: tracking progress, identifying underperformance patterns

## Key Frameworks and Standards

- **Bloom's Taxonomy (Revised)** — Cognitive, affective, and psychomotor learning domains
- **Universal Design for Learning (UDL)** — Multiple means of representation, action/expression, and engagement
- **Understanding by Design (UbD)** — Backward design: enduring understandings and essential questions
- **Kirkpatrick Model** — Four levels of training evaluation
- **ADDIE / SAM** — Systematic instructional design models for e-learning
- **SCORM 1.2 / SCORM 2004 / xAPI (Tin Can)** — E-learning interoperability standards

## Major Challenges

- Designing assessments that genuinely test higher-order thinking rather than rote recall
- Differentiating effectively for wide ability ranges without creating unsustainable workload
- Ensuring e-learning is genuinely interactive rather than passive slide decks
- Writing feedback that improves future performance, not just explains past marks
- Keeping curriculum current while maintaining standards coherence

## How the Modules Help

**Curriculum-design** guides the construction of coherent learning sequences from standards to assessment. **Lesson-planning** produces structured, differentiated lesson frameworks with activities, objectives, and formative checkpoints. **Assessment-builder** generates valid, reliable assessment instruments with accompanying mark schemes and rubrics. **E-learning-design** creates storyboards, scripts, and instructional frameworks for digital learning content. **Student-feedback** produces specific, growth-oriented written feedback on student work.

## Analytical Principles

1. **Start with the outcome** — Design backwards from what learners should know, do, or believe at the end
2. **Alignment is everything** — Objectives, instruction, and assessment must be tightly aligned; misalignment produces measurement failure
3. **Feedback is for learning, not grading** — The purpose of feedback is to improve future performance, not to justify a score
4. **Accessibility by design** — Inclusive design from the start is more effective than retrofitting accommodations
5. **Evaluation drives improvement** — Learning design is never finished; data from learner performance should drive iteration
