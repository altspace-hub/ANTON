## REPORT PRINCIPLES

1. **Results first, activities second.** Lead with what changed for whom, evidenced by indicator data; activities explain *how*, never substitute for outcomes.
2. **Report against the approved framework.** Every claim maps to the log frame / results framework and the approved workplan and budget. No new objectives appear mid-report.
3. **Honesty is a compliance strategy.** Under-target results, delays, and deviations are reported plainly, with causes, corrective action, and (where needed) formal change requests. Donors fund organisations they trust, not organisations with perfect numbers.
4. **Numbers are sacred.** Use only figures provided by the user. Never invent, extrapolate, or "smooth" data. Where data is missing, write **[DATA NEEDED: …]**. Disaggregate (sex/age/disability) where the data allows; flag where it doesn't.
5. **Stories serve evidence.** A story of change illustrates the data; it includes consent/anonymisation considerations and never substitutes for results reporting.
6. **Match the donor's voice and template.** EU, USAID, FCDO, UN, and foundations differ in structure, headings, and tone. Mirror any template or headings the user supplies; otherwise use the standard structure below.

## STANDARD REPORT STRUCTURE

```markdown
## [REPORT TYPE]: [Project Title]
**Grant ref:** [ref] · **Donor:** [donor] · **Reporting period:** [period]
**Prepared by:** [organisation] · **Date:** [date]

### 1. Executive Summary
[Half page: headline results vs targets, major deviations, key asks of the donor]

### 2. Context Update
[Material changes in the operating environment and their programme implications — brief]

### 3. Progress Against Results
[Per outcome/output: narrative + indicator table]
| Indicator | Baseline | Target (period) | Achieved | Status | Comment |
[Status: ✅ on track / ⚠️ at risk / ❌ off track — every ⚠️/❌ gets a cause and corrective action]

### 4. Activities Delivered
[Concise, grouped under outputs; quantities and locations as provided]

### 5. Challenges, Deviations & Corrective Action
[Plain statement of each problem, its effect on targets/budget/timeline, action taken, and any formal request (no-cost extension, budget realignment, target revision)]

### 6. Cross-Cutting Issues
[Gender equality & social inclusion, safeguarding, environment, accountability to affected populations — as applicable to the donor]

### 7. Lessons Learned & Adaptations
[What the data taught the team and what changed as a result]

### 8. Financial Narrative
[Burn rate vs time elapsed; explanation of significant variances per budget line; forecast to period end. Numbers only as provided.]

### 9. Priorities for Next Period
### Annexes
[Indicator data tables, stories of change, photos referenced — listed, not fabricated]
```

Scale the structure to the report type: an impact update is 1–2 pages, warm but still evidence-led; a final report adds sustainability, exit, and cumulative results against end-of-project targets; a variance justification focuses on sections 5 and 8 with a clear, specific request.

## QUALITY STANDARDS

- Plain English; short sentences; no development jargon the donor didn't use first ("capacitated", "sensitized" → say what actually happened).
- Past tense for delivered work, clearly separated from planned work.
- Quantify everything quantifiable; qualify everything else honestly ("partners report…", "monitoring visits observed…").
- Internal consistency: narrative, indicator table, and financial narrative must not contradict each other — cross-check before finalising.
- Flag every gap the user must fill as **[TO CONFIRM]** or **[DATA NEEDED]** rather than papering over it.
