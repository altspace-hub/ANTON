## Rotational Grazing — The Core Concept

Rotational grazing is the single most effective land management practice available to small-scale farmers. The principle is simple: divide your land into 2–4 sections (paddocks). Graze animals in one section, then move them to the next. The grazed section rests and grows back before animals return.

**Why it works:** Continuous grazing on the same land kills grass before it can recover. Rested grass grows back stronger, deeper-rooted, and more nutritious. Well-managed rotational grazing can double the amount of feed a piece of land produces.

**How to start with two paddocks:**
1. Put up a simple fence (posts and wire or thorn bushes) to divide your land in half.
2. Graze animals in Paddock 1 until grass is about 10 cm high.
3. Move animals to Paddock 2. Let Paddock 1 rest for 3–4 weeks.
4. Return to Paddock 1 when grass is 25–30 cm tall.

This costs almost nothing except fencing materials and discipline.

## Stocking Rate

Overstocking is the most common cause of pasture destruction. A rough guide: 1 cattle unit per 2–5 hectares depending on rainfall and grass quality. In dry areas, more land per animal is needed. If your grass is always short and bare, you have too many animals for your land — either reduce numbers or improve pasture quality with improved grasses.

## Improved Pasture Grasses

**Napier grass (Elephant grass):** Best for East and West Africa. Grows fast, high yield, suitable for cut-and-carry. Establish from cuttings — ask your extension officer or neighbour for material. Can produce 50+ tonnes of green matter per hectare per year.

**Brachiaria (Signal grass, Mulato):** Drought-tolerant, good for dry areas. Better for grazing than Napier. Establishes from seed. Suitable for Southern and East Africa.

**Rhodes grass:** Excellent for hay production. Suitable for drier areas of East Africa.

## Dry Season Planning

Feed conservation saves your herd during drought:
- **Hay making:** Cut grass at peak quality (before flowering), dry in sun for 3–5 days, store under cover. Simple and effective for small scale.
- **Crop residues storage:** Store maize stover, sorghum straw, and legume tops after harvest — do not burn them.
- **Silage:** Fermented feed preserved in bags or pits — better nutrition than hay, requires a bit more skill. Seek training from extension officer.

## Water Point Management

Do not overgraze land immediately around water points — animals spend too long there and destroy the grass. Move portable water troughs to different locations, or water by hand if practical. This one change protects the most heavily used ground on your farm.

## Zero-Grazing (Cut-and-Carry)

For farmers with small plots or peri-urban land: cut grass and bring it to housed animals. This eliminates overgrazing completely, reduces parasite burden (worms from contaminated pasture), and is compatible with small plots. Requires more labour but produces better results per square metre of land.

**When to choose zero-grazing:** Plot under 0.5 hectares, peri-urban location, high land cost, or severe overgrazing problem.

Rotational grazing can double your land's carrying capacity. It costs nothing except fencing and discipline — start with just two paddocks.
