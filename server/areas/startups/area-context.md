# Startups & Early-Stage Ventures Area Context

## Domain Landscape

The Startups & Early-Stage Ventures area serves founders, entrepreneurs, early-stage teams, and startup advisors building new companies from idea through to Series A funding. It covers business planning, MVP scoping and product-market fit, pitch deck creation, funding strategy, and co-founder agreements and equity structuring. The domain spans the messy reality of early company building — where resources are scarce, uncertainty is high, and the quality of early decisions compounds into the long-term trajectory of the business.

## Core Startup Domains

**Business Planning:**
- Lean Canvas (Ash Maurya): one-page business model focused on problem, solution, unique value proposition, unfair advantage, customer segments, early adopters, key metrics, channels, cost structure, revenue streams
- Business Model Canvas (Osterwalder): nine-block business model visualisation
- Jobs To Be Done (JTBD): what functional, social, and emotional job is the customer hiring the product to do?
- Market sizing: TAM (Total Addressable Market), SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market), SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market) — bottom-up and top-down methods
- Unit economics: CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost), LTV (Lifetime Value), LTV:CAC ratio, payback period, gross margin
- Financial projections for startups: revenue model assumptions, hiring plan, runway modelling

**MVP Scoping and Product-Market Fit:**
- Lean Startup methodology (Ries): Build-Measure-Learn loop, validated learning, pivot vs. persevere
- MVP definition: the minimum viable experiment to test the riskiest assumption — not the minimum feature set
- Assumption mapping: separating desirability, feasibility, and viability risks
- Product-market fit signals: Sean Ellis test (>40% "very disappointed"), retention curves, NPS, qualitative pull signals
- Prioritisation frameworks: RICE (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort), MoSCoW, Kano model
- Customer discovery: problem interviews, solution interviews, Jobs To Be Done interviews

**Pitch Deck Creation:**
- Sequoia pitch structure: company purpose, problem, solution, why now, market size, product, business model, traction, team, the ask
- Y Combinator pitch principles: clarity, honesty, demonstrable traction, a single compelling narrative
- Common pitch deck mistakes: burying the lede, weak team slide, unrealistic financial projections, unclear use of funds
- Narrative arc: problem acuity → insight → solution → evidence → opportunity size → why us → ask
- Investor-specific tailoring: seed vs. Series A expectations, VC vs. angel audience calibration

**Funding Strategy:**
- Funding stages: pre-seed (£50k-500k), seed (£500k-2M), Series A (£2M-10M+)
- Instrument types: SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity — Y Combinator), KISS, convertible notes vs. priced equity rounds
- UK-specific: EIS (Enterprise Investment Scheme), SEIS (Seed Enterprise Investment Scheme) — investor tax relief implications for funding strategy
- Investor types: friends and family, angels, syndicates, accelerators (YC, Seedcamp, Entrepreneur First), early-stage VCs
- Cap table management: pre-money vs. post-money valuation, dilution modelling, option pool shuffle
- Term sheet key terms: liquidation preferences, anti-dilution provisions, pro-rata rights, information rights, board composition

**Co-founder Agreements and Equity:**
- Equity split frameworks: equal vs. contribution-weighted splits, founder vesting schedules (4-year vest, 1-year cliff)
- Shareholder agreement key provisions: good/bad leaver clauses, drag-along, tag-along, pre-emption rights, IP assignment
- IP assignment: ensuring all founder IP is assigned to the company at formation
- Director duties and fiduciary obligations (UK Companies Act 2006)
- Co-founder separation: buyout mechanics, share valuation disputes, cliff and vesting protection

## Key Frameworks and Concepts

- **Lean Startup (Eric Ries)** — Build-Measure-Learn: validated learning over assumption-based execution
- **Business Model Canvas / Lean Canvas** — Visual business model design tools
- **SAFE Notes** — Y Combinator's standard instrument for early-stage investment
- **EIS/SEIS** — UK government-backed investor tax relief schemes for startup investment
- **Founder Vesting** — 4-year vesting with 1-year cliff: the industry standard for founder equity protection
- **LTV:CAC Ratio** — The fundamental unit economics metric for scalable business models

## Major Challenges

- Avoiding premature scaling before validating product-market fit
- Structuring co-founder equity in a way that is fair and durable under stress
- Building a pitch narrative that is compelling to investors who see hundreds of decks
- Understanding which funding instrument and investor type is right for the current stage
- Modelling realistic financial projections that are credible without being conservative to the point of underwhelming

## Analytical Principles

1. **Test assumptions before building** — Every startup is a bundle of assumptions; test the riskiest ones first at minimum cost
2. **Traction talks** — Evidence of customer demand and engagement is worth more than any projection in a pitch
3. **Founder equity is a long-term commitment** — Co-founder arrangements must be designed to survive disagreement and adversity
4. **Understand your investors** — Different investor types have different return expectations, time horizons, and value-add; match them deliberately
5. **Runway is survival** — Cash management and burn rate management are existential; every financing decision must extend optionality
