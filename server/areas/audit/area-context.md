# Audit & Assurance Area Context

## Domain Landscape

The Audit & Assurance area covers internal audit planning and execution, control testing design, audit findings documentation, assurance reporting, and regulatory examination preparation. It serves internal audit functions, compliance testing teams, and external assurance providers working in financial services.

## Professional Standards

**International Internal Audit Standards:**
- IIA International Standards for the Professional Practice of Internal Auditing (ISPPIA) — 2024 Global Internal Audit Standards
- IIA Three Lines Model (2020)
- IIA Practice Guides and Position Papers

**External Audit and Assurance Standards:**
- ISAE 3402 — Assurance Reports on Controls at a Service Organisation (Type I and Type II)
- ISAE 3000 — Assurance Engagements Other Than Audits or Reviews of Historical Financial Information
- SOC 1 / SOC 2 — System and Organisation Controls reports
- PCAOB Standards (for US-listed entities)
- ISA (International Standards on Auditing)

**Control Frameworks:**
- COSO Internal Control — Integrated Framework (2013)
- COSO Enterprise Risk Management Framework (2017)
- COBIT 2019 (IT governance and management)
- ISO 27001 (Information Security Management)
- NIST Cybersecurity Framework

**Financial Services Regulatory Expectations:**
- EBA Guidelines on Internal Governance (EBA/GL/2021/05)
- EBA Guidelines on ICT and Security Risk Management
- Basel Committee: Principles for the Sound Management of Operational Risk
- Supervisory review and evaluation processes (SREP) — includes review of internal audit function quality

## Key Terminology

- **Three Lines Model** — 1st Line (business operations), 2nd Line (risk/compliance oversight), 3rd Line (internal audit assurance)
- **Control Environment** — The foundation of the control system: tone at the top, governance, policies
- **Attribute testing** — Testing whether a control attribute is present (yes/no); used for frequency and completeness
- **Walk-through** — Follow one transaction end-to-end to understand the process; not sampling
- **Root cause** — The fundamental reason a control failed, not just the symptom
- **Management Action Plan (MAP)** — Management's response to a finding, including remediation action and timeline
- **Ageing** — How long a finding or action has been open; regulatory concern when findings remain open

## Analytical Principles

When working in the Audit & Assurance domain:
1. **Risk-based approach** — Allocate audit effort proportional to risk, not to completeness of coverage
2. **Independence** — Audit cannot audit what it helps design; three lines must remain distinct
3. **Evidence-based** — Every finding must be supported by specific evidence; conclusions must follow from evidence
4. **Root cause over symptom** — Fixing symptoms without addressing root cause leads to repeat findings
5. **The "so what" test** — Every finding must articulate business/regulatory impact, not just describe the gap
6. **No surprises principle** — Management should not be surprised by a finding in the final report
7. **Proportionality** — Scope, methodology, and sample sizes should be appropriate for the risk level
