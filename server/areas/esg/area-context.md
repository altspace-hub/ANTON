# Environment, Sustainability & ESG — Area Context

## Domain Overview

This area covers sustainability reporting, environmental risk analysis, ESG strategy, and climate risk management. The dominant regulatory driver in the EU is the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) and its European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS), which represent the most significant expansion of corporate reporting obligations in a generation.

## Regulatory Framework

### CSRD — Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (2022/2464/EU)
Mandatory sustainability reporting for large and listed EU companies. Replaces the Non-Financial Reporting Directive (NFRD).

**Scope and timeline:**
- FY 2024 (reporting in 2025): Large listed companies already subject to NFRD (~1,000 companies)
- FY 2025 (reporting in 2026): All other large companies (>250 employees, >€40M turnover, >€20M balance sheet — meeting 2 of 3)
- FY 2026 (reporting in 2027): Listed SMEs (with opt-out until 2028)
- FY 2028 (reporting in 2029): Non-EU companies with significant EU turnover (>€150M)

### ESRS — European Sustainability Reporting Standards
Issued by EFRAG, adopted by European Commission. The actual disclosure requirements.

**Universal standards (all entities):**
- **ESRS 1** — General Requirements (principles, drafting, materiality, value chain)
- **ESRS 2** — General Disclosures (governance, strategy, impact/risk/opportunity management, metrics/targets)

**Topical standards (apply if material):**
- **E1** — Climate Change (mitigation, adaptation, energy)
- **E2** — Pollution (air, water, soil, light, noise, substances)
- **E3** — Water and Marine Resources
- **E4** — Biodiversity and Ecosystems
- **E5** — Resource Use and Circular Economy
- **S1** — Own Workforce (working conditions, equal treatment, rights)
- **S2** — Workers in the Value Chain
- **S3** — Affected Communities
- **S4** — Consumers and End-users
- **G1** — Business Conduct (anti-corruption, whistleblowing, lobbying, supplier relationships)

### Double Materiality — The Core Concept
Unlike financial reporting (which only asks "how does the world affect the company?"), ESRS requires assessment from BOTH directions:

1. **Impact materiality** — What is the company's impact on people and the environment? (outward)
2. **Financial materiality** — How do sustainability matters affect the company's financial performance and position? (inward)

A topic is material if it meets EITHER threshold — not both. This is a fundamental departure from traditional financial materiality.

### EU Taxonomy Regulation (2020/852)
Classification system for sustainable economic activities. Activities assessed against:
- **Substantial contribution** to at least one of six environmental objectives
- **Do No Significant Harm (DNSH)** to the other five objectives
- Compliance with **Minimum Social Safeguards** (ILO core conventions, OECD guidelines)

Financial institutions must disclose their **Green Asset Ratio (GAR)** — proportion of assets aligned with the Taxonomy.

### SFDR — Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation
For investment firms and asset managers:
- Entity-level disclosures on sustainability risks (Article 3)
- Product-level disclosures: Article 6 (no sustainability claim), Article 8 (ESG characteristics), Article 9 (sustainable investment objective)
- PAI (Principal Adverse Impacts) statement

### Climate-Related Disclosures
- **TCFD** — Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (now embedded in ESRS E1)
- **Physical risks** — Risks from climate change impacts (flooding, heat, drought, sea level rise)
- **Transition risks** — Risks from the shift to a low-carbon economy (policy, technology, market, reputational)
- **Scope 1, 2, 3 emissions** — Direct, indirect energy, and value chain greenhouse gas emissions

## Key Process: Double Materiality Assessment

The DMA is the foundation of all CSRD reporting. It determines which ESRS standards apply and to what extent.

**Steps:**
1. **Context mapping** — Business model, value chain, geographies, stakeholder groups
2. **Universe of sustainability topics** — Comprehensive list from ESRS 1 Appendix AR 16
3. **Stakeholder engagement** — Understanding affected stakeholders' concerns
4. **Impact assessment** — Severity (scale, scope, irremediability) × Likelihood for negative; significance for positive
5. **Financial assessment** — Financial risks and opportunities from sustainability matters
6. **Materiality determination** — Qualitative and quantitative thresholds
7. **Documentation and review** — Audit trail for assurance purposes

## Assurance Requirements

- CSRD mandates **limited assurance** from the outset (attestation that report is reasonable)
- Pathway to **reasonable assurance** (audit-equivalent) from 2028 as standards develop
- Internal controls over sustainability information are now essential — analogous to ICFR (Internal Controls over Financial Reporting)

## Common Implementation Challenges

1. **Data availability** — Scope 3 emissions data, value chain data, biodiversity metrics often do not exist in usable form
2. **Data quality** — Sustainability data was historically unaudited; bringing it to financial reporting standards takes significant effort
3. **Organisational complexity** — Data ownership spans multiple functions: HR, procurement, facilities, finance, legal, risk
4. **Value chain reach** — Understanding suppliers' and customers' sustainability practices
5. **Estimation methodologies** — Many data points will require estimation; methodology must be documented and defensible
6. **Assurance readiness** — Controls, documentation, and audit trails must be established before year-end
