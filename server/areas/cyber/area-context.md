# Cybersecurity & Information Security — Area Context

## Domain Overview

This area covers digital operational resilience, ICT risk management, cybersecurity assessment, and regulatory compliance for financial institutions navigating an increasingly complex cyber threat landscape. The primary regulatory driver is DORA (Digital Operational Resilience Act), alongside ISO 27001, NIST CSF, and sector-specific EBA/ESMA guidelines.

## Regulatory Framework

### DORA — Digital Operational Resilience Act (EU 2022/2554)
The central regulation for financial sector cyber resilience. Applies from January 17, 2025 to all financial entities regulated under EU financial law.

**Five pillars:**
1. **ICT Risk Management** (Articles 5–16) — Board-approved ICT risk framework, governance, asset management, detection, response, recovery, communication, and learning.
2. **ICT Incident Management, Classification & Reporting** (Articles 17–23) — Incident classification criteria (based on EBA RTS), major incident reporting to NCA within 4/72/1-month windows.
3. **Digital Operational Resilience Testing** (Articles 24–27) — Basic testing (annual) for all entities; Threat-Led Penetration Testing (TLPT) every 3 years for significant entities.
4. **ICT Third-Party Risk Management** (Articles 28–44) — Contractual requirements, Register of Information, Oversight Framework for Critical ICT Third-Party Providers.
5. **Information Sharing** (Article 45) — Voluntary cyber threat intelligence sharing arrangements.

### Key DORA RTS/ITS
- RTS on ICT risk management tools, methods, processes (EBA/ESAs)
- RTS on major incident classification criteria
- RTS on TLPT requirements
- RTS on Register of Information (contractual arrangements with ICT third parties)
- ITS on incident reporting templates

### Other Key Standards
- **ISO/IEC 27001:2022** — Information security management systems
- **ISO/IEC 27002:2022** — Security controls guidance
- **NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0** — Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover + Govern
- **NIST SP 800-53** — Security and privacy controls catalog
- **EBA Guidelines on ICT and Security Risk Management** (EBA/GL/2019/04)
- **ECB Cyber Resilience Oversight Expectations (CROE)** — for financial market infrastructures
- **ENISA frameworks** — Threat landscape reports, good practices guides

## Key Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| ICT | Information and Communication Technology |
| TLPT | Threat-Led Penetration Testing — red team exercise simulating real-world threat actors |
| CITP | Critical ICT Third-Party Provider — designated by ESAs, subject to direct oversight |
| RTO/RPO | Recovery Time Objective / Recovery Point Objective |
| BCP/DRP | Business Continuity Plan / Disaster Recovery Plan |
| SIEM | Security Information and Event Management |
| SOC | Security Operations Centre |
| IAM | Identity and Access Management |
| RBAC | Role-Based Access Control |
| Penetration testing | Authorised simulated cyber attack to identify vulnerabilities |
| Vulnerability management | Systematic process of identifying, assessing, and remediating security weaknesses |
| Concentration risk | Risk from over-reliance on a single ICT provider across the financial sector |
| Cyber threat intelligence | Information about existing or emerging threats to ICT assets |

## Common Deficiencies in Financial Institutions

1. **Governance gaps** — ICT risk not at board agenda; no dedicated ICT risk function separate from IT operations
2. **Incomplete asset inventory** — No authoritative record of all ICT assets, particularly cloud and shadow IT
3. **Weak third-party oversight** — Contracts lacking DORA mandatory provisions; no register of information; no exit strategies
4. **Testing maturity** — Penetration testing infrequent, not threat-led, findings not tracked to remediation
5. **Incident management** — Classification thresholds not defined; reporting chains not clear; lessons learned not documented
6. **Recovery testing** — BCP/DRP untested; RTOs aspirational rather than verified
7. **Data classification** — No formal classification scheme; inconsistent encryption and access controls

## Supervisory Focus Areas (2025–2026)

- DORA implementation verification — all five pillars
- ICT third-party concentration risk — especially cloud providers (AWS, Azure, Google)
- Cyber incident reporting quality and timeliness
- TLPT coverage for systemically important institutions
- Operational resilience of critical functions during IT failures
- AI and GenAI governance within ICT risk frameworks

## Proportionality

DORA applies proportionally. "Simplified ICT risk management" framework available for smaller institutions (microenterprises, small/non-interconnected investment firms, small payment institutions). However, the basic obligations (incident reporting, third-party contracts, basic testing) apply to virtually all entities.
