# Design (UX/UI/Service) — Area Context

Design is the discipline of creating products, services, and experiences that meet human needs. It encompasses user experience (UX) design, user interface (UI) design, and service design — each requiring structured research, analysis, and documentation.

## Core disciplines

**UX Design:** Understanding user needs, behaviours, and pain points through research. Translating insights into wireframes, prototypes, and specifications. Validating solutions through usability testing.

**UI Design:** Creating visual design systems, component libraries, and interface patterns. Ensuring consistency, accessibility, and brand coherence across digital products.

**Service Design:** Mapping the end-to-end service experience across all touchpoints — digital and physical. Aligning front-stage interactions with back-stage processes and systems.

## Design thinking process

1. **Empathise** — Understand users through research (interviews, observation, surveys)
2. **Define** — Frame the problem clearly with user-centred problem statements
3. **Ideate** — Generate and explore potential solutions broadly
4. **Prototype** — Build low-fidelity representations of solutions
5. **Test** — Validate with real users, iterate based on findings

## Key artefacts

- Research plans and discussion guides
- Usability audit reports with severity ratings
- Information architecture diagrams and card sort results
- Service blueprints with swimlanes (user, front-stage, back-stage, support)
- Design system documentation (tokens, components, patterns, accessibility)

## Design quality standards

Good design is: **useful** (solves a real problem), **usable** (easy to use without instruction), **desirable** (people want to use it), **findable** (content is navigable and locatable), **accessible** (works for all abilities), **credible** (users trust it), **valuable** (delivers business value).

## Accessibility requirements

WCAG 2.1 AA is the minimum for most products. Level AAA for public sector or healthcare. Key principles: perceivable, operable, understandable, robust (POUR). Always test with assistive technology users.
