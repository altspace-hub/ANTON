# Consumer Protection Expert — Area Context

## Most Consumers Don't Know They Have Rights

In most developing countries, consumer protection laws exist and are surprisingly comprehensive. The problem is not the law — it is awareness. A market vendor in Lagos who sells you an expired product has broken the law. A mobile money agent in Nairobi who pockets your transfer has committed fraud. A bank in Mumbai that charges you undisclosed fees has violated regulation. But without knowing your rights, knowing how to complain, and knowing who handles that complaint, the law offers nothing practical.

## The Landscape of Consumer Harm

The most common and highest-impact consumer problems across low- and middle-income markets fall into five categories:

- **Defective and counterfeit products** — expired food, fake medicine, non-functional electronics, and imitation goods that are dangerous or simply worthless. Return and refund policies are often non-existent in practice at informal market stalls, even where law requires them.
- **Mobile money disputes** — the fastest-growing category. Wrong-number transfers, failed transactions, SIM-swap fraud, and unauthorised withdrawals. Mobile money platforms process billions of dollars daily for people who have no other banking option. Disputes are time-sensitive — funds can be irretrievable within hours.
- **Financial service opacity** — undisclosed bank fees, loan terms that change after signing, interest rates presented in confusing ways, and accounts closed without explanation. Microfinance and mobile lending have extended credit to millions but also extended predatory practices.
- **Scams and fraud** — advance-fee fraud, prize scams, pyramid investment schemes, romance scams, and job-offer fraud. These are not new, but the internet and mobile phones have dramatically scaled them. Victims are disproportionately people who can least afford the loss.
- **Government service failures** — unreasonable delays, demands for unofficial payments, denied services without explanation, and outright corruption. Citizens in most countries have legal rights to timely, non-discriminatory government services that are almost never enforced by individuals who don't know those rights exist.

## Enforcement: Honest About the Gaps

Consumer protection enforcement varies enormously. South Africa has a functional National Consumer Commission. India has a three-tier Consumer Disputes Redressal structure that handles millions of cases. Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana, Tanzania, and Bangladesh all have consumer protection legislation and designated bodies — but these bodies are often under-resourced, geographically concentrated in capital cities, and difficult to access for rural or informal consumers. Mobile money regulation is generally stronger than other areas because telecoms regulators have direct leverage over platform operators.
