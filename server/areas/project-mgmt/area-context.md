# Project Management & Delivery — Area Context

## Domain Overview

This area covers the planning, execution, and governance of projects and programmes, with particular focus on regulatory change programmes, technology implementations, and business transformation in financial services. Projects in this context are often complex, politically sensitive, and subject to regulatory scrutiny — which makes professional project management essential, not optional.

## Key Methodologies

### Waterfall / Linear Delivery
Sequential phases: Initiation → Planning → Execution → Monitoring → Closure. Best for well-defined regulatory compliance projects where scope is clear and requirements are stable. Common in regulatory implementation programmes where the regulation defines what must be done.

### Agile / Iterative
Sprints, backlogs, incremental delivery. Best for technology build projects where requirements evolve. Increasingly used in regulatory programmes to deliver value incrementally rather than waiting for a "big bang" go-live.

### PRINCE2
Structured project management methodology widely used in UK and Nordic public sector and financial services. Key principles: continued business justification, learn from experience, defined roles and responsibilities, manage by stages, manage by exception, focus on products, tailor to suit the project environment.

### PMI / PMBOK
Process-based approach covering: integration, scope, schedule, cost, quality, resource, communications, risk, procurement, and stakeholder management. The PMP certification is globally recognised.

### MSP (Managing Successful Programmes)
For complex, multi-project programmes with transformational change. Focuses on programme governance, benefits realisation, and stakeholder management.

## Key Project Artefacts

| Artefact | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| **Project Charter / Initiation Document** | Authorises the project, defines high-level scope, objectives, and sponsor |
| **Business Case** | Justifies the investment — costs, benefits, risks, alternatives |
| **Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)** | Hierarchical decomposition of all project work |
| **Project Schedule (Gantt)** | Timeline with tasks, dependencies, milestones, and resource assignments |
| **RAID Log** | Risks, Assumptions, Issues, Dependencies — tracked and owned |
| **Status Report** | Regular update: progress, risks, issues, decisions needed |
| **Change Request** | Formal process for scope, timeline, or budget changes |
| **Lessons Learned Register** | Captured throughout and reviewed at close |
| **Benefits Realisation Plan** | How and when benefits will be measured and who is accountable |

## Regulatory Change Project Specifics

Financial institutions face unique challenges in regulatory implementation:

1. **Non-negotiable deadline** — The regulation comes into force regardless of project readiness
2. **Regulatory scrutiny** — Supervisors may ask for programme documentation and progress reports
3. **Cross-functional complexity** — Regulatory change affects compliance, IT, operations, legal, risk, finance, and business lines simultaneously
4. **Dependency hell** — Often dependent on regulatory body issuing final Level 2 text, EBA guidelines, Q&As
5. **Scope uncertainty** — Early planning must manage incomplete regulatory requirements
6. **Multiple parallel programmes** — DORA, AMLR, CSRD, Basel IV often running simultaneously, competing for the same resources

## Resource Estimation in Financial Services

A persistent failure mode is underestimating resource requirements:

- **BAU absorption** — Compliance teams are often already at capacity; project work competes with BAU
- **Hidden resource needs** — IT, data, HR, legal, comms, training all need project time
- **External support** — Regulatory complexity often requires specialist consultants
- **Senior time** — Senior stakeholder engagement is high-touch and time-consuming; often underbudgeted

Rule of thumb: A regulatory implementation programme that appears to need 5 FTEs will actually need 8–10 FTEs when all workstreams are fully mapped.

## Governance Structures

### Steering Committee
- Senior sponsorship with decision authority
- Meets at least monthly (fortnightly for critical phases)
- Receives executive status report; escalated issues; key decisions
- Can approve budget changes, scope adjustments, timeline extensions

### Project Management Office (PMO)
- Consistency of approach across workstreams
- Consolidated reporting
- Dependency management between workstreams
- Standards, templates, quality assurance

### Workstream Leads
- Technical ownership of their domain (Legal, IT, Compliance, Operations, etc.)
- Responsible for detailed planning and execution within their workstream
- Attend project team meetings; escalate to PM/Steering as needed

## Critical Success Factors

1. **Executive sponsorship** with genuine authority and engagement
2. **Clear scope** with documented what is and is NOT included
3. **Realistic planning** — time for testing, training, and embedding, not just build
4. **Dedicated resources** — partial allocation to a project rarely works
5. **Risk-based prioritisation** — focus on critical path and highest-risk areas first
6. **Early stakeholder engagement** — change management starts from day one, not month six
7. **Regular cadence** — weekly team meetings, monthly steering, clear escalation path
