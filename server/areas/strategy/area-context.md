# Strategy & Business Development — Area Context

## Domain Overview

This area covers strategic analysis, business case development, market assessment, and competitive intelligence for organisations making significant investment or directional decisions. In financial services, strategy work is increasingly intertwined with regulatory change — new regulations reshape competitive dynamics, create new market opportunities, and require strategic responses that go beyond pure compliance.

## Strategy Frameworks

### Porter's Five Forces
Competitive intensity analysis:
1. **Threat of new entrants** — Barriers to entry (regulatory, capital, technology, brand)
2. **Bargaining power of suppliers** — Technology vendors, data providers, regulatory bodies
3. **Bargaining power of buyers** — Customer switching costs, price sensitivity, alternatives
4. **Threat of substitutes** — Crypto, embedded finance, BigTech financial services
5. **Competitive rivalry** — Number and capability of existing competitors

### SWOT Analysis
Internal: Strengths, Weaknesses
External: Opportunities, Threats
Used for strategic options generation — match strengths to opportunities, address weaknesses that amplify threats.

### PESTLE Analysis
External environment scanning:
- **Political** — Regulatory trends, political stability, government priorities
- **Economic** — Interest rates, credit cycles, GDP growth, inflation
- **Social** — Demographics, changing consumer behaviours, trust in institutions
- **Technological** — AI, cloud, open banking, DLT, quantum computing
- **Legal** — Regulation pipeline, enforcement trends, litigation risk
- **Environmental** — Climate transition, physical risks, ESG expectations

### Ansoff Matrix
Strategic growth options:
- **Market Penetration** — More of the same product in the same market
- **Market Development** — Existing products in new markets
- **Product Development** — New products for existing markets
- **Diversification** — New products in new markets (highest risk)

### Blue Ocean Strategy
Create uncontested market space rather than competing in existing "red oceans." Identify: Eliminate/Reduce factors taken for granted; Raise/Create factors that deliver new value.

## Business Case Methodology

### Options Analysis (always present at least three options)
- **Option 0 / Do Nothing** — What is the cost of inaction? (Often the best argument for action)
- **Option A — Minimum Viable** — What is the smallest investment that addresses the core problem?
- **Option B — Recommended** — The balanced, proportionate approach
- **Option C — Ambitious** — What could be achieved with full investment?

### Financial Modelling
- **NPV (Net Present Value)** — Present value of future cash flows minus investment; positive NPV = value creation
- **IRR (Internal Rate of Return)** — Rate of return that makes NPV = 0; compare to hurdle rate
- **Payback Period** — When does the investment break even?
- **Sensitivity Analysis** — How much does NPV change if key assumptions change by ±20%?

### Benefit Categories
- **Hard savings** — Quantifiable cost reductions (headcount, licensing, fines avoided)
- **Soft savings** — Efficiency gains, time savings (harder to quantify)
- **Risk reduction** — Probability × impact of avoided adverse events
- **Revenue enablement** — New business made possible by the investment

## Business Development in Financial Services

### Regulatory Change as Business Opportunity
Every major regulation creates:
- Advisory / consulting demand (helping others comply)
- Technology demand (tools to operationalise compliance)
- Product innovation (new compliant products that meet new customer needs)
- Talent demand (specialists in the new regulatory area)

### Client Acquisition in Professional Services
- **Referrals** — The dominant channel; trust and quality of work are the product
- **Thought leadership** — Regulatory publications, frameworks, training content that demonstrate expertise
- **Network density** — Being deeply embedded in the ecosystem (regulators, industry bodies, trade associations)
- **Differentiated capability** — Specific expertise competitors cannot match

## Strategic Decision-Making in Regulated Environments

Unique considerations for financial institutions:
1. **Regulatory constraints** — Some strategic options simply aren't available (licensing, capital requirements, geographic restrictions)
2. **Supervisory expectations** — Regulators have views on strategy; major strategic changes may require notification or approval
3. **Long approval cycles** — Acquisitions, new licences, significant model changes require regulatory approval (often 6–18 months)
4. **Reputational sensitivity** — A single regulatory failure can undermine years of strategy execution
5. **Stakeholder complexity** — Board, regulators, shareholders, employees, customers all with legitimate interests
