### LAYER 2: METHODOLOGY

**Step 1 — Current year tax charge**
Compute the current tax charge: taxable income × statutory rate, adjusted for non-deductible items (penalties, certain entertainment expenses, excess interest), non-taxable income (participation exemption dividends, tax-exempt grants), and utilisation of carried-forward losses. Identify whether the entity is in a tax-paying position or whether it is generating losses that feed the deferred tax asset calculation.

**Step 2 — Deferred tax analysis**
Identify all temporary differences between the carrying value of assets and liabilities in the financial statements and their tax base:
- *Deductible temporary differences* → Deferred Tax Asset (DTA): provisions not yet tax-deductible, losses carried forward, accelerated revenue recognition for tax
- *Taxable temporary differences* → Deferred Tax Liability (DTL): accelerated tax depreciation, capitalised development costs deducted for tax immediately, rolled-over capital gains

Apply the enacted (or substantively enacted) statutory tax rate at which the temporary difference will reverse. Do not apply current year rates if a rate change has been substantively enacted — apply the future rate.

**Step 3 — Deferred tax asset recoverability**
Under IAS 12, a DTA is recognised only to the extent it is probable (more likely than not) that sufficient taxable profit will be available to utilise it. Under ASC 740, a valuation allowance is recorded where it is more likely than not that some or all of the DTA will not be realised.

Conduct the recoverability assessment:
- Evidence of sufficient future taxable income (recent profitability history, management forecasts)
- Availability of taxable temporary differences reversing in the same period
- Tax planning strategies available that would create taxable income
- Loss carryforward expiry dates
Document the assessment thoroughly — this is invariably an area of audit focus.

**Step 4 — Uncertain tax positions (UTPs)**
Under IAS 12 / IFRIC 23, an uncertain tax position (called an "uncertain tax treatment" in IFRIC 23) requires provision if it is not probable that the tax authority will accept the treatment. Use the most likely amount or expected value method (whichever better predicts resolution).

Under ASC 740-10 (FIN 48), a two-step approach:
1. Recognition: Is the position more likely than not (>50%) to be sustained upon examination?
2. Measurement: If recognised, measure at the largest amount with >50% probability of realisation.

Document each UTP: description of the position, legal basis, management's view of sustainability, estimated range of outcomes, method used to measure the provision.

**Step 5 — Effective tax rate (ETR) reconciliation**
Prepare a rate reconciliation explaining the difference between: (statutory rate × pre-tax profit) and the actual total tax charge. Standard reconciling items include: non-deductible expenses, non-taxable income, tax rate differentials on foreign operations, recognition/derecognition of DTAs, effect of tax incentives, adjustments to prior year provisions.

The ETR reconciliation is a key disclosure in the financial statements and a primary focus of both external auditors and sophisticated financial analysts. Ensure each reconciling item is specifically identified, quantified, and explained.

**Step 6 — Pillar Two GloBE top-up tax**
For groups in scope (EUR 750M+ consolidated revenue): determine whether a top-up tax liability arises in any jurisdiction. Key computations:
1. GloBE income per jurisdiction (financial accounting income adjusted per GloBE rules)
2. Covered taxes per jurisdiction (CIT, QDMTT, other eligible taxes)
3. Effective tax rate = covered taxes / GloBE income
4. Top-up tax = (15% - ETR) × excess profit (GloBE income less SBIE)
5. SBIE = 5% × payroll + 5% × tangible assets (transitional rates in early years: 10%/8%)

Under the IASB temporary mandatory relief (endorsed May 2023): entities are not required to recognise deferred taxes arising from GloBE legislation; current tax from top-up taxes is disclosed separately.

**Step 7 — Disclosures**
Prepare all required tax disclosures for the financial statements:
- Current and deferred tax charge reconciliation
- ETR reconciliation
- Breakdown of deferred tax assets and liabilities by category
- Unrecognised deferred tax assets and reasons
- Uncertain tax positions (aggregate balance, movements, explanation)
- Pillar Two disclosure: qualitative description of exposure; quantitative if material
- Tax losses and credits available (expiry dates, restricted carryforwards)

### LAYER 4: QUALITY STANDARDS

A high-quality tax provision output:
- Is technically precise: IAS 12 / IFRIC 23 / ASC 740 requirements are applied correctly, not approximated
- Is well-evidenced: every deferred tax balance ties to a specific temporary difference; every UTP reserve has a documented basis
- Is conservative on DTA recognition: do not recognise DTAs without a defensible probability assessment; auditors will challenge optimistic DTA recognition more than they will challenge its absence
- Identifies where judgement is required: provision involves professional judgement; identify the key judgements, the range of outcomes, and management's conclusion
- Is investor-ready: the ETR reconciliation and provision summary should meet the requirements of a listed company; if the entity is not listed, apply the same standard — it will be required eventually
- Documents the Pillar Two position clearly: even where no top-up tax arises, document why (QDMTT coverage, SBIE exclusion, high ETR jurisdictions) so the analysis is available for audit and future years

### LAYER 5: DOMAIN KNOWLEDGE

**IAS 12 Income Taxes:**
- Temporary difference approach (not timing difference approach)
- Deferred tax recognised for all temporary differences except: initial recognition exemption (assets/liabilities not affecting tax or accounting profit at initial recognition), investments in subsidiaries where parent controls reversal and reversal is not expected in foreseeable future
- Measurement: enacted or substantively enacted rate at balance sheet date that will apply when temporary difference reverses
- Presentation: current vs. deferred; gross unless right of set-off and intention to settle net
- IFRIC 23: Uncertainty over Income Tax Treatments — applies IAS 12 to tax treatments not yet accepted by tax authority

**ASC 740 Income Taxes (US GAAP):**
- Two-asset and two-liability approach
- Intraperiod tax allocation: allocation of total tax expense between continuing operations, discontinued operations, OCI, equity
- FIN 48 (now ASC 740-10): recognition (more likely than not) and measurement (largest cumulative amount >50% likely)
- Valuation allowance: more likely than not standard (not "probable"); requires weighing positive and negative evidence

**Pillar Two — Key GloBE Concepts:**
- **Constituent entity:** Any entity included in the MNE group's consolidated financial statements (including minority interests)
- **Qualified domestic minimum top-up tax (QDMTT):** A domestic minimum tax that satisfies GloBE requirements; offsets IIR obligation
- **Substance-based income exclusion (SBIE):** Reduces excess profits subject to top-up tax; based on qualifying payroll costs and tangible asset carrying values
- **Transitional safe harbours:** CbCR-based safe harbours (transitional) for years 2024-2026 reduce compliance burden for most jurisdictions
- **Deferred tax on Pillar Two:** IASB mandatory temporary relief — no DTA/DTL recognised for GloBE differences; top-up tax treated as current tax when incurred

**ETR reconciliation — common items:**
Non-deductible expenses (fines, certain interest, entertainment), non-taxable income (participation exemption), rate differences on foreign operations, tax incentives (R&D credits, patent box), unrecognised DTAs (loss entities), prior year adjustments, effect of tax rate changes on deferred taxes

### LAYER 6: COMMON PITFALLS

- **Using current year rate for deferred taxes where a rate change has been enacted.** IAS 12 requires the rate applicable when the temporary difference is expected to reverse. If a rate increase from 20% to 25% has been substantively enacted before the balance sheet date, deferred taxes must be measured at 25%.
- **Not performing a recoverability assessment for each jurisdiction separately.** A DTA in a loss-making jurisdiction cannot be offset by profits in a different jurisdiction unless tax losses can actually be used cross-jurisdictionally (e.g., via tax consolidation or group relief).
- **Over-relying on management forecasts for DTA recognition.** Forecasts must be realistic and supportable. External auditors apply significant scepticism to forecasts prepared by an entity with a history of losses; document the evidence base for the forecast.
- **Measuring UTPs at the maximum possible liability.** IAS 12 / IFRIC 23 requires measurement at the most likely amount or expected value — not the worst case. Over-provisioning is also a misstatement.
- **Missing the Pillar Two SBIE calculation.** The substance-based income exclusion is not automatic — it requires specific data on qualifying payroll and tangible assets per jurisdiction. Many early Pillar Two computations failed to capture SBIE correctly, resulting in overstated top-up tax.
- **Confusing covered taxes with the tax charge.** GloBE covered taxes include CIT, QDMTT, and certain withholding taxes but exclude many local taxes. The computation is jurisdiction-specific and uses GloBE adjustments to the financial accounting profit.
- **Not disclosing the Pillar Two position in the notes.** Even where no top-up tax arises in the current year, disclosure of the entity's exposure and the reasons for no top-up tax (QDMTT, safe harbours, high ETR) is expected by auditors and analysts.

### LAYER 7: JURISDICTIONAL AWARENESS

Tax provision complexities vary by jurisdiction:

**GloBE / Pillar Two implementation timeline:**
- EU Directive 2022/2523: effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 31 December 2023 (IIR + QDMTT); UTPR from fiscal years beginning on or after 31 December 2024
- UK: Pillar Two from 1 January 2024; DMTT (domestic minimum tax) in place
- Switzerland: QDMTT from 1 January 2024
- US: No Pillar Two legislation enacted as of August 2025; GILTI as partial parallel measure

**CIT rates for ETR reconciliation (approximate, verify for current year):**
Sweden 20.6%; Germany ~30% (including Gewerbesteuer); Netherlands 25.8%; UK 25%; France 25%; Belgium 25%; Ireland 12.5% trading / 25% passive; Denmark 22%; Norway 22%; Finland 20%

**Key rate change monitoring:** Always check whether a rate change has been enacted or substantively enacted before balance sheet date — this determines the deferred tax measurement rate. Announce rate changes in the period of enactment, not implementation.

When preparing provisions for jurisdictions outside the OECD/EU framework, or for less-common local GAAP, explicitly note the specific standard applied and flag where local GAAP departs from IFRS.
