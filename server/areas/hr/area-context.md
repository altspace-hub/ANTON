# Human Resources & People Management Area Context

## Domain Landscape

The Human Resources & People Management area covers the full employee lifecycle — from talent attraction and acquisition through performance management, learning and development, compensation design, and organisational restructuring. It serves HR directors, HR business partners, talent acquisition specialists, learning and development managers, and CHROs in mid-to-large organisations. The domain sits at the intersection of employment law, organisational psychology, commercial strategy, and operational delivery.

## Core HR Domains

**Talent Acquisition:**
- Job architecture: levelling frameworks, job families, competency requirements
- Job description and job posting optimisation: attracting the right candidates
- Structured interviewing: competency-based, behavioural (STAR method), and technical assessment frameworks
- CV screening: shortlisting criteria, blind screening protocols, diversity and inclusion considerations
- Interview question design: behavioural, situational, and strengths-based questions
- Offer and negotiation strategy

**Performance Management:**
- Goal-setting frameworks: OKRs (Objectives and Key Results), SMART objectives, Balanced Scorecard alignment
- Performance review cycles: mid-year and annual review design and facilitation
- 9-box grid talent assessment: performance vs. potential calibration
- Performance improvement plans (PIPs): structure, documentation, legal requirements
- Continuous feedback culture: tools, cadence, and manager capability building
- Performance review summarisation and calibration support

**Compensation and Benefits:**
- Compensation benchmarking: market data sources (Mercer, Willis Towers Watson, Radford/Aon)
- Job evaluation methodologies: Hay/Korn Ferry, Mercer IPE, Willis Towers Watson Global Grading
- Pay band and salary range design
- Variable pay: bonus structures, commission schemes, long-term incentive plans (LTIPs)
- Benefits benchmarking and total reward strategy
- Pay equity analysis and equal pay obligations

**Learning and Development:**
- L&D needs analysis: skills gap assessment, performance data, strategic capability requirements
- Learning programme design: blended learning, 70:20:10 model, cohort programmes
- Leadership development: competency frameworks, succession pipeline management
- Onboarding programme design: pre-boarding, Day 1, first 90 days
- Return on learning investment measurement

**HR Policy and Employee Relations:**
- Policy development: disciplinary, grievance, absence management, flexible working, DEIB
- Employment law compliance (UK: Employment Rights Act, Equality Act; EU: various directives)
- ER case management: documentation standards, procedural fairness
- Exit interview analysis: identifying retention risk drivers and systemic issues

**Organisational Design:**
- Org restructuring: spans of control, delayering, team consolidation
- RACI and role clarity analysis
- Change management integration: communication planning, stakeholder engagement

## Key Frameworks and Standards

- **HRBP Model (Ulrich)** — Strategic partner, change agent, employee champion, administrative expert
- **9-Box Grid** — Performance vs. potential talent assessment matrix
- **70:20:10 Model** — Learning through experience (70%), exposure (20%), education (10%)
- **Competency Frameworks** — Behavioural and technical competencies by level and function
- **CIPD Professional Standards** — UK: Chartered Institute of Personnel and Development
- **SHRM/HRCI** — US: professional HR standards and certification bodies

## Major Challenges

- Hiring for potential in rapidly changing skill landscapes
- Maintaining pay equity while managing market rate pressures and internal relativities
- Designing performance management systems that motivate rather than demotivate
- Scaling HR processes in high-growth organisations without losing quality or compliance
- Managing restructuring programmes that preserve engagement and limit legal exposure
- Building skills data infrastructure to support evidence-based workforce planning

## Analytical Principles

1. **People decisions are business decisions** — Every HR recommendation must connect to a commercial or organisational outcome
2. **Fairness and consistency** — Inconsistent application of process creates legal risk and destroys trust
3. **Data-informed, not data-driven** — Workforce data illuminates; human judgement decides
4. **Manager capability is the multiplier** — HR systems are only as good as the managers implementing them
5. **Legal compliance is the floor, not the ceiling** — Compliance prevents harm; great HR practice creates value beyond that baseline
