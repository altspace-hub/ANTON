# Journalism & Content Writing — Area Context

Journalism and content writing are foundational disciplines for communicating complex information clearly, accurately, and compellingly. From investigative reporting to branded content strategy, these crafts share a commitment to truthfulness, audience clarity, and purposeful structure.

## Journalism principles

**The core standards of journalism:**
- **Accuracy** — Report only what can be verified. Attribution matters. If uncertain, say so.
- **Fairness** — Represent all significant viewpoints. Give subjects the right to respond.
- **Independence** — Serve the public interest, not advertisers, sources, or personal views.
- **Minimise harm** — Consider the impact of publishing on individuals and communities.
- **Accountability** — Journalists must be transparent about methods, sources, and corrections.

**News values (what makes a story):**
- Timeliness, Proximity, Significance, Prominence, Human interest, Conflict, Novelty

**The inverted pyramid:**
Most important information first, supporting detail next, background last. Editors cut from the bottom; readers stop reading when satisfied.

## Content writing principles

**Content strategy fundamentals:**
- **Purpose** — Every piece of content must serve a clear objective (inform, persuade, convert, retain)
- **Audience** — Who is the reader? What do they know? What do they need? What language do they use?
- **Channel** — The same story told differently for LinkedIn, a white paper, an email, and a landing page
- **Measurement** — Content without metrics is decoration. Define success before writing.

**Quality content standards:**
- Opens with the reader's problem or interest, not the writer's needs
- Uses active voice, short sentences, concrete nouns
- Supports every claim with evidence, examples, or quotes
- Structures information so readers can scan (headers, bullets, short paragraphs)
- Ends with a clear next action or takeaway

## Research and fact-checking standards

- Every factual claim requires a source
- Primary sources (original documents, direct interviews) beat secondary sources (reports, articles)
- Statistics must be contextualised — raw numbers without denominators mislead
- Dates, names, titles, and figures must be verified and double-checked
- Corrections must be published promptly and transparently

## Interview craft

The quality of the story depends on the quality of the interview. Preparation determines outcome:
- Research the subject's background thoroughly before the interview
- Prepare open, specific questions — not yes/no questions
- Listen for what's not said as much as what is
- Follow unexpected threads — the best quotes often come from tangents
- Always confirm quotes before publication

## Editorial quality

Great editing improves not just grammar but structure, logic, and impact. The editor's question is always: "Will the reader understand this? Does this serve the story's purpose?"
