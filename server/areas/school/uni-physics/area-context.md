# University Physics — University Level

## Subject Overview
University physics extends secondary-school mechanics and electricity into rigorous mathematical
frameworks. Students engage with classical mechanics, electromagnetism, thermodynamics, quantum
mechanics, and special relativity, applying calculus and linear algebra throughout. Experimental
methodology and uncertainty analysis are integral to the discipline.

## Core Areas

### Classical Mechanics
- Newtonian mechanics with vector calculus: equations of motion, work-energy theorem
- Lagrangian mechanics: generalised coordinates, the Euler-Lagrange equation
- Hamiltonian mechanics: phase space, Poisson brackets, conservation laws
- Rigid body dynamics: moments of inertia, angular momentum, torque
- Oscillations and waves: SHM, coupled oscillators, wave equation, dispersion

### Electromagnetism
- Maxwell's equations in integral and differential form
- Electrostatics: Gauss's law, electric potential, capacitance
- Magnetostatics: Ampere's law, Biot-Savart law, magnetic vector potential
- Electromagnetic induction: Faraday's law, mutual and self-inductance
- Electromagnetic waves: propagation, polarisation, reflection, transmission

### Thermodynamics & Statistical Mechanics
- Laws of thermodynamics; entropy, heat engines, Carnot efficiency
- Thermodynamic potentials: Helmholtz free energy, Gibbs free energy, enthalpy
- Kinetic theory: Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution, equipartition theorem
- Statistical ensembles: microcanonical, canonical, grand canonical

### Quantum Mechanics (Introduction)
- Wave-particle duality; de Broglie wavelength; photoelectric effect
- Schrödinger equation (time-dependent and time-independent)
- Infinite square well, harmonic oscillator, hydrogen atom
- Operators, eigenstates, measurement, and the uncertainty principle

### Special Relativity
- Lorentz transformations; time dilation, length contraction, relativity of simultaneity
- Relativistic energy and momentum: $E^2 = (pc)^2 + (mc^2)^2$
- Minkowski spacetime; four-vectors; invariant interval

## Pedagogical Approach
Professor Lindström emphasises the mathematical structure underlying physical laws:
1. **From principle to prediction** — start from fundamental principles and derive consequences
2. **Dimensional analysis** — always check units and limiting cases before numerical work
3. **Physical intuition alongside rigour** — build intuition first, then formalise
4. **Problem decomposition** — break complex problems into sub-problems with clear physics
5. **Order-of-magnitude estimation** — Fermi estimation as a check on detailed calculation

## Academic Standards
- Use SI units throughout; justify any non-SI choices
- LaTeX notation for equations (e.g., $\nabla \times \mathbf{B} = \mu_0 \mathbf{J}$)
- Reference standard texts: Griffiths (EM, QM), Taylor (Classical Mechanics), Kittel (Statistical Mechanics)
- Distinguish clearly between exact results and approximations
