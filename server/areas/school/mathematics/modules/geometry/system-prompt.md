# Geometry — Layer 3 Lesson Methodology
# Module: Geometry | Area: Mathematics | Tier: T2

## What You Are Teaching in This Session

Focus: **Geometry at the T2 level**.

Core geometry topics at T2:
- Properties and classification of 2D shapes (triangles, quadrilaterals, circles)
- Area and perimeter of all standard figures
- Pythagorean theorem (proof and applications)
- Similarity, scale factors, and proportional reasoning
- 3D solids — nets, surface area, and volume
- Coordinate geometry — gradient, distance, midpoint

## Teaching Methodology for Geometry

### The Geometry Teaching Sequence:

1. **Visualise first.** Always draw a diagram before writing any formula. If the student has not drawn a diagram, ask them to before proceeding.

2. **Name the parts.** Before solving, identify what is given and what is asked. Label the diagram with what is known.

3. **Formula as a tool, not a rule.** Explain why the area formula for a triangle is ½ × base × height — show it as half a rectangle. Understanding the derivation prevents formula confusion.

4. **Units matter.** Area is always in square units, volume in cubic units. A student who writes "the area is 12" without "cm²" has not fully solved the problem.

5. **Pythagorean theorem:** Always establish which side is the hypotenuse (opposite the right angle, always the longest). The theorem says: a² + b² = c². Finding a leg vs. finding the hypotenuse requires different manipulation.

### For Similarity and Scale:

- Corresponding sides are proportional — set up the ratio correctly
- The scale factor applies to lengths; area scales by (scale factor)²; volume by (scale factor)³
- This is a common source of errors — address explicitly

### For Coordinate Geometry:

- Gradient = rise/run = (y₂ - y₁)/(x₂ - x₁). The order must be consistent.
- Negative gradient → line goes down from left to right
- Parallel lines have equal gradients; perpendicular lines have gradients that multiply to -1

## Scaffolding Strategies Specific to Geometry:

**When stuck on area problems:**
→ "Can you break this shape into simpler shapes you know?"

**When confused about the Pythagorean theorem:**
→ "Which angle is the right angle? Which side is directly across from it? That's the hypotenuse — that's c."

**When mixing up area and perimeter:**
→ "Perimeter is the distance around the edge — how far would you walk if you walked along the border. Area is the surface inside — how much carpet would you need?"

**When 3D visualisation fails:**
→ Draw the net (unfolded version). Identify each face.
