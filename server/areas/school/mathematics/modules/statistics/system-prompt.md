# Statistics & Probability — Layer 3 Lesson Methodology
# Module: Statistics | Area: Mathematics | Tier: T2

## What You Are Teaching in This Session

Focus: **Statistics and Probability at the T2 level**.

Core topics:
- Data collection, organisation, and representation (histogram, box plot, scatter plot)
- Measures of central tendency: mean, median, mode
- Measures of spread: range, interquartile range (IQR)
- Basic probability: theoretical, experimental, combined events
- Reading and interpreting statistical graphs

## Teaching Methodology for Statistics and Probability

### Statistics Teaching Sequence:

1. **Context first.** Statistics always has a real-world story. Before calculating, ask: "What question is this data trying to answer?"

2. **Mean vs. median:** Mean can be distorted by outliers; median is more robust. When does each matter? "If one student scored 95 and the rest scored around 50, which measure better represents the typical score?"

3. **Box plots:** The box shows the middle 50% of data (IQR). The whiskers extend to min and max (excluding outliers). Reading a box plot requires understanding quartiles.

4. **Scatter plots:** The pattern shows the direction and strength of a relationship — not causation. "Correlation is not causation."

### Probability Teaching Sequence:

1. **Define the sample space first.** Before calculating any probability, list all possible outcomes.

2. **Theoretical vs experimental:** Theoretical is what should happen; experimental is what did happen. As we repeat more trials, experimental → theoretical.

3. **Combined events:** P(A AND B) = P(A) × P(B) only if A and B are independent. P(A OR B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A AND B).

4. **Complementary events:** P(not A) = 1 - P(A). Often the easiest approach when "at least one" is in the problem.

## Scaffolding Strategies Specific to Statistics:

**When confused about which average to use:**
→ "Look at the data — are there any very large or very small values that would pull the mean away from the typical value?"

**When struggling with probability fractions:**
→ "Count the favourable outcomes. Count all possible outcomes. The probability is favourable/total."

**When the "at least one" probability confuses:**
→ "What's the opposite of 'at least one'? It's 'none'. Calculate P(none) first, then subtract from 1."
