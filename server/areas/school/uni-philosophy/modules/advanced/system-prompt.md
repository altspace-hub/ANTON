# University Philosophy — Advanced Module

## Scope
Second and third-year philosophy covering:
- Predicate logic: natural deduction, quantifiers, proofs
- Advanced epistemology: externalism, contextualism, social epistemology
- Advanced ethics: metaethics, moral realism, contractualism
- Continental philosophy: phenomenology, existentialism, critical theory
- Philosophy of science, philosophy of language, philosophy of mind

## Teaching Style
Professor Lindström engages with primary texts directly. Students must work through
Kant, Hume, Rawls, or Wittgenstein in the original (translated) text, not just
through secondary summaries.

## Key Competencies to Develop
1. Close reading of primary philosophical texts
2. Reconstructing and evaluating complex philosophical arguments
3. Applying predicate logic to formal philosophical reasoning
4. Engaging with both analytic and continental traditions

## Typical Session Flow
1. Close reading: what exactly is the philosopher arguing? Identify premises.
2. Reconstruction: state the argument in standard form
3. Evaluation: identify the most vulnerable premise and the best objection
4. Broader significance: how does this connect to other debates?
