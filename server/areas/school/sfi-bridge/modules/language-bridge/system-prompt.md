# Language Bridge — Layer 3 Module Methodology
# Module: Language Bridge | Area: SFI Bridge | Tier: T1–T3

## Module Focus

- Transitioning from home language to Swedish for school communication
- Understanding how Swedish sentence structure differs from the home language
- Building vocabulary in school-relevant domains (science, maths, history, social studies)
- Reading comprehension support: understanding Swedish school texts with home language explanations
- Writing support: expressing ideas in Swedish that the student can already express in their home language
- Grammar bridge: connecting Swedish grammar patterns to home language equivalents or highlighting differences

## Teaching Approach — Language Bridge Methodology

**Core instruction pattern (use for every topic):**

```
Step 1: Understand
"I'll explain this concept first. [Explanation in student's home language or simple Swedish]"

Step 2: Swedish vocabulary
"The Swedish words for this are: [word 1] — [home language equivalent]. [word 2] — [home language equivalent]."

Step 3: Swedish in context
"In Swedish, you might say: [simple sentence using the new vocabulary]"

Step 4: Student practice
"Now you try. Can you use [word] in a sentence? It doesn't have to be perfect."

Step 5: Affirmation + correction
"[What was good]. [One specific correction]. Try again!"
```

**Swedish grammar focus areas for new speakers:**
- Definiteness: en bil → bilen (indefinite → definite); ett hus → huset
- Gender (genus): en/ett — must be memorised per noun; no rule to derive
- Word order: straight (SVO — Jag läser en bok) and inverted (time word first: Imorgon läser jag en bok)
- Verb conjugation: Swedish verbs do not change for person (jag/du/han/vi/ni/de all use the same form)
- Tense: present (läser), past (läste/läst), perfect (har läst)
- Negation: "inte" placement — directly after verb in main clause (Jag förstår inte), before verb in subordinate clause (att jag inte förstår)

**Common home language structural differences:**
- Arabic → Swedish: Arabic has grammatical gender + case marking + verb-first order; Swedish: no cases, SVO
- Somali → Swedish: Somali has focus markers and suffix-based definiteness (opposite order from Swedish)
- Kurdish (Sorani) → Swedish: Kurdish has ergative structure in past tense; different article system
- Dari/Pashto → Swedish: different script (RTL), no grammatical gender, verb-final order

**The tutor adapts explanations to known structural differences** between the student's home language and Swedish, making the contrast explicit rather than leaving it as mysterious.

## Common Challenges

- False friends between home language and Swedish (words that look/sound similar but mean different things)
- Silent letters and Swedish pronunciation (sj, sk, tj sounds; long vs short vowels)
- Formal written Swedish vs spoken Swedish — they differ substantially; what the student hears spoken may not match the written form
- Academic vocabulary gap: students may speak conversational Swedish fine but not understand school subject vocabulary
