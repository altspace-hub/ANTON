# Everyday Swedish — Layer 3 Module Methodology
# Module: Everyday Swedish | Area: SFI Bridge | Tier: T1–T3

## Module Focus

- Practical Swedish for school and community life: talking to teachers, writing to the school, understanding letters from the municipality
- Telephone and digital communication in Swedish: how to write a formal email, how to leave a message
- Reading everyday Swedish: forms, signs, timetables, food labels, medicine instructions
- Social language at school: asking for help, asking for repetition, joining group work, navigating misunderstandings
- Understanding Swedish cultural norms at school: punctuality expectations, direct communication style, concept of "lagom"
- Emergency and safety vocabulary: knowing how to communicate about health, safety, and urgent situations

## Teaching Approach — Everyday Swedish

**Immediate practical relevance.** Every lesson topic should answer: "When will I need this?" Not "let's study Swedish" but "let's learn what you need for your appointment/school meeting/doctor's visit tomorrow."

**Dialogue practice — role play key situations:**

*Talking to a teacher:*
Student: "Förlåt, jag förstod inte. Kan du förklara igen, lite långsammare?" (Sorry, I didn't understand. Can you explain again, a bit slower?)
Teacher: "Självklart! Ska vi ta det från början?" (Of course! Shall we start from the beginning?)

*Writing a message to school:*
"[Student's name] kan tyvärr inte komma till skolan idag på grund av sjukdom. Med vänliga hälsningar, [Parent name]."
([Student] unfortunately cannot come to school today due to illness. With kind regards, [Parent name].)

*Asking for help:*
"Kan du hjälpa mig? Jag hittar inte mitt klassrum." (Can you help me? I can't find my classroom.)
"Var finns biblioteket?" (Where is the library?)
"Jag behöver prata med kuratorn." (I need to talk to the school counsellor.)

**Reading forms and official Swedish:**
Official Swedish uses formal register and specific conventions:
- Formal openings: "Till [authority/person]" or simply start with the matter
- Passive constructions: "Det informeras härmed om att..." (You are hereby informed that...)
- Teach the student to identify the key information: who sent this, what does it want me to do, by when

**Swedish cultural context:**
- Swedish directness: being concise and clear is polite, not rude. In Swedish culture, indirect requests can be misunderstood.
- "Du" to everyone: Swedish dropped formal address (Ni) in the 1960s. Address teachers, doctors, and officials with "du" — it is not disrespectful.
- Lagom: the Swedish cultural concept of "just right, not too much, not too little." Useful for understanding social expectations.
- Punctuality: arriving on time or slightly early is expected; being late requires an explanation.

**Emergency vocabulary — teach first:**
- 112 = Swedish emergency number (police, fire, ambulance)
- "Jag behöver hjälp" (I need help)
- "Det är en nödsituation" (It is an emergency)
- "Ring ambulansen/polisen" (Call an ambulance/the police)
- "Jag mår inte bra" (I don't feel well)

## Common Challenges

- Formal vs informal register: Swedish social norms around formality are different from many home countries (less hierarchical in language, but different social cues)
- Letter and email conventions: official Swedish letters have specific formats that differ from home country norms
- Homesickness and school adjustment: many new students are managing enormous life changes. The tutor acknowledges this warmly without overstepping into counsellor territory
