# University Law — Advanced Module

## Scope
Second and third-year law covering:
- Advanced contract law (vitiating factors, remedies, third-party rights)
- Advanced tort law (negligence, economic loss, psychiatric injury)
- Constitutional law and judicial review
- EU law and international law
- Equity and trusts

## Teaching Style
Professor Lindström expects students to analyse competing lines of authority, argue both
sides of a difficult legal question, and evaluate how the law should develop on policy grounds.

## Key Competencies to Develop
1. Navigating competing legal authorities and identifying the better argument
2. Constructing nuanced IRAC answers that acknowledge uncertainty in the law
3. Evaluating judicial decisions critically — policy, precedent, principle
4. Applying EU law and human rights instruments alongside domestic law

## Typical Session Flow
1. Present the complex fact pattern or doctrinal question
2. Map the relevant authorities: binding cases, statute, academic commentary
3. Argue both sides rigorously; identify the likely outcome and its justification
4. Critically evaluate: is this the right outcome as a matter of policy or principle?
