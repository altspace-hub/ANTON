# Higher Mathematics / Calculus — University Level

## Subject Overview
University mathematics demands rigorous proof, abstract reasoning, and the ability to work across
multiple branches simultaneously. This subject covers analysis (limits, continuity, differentiability,
integration), linear algebra (vector spaces, matrices, eigenvalues), and discrete mathematics.

## Core Areas

### Calculus & Real Analysis
- Limits and continuity (ε-δ definitions)
- Differentiability, Taylor series, L'Hôpital's rule
- Riemann integration, improper integrals, Fubini's theorem
- Multivariable calculus: partial derivatives, gradient, divergence, curl
- Vector calculus: line integrals, surface integrals, Green/Stokes/Divergence theorems

### Linear Algebra
- Vector spaces, subspaces, basis, dimension
- Linear transformations and their matrix representations
- Eigenvalues, eigenvectors, diagonalisation
- Inner product spaces, orthogonality, Gram-Schmidt
- Applications: least squares, principal component analysis

### Differential Equations
- First and second order ODEs (separable, linear, homogeneous)
- Systems of ODEs
- Laplace transforms
- Partial differential equations (heat equation, wave equation)

### Proof Methods
- Direct proof, proof by contradiction, proof by induction
- Constructive vs. non-constructive proofs
- Formal logical structure and mathematical writing

## Pedagogical Approach
Professor Lindström uses the Socratic seminar method at university level:
1. **Definitions first** — always start from precise, formal definitions
2. **Counterexamples** — challenge incorrect intuitions with carefully chosen counterexamples
3. **Proof sketches** — guide students to construct proofs themselves before presenting solutions
4. **Connections** — show how results in one area illuminate another
5. **Historical context** — brief mentions of historical development and major contributors
