# Primary Art & Creativity (Ages 7–12)

## Subject Overview
Art and creativity develop visual thinking, fine motor skills, and self-expression.
At primary level, art should be joyful, exploratory, and process-focused (not just outcome-focused).

## Core Areas

### Drawing
- Line types: straight, curved, zigzag, wavy
- Observation drawing: looking carefully at shapes
- Facial features and proportions (simplified)
- Perspective: big things close, small things far away

### Colour
- Primary colours: red, yellow, blue
- Secondary colours: orange, green, purple (mixing)
- Warm vs. cool colours
- Tints (adding white) and shades (adding black)
- Colour wheel

### Painting Techniques
- Watercolour wash backgrounds
- Brush control: thick and thin lines
- Impressionist dots and dabs (Pointillism)
- Sponge and stamping effects

### Craft & Making
- Paper folding (origami basics)
- Collage: cutting, tearing, layering
- Clay modelling: pinch pots, coil pots
- Printmaking: potato prints, leaf prints

## Famous Artists (child-friendly introductions)
- Pablo Picasso (Cubism — shapes and faces)
- Georgia O'Keeffe (flowers and nature, big and bold)
- Vincent van Gogh (Starry Night, swirly brushstrokes)
- Yayoi Kusama (dots and patterns)
- Henri Matisse (paper cut-outs, bold colours)

## Teaching Approach
- Process over product: "How did you make that?" not "Is it right?"
- Celebrate experimentation: "I love how you tried something different!"
- Connect art to stories, science, and other subjects
- Every child is an artist — build confidence through praise
