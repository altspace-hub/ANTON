# Psychology & Behavioural Science — University Level

## Subject Overview
University psychology encompasses the scientific study of mind and behaviour. Students engage with
empirical research methods, biological foundations of behaviour, cognitive processes, social
influences, developmental trajectories, abnormal psychology, and applied domains. Statistical
literacy and critical evaluation of research evidence are central competencies.

## Core Areas

### Research Methods & Statistics
- Scientific method: hypothesis generation, operationalisation, falsifiability (Popper)
- Research designs: experimental (RCT), quasi-experimental, observational, case study
- Sampling: random, stratified, convenience; sampling bias and generalisability
- Quantitative analysis: descriptive statistics, t-test, ANOVA, correlation, regression
- Qualitative methods: thematic analysis, grounded theory, IPA
- Open science: pre-registration, replication crisis, p-hacking, effect sizes vs. p-values

### Biological Bases of Behaviour
- Neuroanatomy: lobes, limbic system, basal ganglia, cerebellum; lesion studies
- Neurotransmission: action potentials, synaptic transmission, key neurotransmitters (DA, 5-HT, NE)
- Endocrine system: HPA axis, cortisol, hormones and behaviour
- Genetics and behaviour: twin studies, heritability, gene-environment interaction
- Evolutionary psychology: adaptationist framework, inclusive fitness, kin selection

### Cognitive Psychology
- Attention: selective, divided, sustained; spotlight and spotlight models
- Memory: encoding, storage, retrieval; multi-store model (Atkinson & Shiffrin); working memory (Baddeley)
- Long-term memory: episodic, semantic, procedural; consolidation and reconsolidation
- Language: Chomsky's Universal Grammar; Sapir-Whorf hypothesis; language acquisition
- Decision-making and reasoning: heuristics, cognitive biases (Kahneman & Tversky), dual-process theory

### Social Psychology
- Social influence: conformity (Asch), obedience (Milgram), groupthink
- Attitudes and attitude change: elaboration likelihood model, cognitive dissonance (Festinger)
- Attribution theory: fundamental attribution error, actor-observer bias, locus of control
- Group dynamics: in-group/out-group, social identity theory (Tajfel & Turner), deindividuation
- Prosocial behaviour and aggression: bystander effect, frustration-aggression hypothesis

### Developmental Psychology
- Prenatal development and infancy: reflexes, sensorimotor development, attachment (Bowlby, Ainsworth)
- Cognitive development: Piaget's stages; Vygotsky's ZPD and scaffolding
- Adolescence: identity formation (Erikson); peer influence; puberty and risk-taking
- Adult development and ageing: personality stability, cognitive decline, wisdom

### Abnormal Psychology & Clinical Applications
- Classification systems: DSM-5 and ICD-11; reliability, validity, cultural considerations
- Major disorders: depression, anxiety, schizophrenia, PTSD, personality disorders
- Psychological therapies: CBT, psychodynamic, humanistic, ACT, DBT
- Biological treatments: pharmacotherapy, ECT; ethical considerations

## Pedagogical Approach
Professor Lindström emphasises evidence-based critical thinking:
1. **Study design first** — evaluate any empirical claim by examining its methodology
2. **Effect sizes matter** — statistical significance is not the same as practical significance
3. **Multiple perspectives** — compare biological, cognitive, social, and cultural explanations
4. **Replication awareness** — discuss landmark studies alongside their replication status
5. **Ethics** — consider participant welfare, informed consent, and societal implications

## Academic Standards
- APA 7th edition citations and references
- Distinguish correlational from causal claims in all research discussion
- University-level precision: avoid lay terms where technical vocabulary exists
- Cite both classic studies and recent meta-analyses where available
