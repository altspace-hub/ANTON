# Waves — Layer 3 Module Methodology
# Module: Waves | Area: Physics | Tier: T3

## Module Focus (Fysik 1–2)

- Wave properties: amplitude, wavelength (λ), frequency (f), period (T = 1/f), wave speed (v = fλ)
- Transverse vs longitudinal waves; mechanical vs electromagnetic waves
- Superposition and interference: constructive (path difference = nλ), destructive (path difference = (n+½)λ)
- Standing waves: nodes and antinodes; resonant frequencies of strings (fn = n·v/2L) and open/closed pipes
- Doppler effect: f_obs = f_source · (v ± v_obs)/(v ∓ v_source)
- Sound: decibel scale (logarithmic), speed of sound in different media
- Light: electromagnetic spectrum, reflection (law of reflection), refraction (Snell's law: n₁sinθ₁ = n₂sinθ₂)
- Wave optics: double-slit (Young's experiment: y = λL/d), diffraction grating

## Teaching Approach

**Viktor's wave mystery:** "Why does sound travel faster in steel than in air? What does that tell us about how waves move?"

**Draw every wave problem.** Label wavelength, amplitude, direction of travel. For interference, draw both waves and show the resultant.

**Snell's law intuition:** Light slows down in a denser medium and bends toward the normal. Use the analogy of a car wheel on different surfaces.

**Standing waves:** The string/pipe is selecting specific wavelengths that "fit". Boundary conditions (nodes at fixed ends, antinodes at open ends) determine which frequencies are allowed.

## Common Errors

- Confusing period with frequency (they're reciprocals)
- Doppler: sign convention — observer moving toward source increases frequency
- Destructive interference: path difference of half a wavelength, not zero
- Snell's law angles: always measured from the NORMAL, not the surface
