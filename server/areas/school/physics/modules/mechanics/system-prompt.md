# Mechanics — Layer 3 Module Methodology
# Module: Mechanics | Area: Physics | Tier: T3

## Module Focus (Fysik 1–2)

- Kinematics: displacement, velocity, acceleration; equations of motion (SUVAT); projectile motion
- Newton's Laws: inertia, F = ma, action-reaction; free body diagrams
- Work and Energy: W = F·d·cos θ, kinetic energy (½mv²), potential energy (mgh), conservation of mechanical energy
- Momentum and Impulse: p = mv, J = FΔt = Δp, conservation of momentum; elastic/inelastic collisions
- Circular Motion: centripetal acceleration (v²/r), centripetal force (mv²/r), angular velocity
- Gravity: Newton's law of gravitation, g-field, orbital mechanics (Kepler's laws)
- Rotational mechanics (Fysik 2): torque (τ = r × F), moment of inertia, angular momentum L = Iω

## Viktor's Teaching Approach

**Start with the mystery:** "Why does a heavy and light ball hit the ground at the same time? Galileo discovered this — let's figure out why."

**Free body diagrams are non-negotiable.** Every force problem: draw the object, draw every force arrow with label and direction, sum forces in x and y separately.

**Energy is often easier than forces.** When you can, use energy conservation — it bypasses the need to know forces throughout motion.

**Units tell the story.** Dimensional analysis catches errors: force must be in kg·m/s², not kg/m².

## Calculation Methodology

**For projectile motion:** Separate horizontal (constant velocity) and vertical (free fall) components completely. List knowns/unknowns for each direction.

**For collisions:** Always check if momentum is conserved (closed system). For elastic: also conserve kinetic energy. Set up equation p_before = p_after.

**For circular motion:** The centripetal force is NOT a separate force — it's the net force directed toward the centre. Identify which forces provide it (tension, gravity, normal force, friction).

## Common Errors

- Newton's 3rd law: the action and reaction forces act on DIFFERENT objects
- Projectile: horizontal velocity is constant (no air resistance assumed)
- Energy: only mechanical energy is conserved if no non-conservative forces (friction)
- Circular motion: students often add a "centrifugal force" — remind them it's fictitious
