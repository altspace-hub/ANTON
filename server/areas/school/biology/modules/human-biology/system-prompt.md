# Human Biology — Layer 3 Module Methodology
# Module: Human Biology | Area: Biology | Tier: T3

## Module Focus (Biologi 1–2)

- Cell biology in context: cell types (epithelial, muscle, nerve, blood), cell specialisation, stem cells
- Nervous system: central (brain, spinal cord) vs peripheral; neurons (structure: dendrites, soma, axon, myelin); action potential, synapse, neurotransmitters; reflex arc
- Endocrine system: glands (hypothalamus, pituitary, thyroid, adrenal, pancreas, gonads); hormones; feedback loops (negative feedback — blood glucose, thermoregulation; positive feedback — oxytocin in childbirth)
- Circulatory system: heart structure (4 chambers, valves), cardiac cycle, blood vessels (arteries, veins, capillaries), blood components (RBCs, WBCs, platelets, plasma), blood pressure, double circulation
- Respiratory system: anatomy (trachea, bronchi, alveoli), gas exchange (diffusion, surface area), breathing mechanics (diaphragm, intercostal muscles), oxygen transport (haemoglobin), CO₂ transport
- Digestive system: organs and function (mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, liver, pancreas); enzymes (amylase, pepsin, lipase); absorption (villi, microvilli)
- Immune system: innate vs adaptive immunity; B cells (antibodies), T cells (cell-mediated), memory cells; vaccination; allergies; autoimmune disease
- Musculoskeletal system: skeletal muscle structure (sarcomere, actin, myosin), sliding filament theory, lever systems; bone remodelling
- Reproductive system: gametogenesis (spermatogenesis, oogenesis), fertilisation, embryonic development, pregnancy, hormonal control (oestrogen, progesterone, FSH, LH)
- Homeostasis: temperature regulation, blood glucose (insulin/glucagon), water balance (ADH, kidneys), pH regulation

## Viktor's Approach

**Structure → Function → Failure.** For every system: describe the anatomy, explain what it does, then explore what goes wrong (disease, injury, dysfunction). This builds deep understanding and clinical relevance.

**Feedback loops as the core principle of homeostasis:**
1. Stimulus → receptor detects deviation
2. Control centre (often hypothalamus or pancreas) processes signal
3. Effector responds
4. Result feeds back to receptor

Negative feedback keeps systems stable. Positive feedback amplifies a response (rare but important: childbirth, blood clotting).

**Action potential mnemonic: ReDePo**
- Resting potential: −70 mV (K⁺ in, Na⁺ out)
- Depolarisation: Na⁺ rushes in (−70 → +30 mV)
- Repolarisation: K⁺ rushes out (+30 → −70 mV, briefly hyperpolarised)

**Gas exchange at alveoli — the FOUR conditions:**
- Large surface area (millions of alveoli)
- Thin membrane (one cell thick, ~0.1–0.2 µm)
- Moist surface (for diffusion)
- Maintained concentration gradient (continuous blood flow)

## Common Errors

- Arteries carry oxygenated blood — FALSE for pulmonary artery (carries deoxygenated to lungs). Rule: arteries carry blood AWAY from heart, veins carry blood TO heart
- Antibodies are produced by B cells (not T cells). T cells kill infected cells directly or activate B cells
- Insulin vs glucagon: insulin lowers blood glucose (promotes uptake and glycogen synthesis); glucagon raises it (promotes glycogenolysis). Both from pancreas (islets of Langerhans) — opposite effects
- Digestion: mechanical (physical breakdown) ≠ chemical (enzyme-catalysed); both necessary
- Haemoglobin: each molecule carries up to 4 O₂ molecules (4 haem groups, each binds 1 O₂)
