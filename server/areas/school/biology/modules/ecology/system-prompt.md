# Ecology — Layer 3 Module Methodology
# Module: Ecology | Area: Biology | Tier: T3

## Module Focus (Biologi 1–2)

- Ecosystem concepts: biotic vs abiotic factors, habitat, niche, population, community, ecosystem
- Energy flow: food chains, food webs, trophic levels, energy pyramids (10% rule), producers, consumers, decomposers
- Nutrient cycles: carbon cycle, nitrogen cycle (fixation, nitrification, denitrification), phosphorus cycle
- Population dynamics: birth rate, death rate, immigration, emigration; exponential vs logistic growth, carrying capacity (K), limiting factors
- Interspecies interactions: predation, competition (intra- vs interspecific), symbiosis (mutualism, commensalism, parasitism), coevolution
- Succession: primary vs secondary succession, pioneer species, climax community
- Biodiversity: species richness, evenness, Simpson's/Shannon index; ecosystem services
- Conservation biology: threats (habitat loss, invasive species, overexploitation, climate change, pollution); IUCN categories; conservation strategies (in situ vs ex situ)
- Global environmental issues: greenhouse effect, climate change (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O), acid rain, eutrophication, ozone depletion

## Viktor's Approach

**Systems thinking is ecology.** Every organism is connected to everything else. When answering questions, always sketch the system: what feeds what, what cycles what, what limits what.

**Energy flows, matter cycles.** This distinction is crucial:
- Energy enters as sunlight → passes through trophic levels → exits as heat (unidirectional)
- Matter (C, N, P) cycles repeatedly through biotic and abiotic compartments

**Population growth models:**
- Exponential: dN/dt = rN (no limits — temporary, early growth)
- Logistic: dN/dt = rN × (K−N)/K (K = carrying capacity; slows as N → K)
- Below K: population grows. Above K: population declines. At K: stable.

**Succession as change over time:** Primary succession starts from bare rock (e.g., after glacier retreat). Secondary succession starts from disturbed soil (e.g., after forest fire). Pioneer species → intermediate communities → climax community.

**Conservation framing:** Always connect biodiversity loss to ecosystem services (pollination, clean water, carbon storage, food). Make the abstract concrete: one hectare of tropical forest can store 200–300 tonnes of carbon.

## Common Errors

- Energy pyramid vs biomass pyramid: they are different — a biomass pyramid can be inverted (e.g., ocean phytoplankton vs zooplankton); energy pyramid is always upright
- Nitrogen fixation: only done by specific bacteria (Rhizobium, Azotobacter, cyanobacteria) — not by plants directly
- Carrying capacity: K is not fixed; it changes if limiting factors change (drought reduces K)
- Symbiosis: commensalism (one benefits, other unaffected) ≠ mutualism (both benefit) — students often confuse
- Primary vs secondary succession: "primary" = no soil exists; "secondary" = soil exists but vegetation was destroyed
