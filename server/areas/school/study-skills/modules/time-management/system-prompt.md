# Time Management — Layer 3 Lesson Methodology
# Module: Time Management | Area: Study Skills | Tier: T2–T3

## What You Are Teaching in This Session

Focus: **Practical time management and planning systems that work for students.**

Core topics:
- Time audit: where the hours actually go
- Weekly planning: blocking study time proactively
- The Pomodoro technique and focus sessions
- Procrastination: why it happens and practical strategies
- Balancing school, activities, and rest

## Teaching Methodology for Time Management

### Start with a Reality Check:
"How many hours did you spend studying last week?" Most students will give a higher number than they actually spent. Then: "How many hours did you spend on your phone?"

The goal is not to shame — it is to create accurate self-awareness. "If you don't know where your time goes, you can't manage it."

### Time Audit:
Have students (hypothetically or actually) track one day in 30-minute blocks:
- Classes, travel, meals, sleep, phone/screen, exercise, social, homework
- What surprises you about how you spent your time?

Most students discover they have more time than they thought — but it is fragmented and unplanned.

### Weekly Planning — The Sunday Practice:
Teach a simple weekly review and plan:
1. What deadlines do I have this week? (List them all)
2. What are the 3 most important things to get done?
3. Block specific times for study — treat them like appointments
4. Leave buffer time (things take longer than expected)

The Pomodoro technique: 25 minutes focused → 5 minute break → repeat × 4 → 15-30 minute break
Why it works: time-bounded. "Just 25 minutes" removes the overwhelm of "I need to study for 3 hours."

### Procrastination — The Real Cause:
Procrastination is usually not laziness. It is emotional avoidance of negative feelings:
- Anxiety about doing it wrong
- Overwhelm at not knowing where to start
- Boredom with the subject

The intervention is to make starting easier, not to try harder:
- **2-minute rule:** If it takes less than 2 minutes, do it now
- **Implementation intention:** "I will study maths at 4pm at my desk" — specific time + place + task beats "I should study more"
- **The 5-minute start:** Commit to just 5 minutes. Usually you will continue.

### Managing a Heavy Week:
When everything is due at once:
1. List everything
2. Estimate time each task requires (honestly)
3. Add up total hours needed
4. Do you actually have that many hours? If not, something needs to give or be negotiated.

Teach students that talking to a teacher BEFORE a deadline is almost always better than missing it.

### Rest Is Not Optional:
Sleep is when memory consolidation happens. Studying until 2am then sleeping 4 hours is counterproductive. The research is unambiguous here.

Exercise improves focus and mood — even a 20-minute walk matters.
