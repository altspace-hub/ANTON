# Exam Strategy — Layer 3 Lesson Methodology
# Module: Exam Strategy | Area: Study Skills | Tier: T2–T3

## What You Are Teaching in This Session

Focus: **Performing well in exams — before, during, and after.**

Core topics:
- Building an effective revision plan
- Swedish NP (Nationellt prov) technique
- Reading exam questions correctly (command words)
- Managing nerves and mind blanks
- Time allocation during the exam
- Analysing mistakes after an exam

## Teaching Methodology for Exam Strategy

### The Before — Revision Planning:

The biggest mistake: starting revision too late and cramming.
The solution: work backwards from the exam date.

Revision plan structure:
1. List all topics that could appear
2. Rate each: confident / shaky / don't know
3. Prioritise "shaky" and "don't know" — revisiting what you know wastes time
4. Assign specific topics to specific sessions, working backwards from exam day
5. Leave 2-3 days before the exam for review, not new learning

Practice with past papers: the most effective revision for exams IS doing past exams.
Time yourself. Replicate the exam conditions.

### Swedish NP Specifics:
NP (Nationellt prov) grades on a criterion-referenced scale (E, C, A).
Teach students to read the marking criteria:
- E: basic demonstration of the requirement
- C: developed and nuanced response
- A: thorough, exemplified, connected to broader context

The word "redogör" means explain clearly. "Analysera" means go deeper — causes, consequences, relationships. "Värdera" means make a judgement and defend it.

Understanding what the question actually asks is half the battle.

### During the Exam — Time Allocation:
Before starting: read through ALL questions. Mark the ones you know well.
Allocate time proportionally to marks. A 2-mark question gets 5 minutes; a 10-mark question gets 20 minutes.

Start with questions you know well — builds confidence and ensures marks.
Don't leave blank answers — partial credit is always possible. Show your thinking.

### Managing a Mind Blank:
Common experience: "I knew this. Now it's gone."
What to do:
1. Skip and come back — sometimes other questions trigger memory
2. Write down everything you DO know about the topic — this often unlocks the rest
3. If a word is gone: describe what it means in plain language ("the process where plants turn sunlight into energy") — shows understanding even without the term

### The Day Before and Morning Of:
Day before: light review only. No new material. Sleep at normal time.
Morning of: eat breakfast, arrive early, have materials ready
In the exam room: controlled breathing if anxious (4 seconds in, hold 4, out 4)

### After the Exam — Learning from Mistakes:
Most students avoid looking at graded exams carefully.
The most efficient study tool is understanding exactly where and why you lost marks:
- "I didn't know this" → add to revision list
- "I knew this but explained it wrong" → practise articulating it
- "I misread the question" → practise underlining command words before answering
