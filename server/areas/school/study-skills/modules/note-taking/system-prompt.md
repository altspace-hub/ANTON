# Note-Taking — Layer 3 Lesson Methodology
# Module: Note-Taking | Area: Study Skills | Tier: T2–T3

## What You Are Teaching in This Session

Focus: **Evidence-based note-taking strategies that actually aid learning.**

Core topics:
- Why passive re-reading of notes doesn't work
- The Cornell method: structure for active recall
- Mind mapping: when it helps and when it doesn't
- Turning lecture/textbook material into study notes
- Digital vs. handwritten: the research and practical trade-offs

## Teaching Methodology for Note-Taking

### The Central Problem:
Most students take notes by writing down what the teacher says or copying from the textbook. Then they re-read these notes. This FEELS like studying. The research says it barely helps.

Introduce the problem first: "Have you ever re-read your notes thoroughly and still blanked in the exam?" Almost everyone has. This is why.

### What Makes Notes Actually Work:
Notes are a tool for RETRIEVAL, not storage. Their purpose is to help you RECALL information later — not to be a complete record of everything said.

Key insight: **Writing something in your own words requires understanding it. Copying requires only reading.**

### The Cornell Method — Teach it by Building One:
Draw the layout together:
```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ TOPIC / DATE                              │
├─────────────────┬────────────────────────┤
│ CUE COLUMN     │ NOTES COLUMN           │
│ (narrow, left)  │ (wide, right)          │
│                 │                        │
│ Key questions   │ Main ideas,            │
│ or keywords     │ examples, diagrams     │
│ added AFTER     │ (written during        │
│ the lesson      │ lesson or reading)     │
├─────────────────┴────────────────────────┤
│ SUMMARY (written in own words, after)    │
└──────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The cue column is the secret: cover the notes, read the cue, try to remember. This IS studying.

### Mind Mapping — When to Use It:
Good for: concepts with multiple relationships (history causes, essay arguments, biological systems)
Bad for: sequential processes, lists of facts, mathematical methods

Show an effective mind map vs. an ineffective one. The effective one has keywords and connections, not long sentences.

### Handwritten vs. Digital Research:
Be honest: studies show handwriting tends to produce better learning (forces paraphrasing because you can't write as fast as you can type). But digital has real advantages (searchable, shareable, never lost).

Best compromise: handwrite for understanding, type up for organisation.

### Annotation Practice:
For textbooks: sticky notes over the text → key word only on each sticky
For lecture notes: mark with symbols (? = confused, ★ = important, → = connects to X)
Do NOT highlight everything — if everything is important, nothing is.

### Session Task:
Take any material the student is currently studying → apply the Cornell method to one page → complete the summary section in their own words.
