# Personal Finance — Layer 3 Lesson Methodology
# Module: Personal Finance | Area: Life Skills | Tier: T2–T3

## What You Are Teaching in This Session

Focus: **Practical money management for young people in Sweden.**

Core topics:
- Building a monthly budget (income vs. expenses)
- Understanding Swedish taxes: what gets taken out and why
- Bank accounts: how they work, savings accounts, ISK accounts
- CSN: student grants and loans — how they work and what repayment means
- Consumer rights: cooling-off period, warranty, returns
- Avoiding debt traps: interest rates, sms-loans, payment notes (betalningsanmärkning)

## Teaching Methodology for Personal Finance

### Start Concrete, Not Abstract:
Never open with theory. Open with a scenario: "You just got paid 8 000 kr for your summer job. What happens next?" Work backwards from there.

### Budget Building — Step by Step:
1. List income: job, CSN, any allowance, odd jobs
2. List fixed costs: phone contract, gym membership, subscriptions
3. List variable costs: food, transport, clothes, socialising
4. What's left? Is it positive or negative?

Use real Swedish numbers. If teaching T2: pocket money + possible part-time job. T3: full CSN + part-time work.

### Swedish Tax Explanation:
The classic confusion: "Why do I only get 80% of what I earned?"
- Show a simple Swedish payslip: gross salary → preliminary tax → net salary
- Explain what taxes fund (schools, healthcare, roads, etc.)
- How to file: most Swedish tax returns are pre-filled — you just confirm or adjust

### Credit and Debt — Without Scaremongering:
Context first: "Credit is just borrowing money from your future self."
The danger is misunderstanding the cost:
- Calculate a real example: borrow 5 000 kr at 25% APR, minimum payments only
- sms-loans: the effective annual interest rate is often 200%+
- betalningsanmärkning: what it affects (apartment applications, phone contracts) and how long it lasts (3 years)

Make it real, not preachy: "This is information. Now you can make an informed decision."

### Consumer Rights:
- Ångervecka (14-day cooling off): when it applies (distance selling only, not in-store)
- Garantirätt vs. reklamationsrätt (warranty vs. complaint right — important distinction)
- Where to complain: Konsumentombudsmannen, Hallå Konsument

### Key Questions to Drive Discussion:
- "If you had 500 kr left over this month, what would you do with it? What would be most useful?"
- "Have you ever regretted a purchase? What made it a bad decision in retrospect?"
- "What would it look like to save 10% of everything you earn, starting now?"
