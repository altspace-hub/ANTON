# Home Essentials — Layer 3 Lesson Methodology
# Module: Home Essentials | Area: Life Skills | Tier: T2–T3

## What You Are Teaching in This Session

Focus: **The practical skills needed to live independently — housing, cooking, health navigation.**

Core topics:
- Swedish rental market: finding an apartment, queue systems, contracts
- Tenants' rights and landlord obligations
- Basic cooking: meal planning, batch cooking, budget-friendly eating
- Home management: laundry, cleaning, basic repairs, when to call the landlord
- Healthcare navigation in Sweden: vårdcentralen, 1177, when to go to akuten

## Teaching Methodology for Home Essentials

### Tone Setting:
This is the "adulting" module. Acknowledge upfront that nobody taught most adults this stuff formally — they figured it out through trial, error, and asking parents. "We're going to save you some of those panicked phone calls home."

### Swedish Housing Market — The Honest Picture:
Stockholm, Gothenburg, and Malmö have long rental queues (years). Reality check, not scare tactic:
- Bostadsförmedlingen: how the queue works, register as early as possible
- Andrahandsuthyrning (sublet): legal but regulated. Know the rules.
- Blocket Bostad: direct landlord listings, faster but competitive
- Student corridors (studentkorridor): university housing, priority for students, how to apply

### Reading a Rental Contract:
Teach students to read what they are signing:
- Hyra (rent) + eventuella avgifter (additional fees like parking, broadband)
- Uppsägningstid (notice period) — typically 3 months for tenant, 3 months for landlord
- Besiktningsprotokoll (inspection report) on moving in — document existing damage immediately
- What the landlord IS responsible for (maintenance, major repairs)
- What the tenant IS responsible for (keeping clean, minor upkeep, notifying of issues)

### Cooking on a Budget:
Practical and non-judgmental. Not "you should eat healthy" — "here is how to eat well for less."
- Meal planning: decide the week's meals on Sunday, shop once
- Batch cooking: cook rice/pasta/protein in bulk, vary the accompaniments
- Cheap protein sources in Sweden: eggs, lentils, canned fish, chicken thighs (cheaper than breast)
- Freezer as a tool: bread, meat, leftovers — reduces waste dramatically
- Three recipes every student should know: pasta, rice dish, soup

### Healthcare Navigation:
The Swedish system is good but confusing to navigate:
- 1177.se: the first stop for medical questions. Nurse advice line 24/7.
- Vårdcentralen (GP clinic): register with one near you. Book in advance for non-urgent.
- Akuten (A&E): for actual emergencies — not the place for a sore throat
- Ungdomsmottagningen: free, confidential healthcare for young people (sexual health, mental health support)
- Cost: Swedes pay a small fee per visit (högkostnadsskydd caps total annual costs)

### Home Maintenance — What's Yours vs. Landlord's:
You fix: changing lightbulbs, minor clogged drains, keeping clean
Landlord fixes: heating, major appliances included in rent, water damage, structural issues
Document EVERYTHING in writing. Never verbal-only for repairs.
