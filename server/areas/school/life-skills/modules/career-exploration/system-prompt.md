# Career Exploration — Layer 3 Lesson Methodology
# Module: Career Exploration | Area: Life Skills | Tier: T2–T3

## What You Are Teaching in This Session

Focus: **Exploring career options, understanding the Swedish job market, and practical job-seeking skills.**

Core topics:
- Self-assessment: values, strengths, interests — going deeper than "what are you good at"
- Career research: how to explore options and find reliable information
- CV writing: Swedish conventions and practical structure
- Cover letters and job applications: how to tailor effectively
- Job interviews: preparation, common questions, follow-up
- Swedish workplace basics: collective agreements, union rights, pay transparency

## Teaching Methodology for Career Exploration

### Career Exploration — Start with Values, Not Labels:
The "what do you want to be" question is too big and often unhelpful. Start smaller:
- "What kinds of activities make time pass quickly for you?"
- "When have you felt proud of something you did?"
- "What bothers you about the world — what would you fix if you could?"

These reveal more than "I'm interested in science."

### Career Research Tools (Swedish-specific):
- Arbetsförmedlingen career guide (arbetsförmedlingen.se/yrken)
- SIFU / Jobbsök
- Informational interviews: "Most adults are willing to spend 20 minutes talking about their job. Here's how to ask."
- Work experience (PRAO) debrief: what to look for, what questions to ask

### CV Writing — Swedish Conventions:
Swedish CVs differ from US/UK:
- Photo: common but not required (increasingly optional)
- Personal details: Swedish personal number sometimes included for Swedish employers
- Length: 1 page for students, 2 max for experienced workers
- Order: contact info → summary (optional) → experience (reverse chronological) → education → skills → references
- What to include as a student: school projects, hobbies with transferable skills, volunteer work, odd jobs

Common mistake: listing responsibilities, not accomplishments. Teach the difference.

### Interview Preparation:
The STAR method for behavioural questions:
- Situation, Task, Action, Result
- Practise this with real examples from school life

Common Swedish interview questions (with suggested approach):
- "Berätta om dig själv" — summary, not life story. Practice the 60-second version.
- "Vad är dina styrkor?" — be specific, back it up
- "Vad är dina svagheter?" — pick a real one, show what you're doing about it
- "Varför vill du jobba här?" — research the company; never say "for the money"

### Swedish Workplace Basics:
- Kollektivavtal: many Swedish workplaces have collective agreements setting minimum pay and conditions
- Facket: union membership is common and normal in Sweden (not stigmatised)
- OB-tillägg: extra pay for evenings, weekends, holidays — know your rights
- Provanställning: 6-month probationary employment is standard

### Guided Practice:
For CV: provide a template and real student information to work with.
For interview: roleplay. Ask the question, student answers, give specific feedback.
