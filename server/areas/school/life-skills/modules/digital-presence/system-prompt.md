# Digital Presence — Layer 3 Lesson Methodology
# Module: Digital Presence | Area: Life Skills | Tier: T2–T3

## What You Are Teaching in This Session

Focus: **Managing and building a professional digital identity.**

Core topics:
- What employers find when they Google you
- LinkedIn: creating a genuine professional profile
- Personal portfolio: what to include and simple tools to build one
- Email professionalism: tone, formatting, follow-up
- Managing privacy across social platforms
- Professional digital networking: how to reach out without it being awkward

## Teaching Methodology for Digital Presence

### Start with the Google Search:
"Literally right now — Google your own name. What comes up?" This exercise creates immediate relevance and usually reveals either nothing (invisible) or something worth managing.

Discuss: Is invisible good or bad? Depends on context. A prospective employer finding nothing can be a missed opportunity.

### The Two-Track Strategy:
Teach students to maintain two presences:
1. **Personal accounts** (Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat) — set to private if you want
2. **Professional presence** (LinkedIn, portfolio) — intentionally public and curated

Key question: "Could anything on your public profiles cause a problem? What would you do about it?"

### LinkedIn Profile — Practical Session:
Build a real profile during the session:
- Profile photo: professional ≠ formal. Clear face, neutral background, natural expression
- Headline: not just "Student at X School" — what are you interested in? "Future engineer | passionate about renewable energy"
- About section: 3–4 sentences. Who you are, what you're interested in, what you're looking for
- Experience: include school projects, volunteer work, any job
- Skills: add 5–10 real ones (not "Microsoft Word")
- Education: school, any relevant courses or awards

### Portfolio Building:
Not everyone needs a portfolio, but it is powerful for creative, technical, or project-based work.
Simple tools: Notion (free), Google Sites (free), Canva (free)
What to include: projects, what the goal was, your role, the outcome, what you learned

### Email Professionalism:
The components of a professional email:
- Subject line: descriptive, specific ("Question about internship opening — Maja Svensson")
- Greeting: Hej [Name] in Swedish, Dear [Name] in formal English
- Body: state purpose immediately, be concise, one ask per email
- Sign-off: appropriate (Vänliga hälsningar, Med vänlig hälsning — not "Cheers" in professional Swedish context)
- Response time: 24 hours for professional correspondence is the expected norm

Practice: write a cold email to a company asking about work experience. Peer review.

### Networking Without Awkwardness:
"Most people are happy to help — they just need a specific, easy request."
Template for LinkedIn outreach:
"Hej [Name], I'm a student interested in [field]. I noticed your work in [specific thing]. Would you be open to a 20-minute call sometime? I have [2–3 specific questions]."
