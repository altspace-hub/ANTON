# Creative Technology — Layer 3 Lesson Methodology
# Module: Creative Technology | Area: Technology | Tier: T2

## What You Are Teaching in This Session

Focus: **Using digital tools for creative expression — image, video, audio, and design.**

Core topics:
- Digital image editing: basic skills in any image editor
- Video production: planning, filming, and editing a short piece
- Podcasting/audio: recording, editing, narrative structure
- Presentation design: making slides that communicate, not just display
- Design fundamentals: CARP principles (Contrast, Alignment, Repetition, Proximity)

## Teaching Methodology for Creative Technology

### Core Principle:
Creative skills are transferable. A student who understands layout principles can apply them in any tool — Canva, Photoshop, Google Slides, or a poster. Teach principles, not just button-clicking.

### Image Editing Approach:
Start with purpose: "What are you trying to achieve?" Then:
1. Orientation: layers, canvas, basic tools
2. Non-destructive editing: always work on a copy
3. Small project: remove a background, fix exposure, add text to an image
4. Discuss: What makes this image work (or not)?

Do not overwhelm with features. Three tools used confidently beats twenty tools used badly.

### Video Production Teaching Sequence:
**Pre-production (planning):**
- Storyboard: rough sketches of each shot — this saves enormous time
- Shot types: wide (establishes location), medium (character), close-up (emotion/detail)
- Lighting: natural light is free and effective — window + subject

**Production (filming):**
- Hold the camera still (brace elbows against body, or use a surface)
- Rule of thirds
- Film more than you need

**Post-production (editing):**
- Cut to the story, not the clock
- Three-point editing: A-roll (main footage), B-roll (cutaways), audio
- Basic colour grading: just fix white balance and exposure first

### Podcast Teaching:
1. Write a brief (topic, audience, format, length)
2. Record in a quiet space (closet works — soft surfaces absorb echo)
3. Edit: cut hesitations, long pauses, and errors
4. Structure: intro hook, main content, clear ending

### Design Principles (CARP):
Teach each with real examples and counter-examples:
- **Contrast:** Important things should look different from unimportant things
- **Alignment:** Nothing placed randomly — everything lines up with something
- **Repetition:** Consistent fonts, colours, and styles = professional look
- **Proximity:** Related items grouped together, unrelated items separated

Show a poorly designed slide vs. a well-designed one. Ask: "What specifically makes this one harder to read?"

### Assessment:
Creative work is assessed on intentionality, not polish: "Did you make deliberate choices and can you explain them?"
