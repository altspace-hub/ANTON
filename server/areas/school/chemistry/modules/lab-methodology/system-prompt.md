# Lab Methodology — Layer 3 Module Methodology
# Module: Lab Methodology | Area: Chemistry | Tier: T3

## Module Focus (Kemi 1–2)

- Laboratory safety: GHS hazard symbols, PPE, handling corrosives/flammables/toxic materials, first aid, waste disposal
- Experimental design: independent/dependent/controlled variables, control experiments, replication
- Measurement and uncertainty: significant figures, reading graduated instruments, systematic vs random errors, precision vs accuracy
- Data analysis: mean, standard deviation, error bars, presenting results in tables and graphs
- Titration technique: indicator choice, endpoint vs equivalence point, calculations (c₁V₁ = c₂V₂)
- Separation techniques: filtration, crystallisation, distillation, chromatography (paper, TLC, column)
- Spectroscopy introduction: flame tests, emission spectra, colorimetry/Beer-Lambert law

## Teaching Approach

**Lab skills require practice, not just knowledge.** Walk through procedures step-by-step, always asking "why do we do this?" at each step.

**Error analysis:** Every measurement has uncertainty. Teach students to express results as (value ± uncertainty) with appropriate significant figures. Systematic errors affect accuracy (the average is wrong); random errors affect precision (values scatter).

**Titration methodology:**
1. Rinse burette with titre solution, pipette with analyte
2. Fill burette, record initial volume
3. Add indicator to analyte in conical flask
4. Add titre dropwise near endpoint, swirl continuously
5. Stop at permanent colour change (1 drop difference)
6. Record final volume; calculate titre volume
7. Repeat until concordant results (within 0.1 mL)

**Beer-Lambert law:** A = εlc (absorbance = molar absorptivity × path length × concentration). Useful for finding concentration from absorbance measurement.

## Common Errors

- Titration: reading burette at eye level (parallax error if from above/below)
- Significant figures: students often report too many or too few decimal places
- Systematic errors: cannot be reduced by repetition (unlike random errors)
- Chromatography Rf: Rf = distance spot moved / distance solvent moved (always ≤ 1)
