# Chemical Reactions and Equilibrium — Layer 3 Module Methodology
# Module: Reactions | Area: Chemistry | Tier: T3

## Module Focus (Kemi 1–2)

- Reaction types: combination, decomposition, single/double displacement, combustion, redox, acid-base
- Stoichiometry: mole ratios from balanced equations, limiting reagent, percentage yield
- Chemical equilibrium: dynamic equilibrium, equilibrium constant K (Kc, Kp)
- Le Chatelier's principle: effect of concentration, pressure, temperature changes on equilibrium position
- Acid-base equilibria: Ka, Kb, pKa, Henderson-Hasselbalch equation, buffer solutions
- Reaction kinetics: rate laws, rate constant k, Arrhenius equation (k = Ae^(−Ea/RT)), activation energy
- Thermochemistry: ΔH (enthalpy), Hess's law, ΔG = ΔH − TΔS (Gibbs free energy and spontaneity)
- Electrochemistry: standard electrode potentials, cell EMF, Nernst equation

## Teaching Approach

**Le Chatelier as a stress response:** "The system is in equilibrium. We apply a stress. The equilibrium shifts to minimise that stress. Always ask: which direction reduces the stress?"

**Stoichiometry methodology:**
1. Write and balance the equation
2. Convert given quantities to moles
3. Use mole ratios to find moles of desired substance
4. Convert back to required units
5. Identify limiting reagent (whichever runs out first)

**Ka and pH:** Ka is the equilibrium constant for acid dissociation. Larger Ka = stronger acid = lower pH (for equal concentration). pH = −log[H⁺]. For weak acids: [H⁺] = √(Ka × C).

**Gibbs Free Energy:** ΔG < 0 = spontaneous. ΔG = ΔH − TΔS — temperature determines which factor dominates.

## Common Errors

- Le Chatelier and temperature: adding heat shifts equilibrium away from the exothermic direction
- Limiting reagent: it's the one that produces LESS product, not the one present in less mass
- Ka expressions: only include aqueous species (not pure liquids or solids)
- Nernst equation sign: Q > K means reaction proceeds in reverse; Q < K means forward
