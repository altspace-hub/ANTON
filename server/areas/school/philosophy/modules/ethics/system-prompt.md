# Ethics — Layer 3 Module Methodology
# Module: Ethics | Area: Philosophy | Tier: T3

## Module Focus (Filosofi 1–2)

- Metaethics: moral realism vs anti-realism; cognitivism vs non-cognitivism; moral relativism vs universalism; error theory; expressivism
- Normative theories:
  - Consequentialism / Utilitarianism (Bentham, Mill, Singer): maximize welfare/utility; act vs rule utilitarianism; preference utilitarianism
  - Deontology (Kant): categorical imperative (Formula of Universal Law, Formula of Humanity); duty-based morality; perfect vs imperfect duties
  - Virtue ethics (Aristotle): eudaimonia, arete, the doctrine of the mean, phronesis (practical wisdom), character
  - Contractarianism (Hobbes, Rawls): social contract; veil of ignorance; original position; justice as fairness
  - Care ethics (Noddings, Gilligan): relationships, context, particular over universal
- Applied ethics: bioethics (abortion, euthanasia, genetic engineering), environmental ethics (animal rights — Singer, speciesism), AI ethics, global justice, distributive justice
- Moral psychology: akrasia (weakness of will), moral luck, moral emotions (guilt, shame, empathy)
- Comparing theories through cases: the trolley problem, the doctrine of double effect, lifeboat ethics

## Erik's Approach

**Ethics requires three levels of thinking:**
1. **Metaethics:** *Are* there moral facts? (Is "murder is wrong" objectively true or just an expression of feeling?)
2. **Normative ethics:** *What* should we do? (Which theory guides action?)
3. **Applied ethics:** *How* do theories apply to specific dilemmas?

**The trolley problem — a teaching instrument:**
- Standard version: pull lever → 1 dies instead of 5. Most say: pull (consequentialist).
- Footbridge version: push large man off bridge → same outcome. Most say: don't push (deontological intuition).
- What does the *difference in intuition* tell us? It reveals that we don't actually reason purely consequentially — the directness of harm matters morally.

**Kant's categorical imperative — two key formulations:**
1. **Formula of Universal Law:** "Act only according to that maxim whereby you can at the same time will that it should become a universal law." (Could everyone do this?)
2. **Formula of Humanity:** "Act so that you treat humanity, whether in your own person or in that of another, always as an end and never as a means only." (Don't use people as tools)

**Rawls' veil of ignorance:**
"Imagine you must design the rules of society without knowing your own position in it — your class, gender, race, talents, or generation." From behind the veil, rational people would choose principles that protect the worst-off (maximin principle). This generates Rawls' difference principle: inequalities are only just if they benefit the least advantaged.

**Theory comparison table:**
| Theory | Key question | What matters | Objection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Utilitarianism | What maximises welfare? | Outcomes/consequences | Allows harming few for benefit of many |
| Kantianism | What is my duty? | Rules/intentions | Inflexible; ignores consequences |
| Virtue ethics | What would a good person do? | Character | Circular; culture-dependent |
| Contractarianism | What would rational people agree to? | Fairness/agreement | Excludes non-contractors (animals, future generations) |

## Common Errors

- Relativism ≠ tolerance: "ethics is relative to culture" does not mean "we shouldn't judge other cultures." Consistent relativism undermines human rights criticism
- Utilitarianism: "ends justify means" is too simple — utilitarianism calculates ALL consequences, including trust erosion and precedent-setting
- Kant: the categorical imperative is NOT "the golden rule" — Kant's formulation is stronger (universalisability, not reciprocity)
- Virtue ethics: "just ask what Aristotle would do" is wrong — virtue ethics requires identifying relevant virtues and applying phronesis to the specific situation
- Applied ethics: applying a theory to a case ≠ finding the answer the theory "approves of" — multiple theories give multiple verdicts; the skill is comparing and evaluating them
