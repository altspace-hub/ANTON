# Literary Analysis — Layer 3 Module Methodology
# Module: Literary Analysis | Area: Swedish Advanced | Tier: T3

## Module Focus (Svenska 2–3)

- Close reading: textual evidence, quotation integration, in-text citation (author, page/line)
- Literary periods and movements: Romanticism, Realism, Modernism, Postmodernism — Swedish and Nordic contexts
- Narrative analysis: narrator (I-narrator, omniscient, unreliable); point of view; narrative levels; focalization (Gérard Genette)
- Character analysis: static vs dynamic characters, motivation, development arc, foil characters
- Themes and motifs: identifying central themes; recurring motifs; symbolic readings
- Language and style: diction, syntax, imagery, metaphor, simile, irony, paradox, allusion; tone vs mood
- Drama analysis: stage directions, dialogue, dramatic irony, tragedy (Aristotle's hamartia, catharsis), comedy, absurdist theatre
- Poetry: prosody (metre, rhythm, rhyme scheme), free verse, lyrical speaker, imagery, sound devices (alliteration, assonance, consonance)
- Comparative analysis: comparing two texts (theme, narrative technique, perspective, context)
- Reception and context: historical, biographical, sociocultural context; reader response theory; intertextuality
- Argumentative literary essay: thesis → body paragraphs (claim–evidence–analysis) → conclusion; academic register; source criticism

## Saga's Approach

**A text is not self-evident — it is constructed.** Every literary choice (word, structure, point of view, image) is deliberate. The analyst's job is to ask: *Why this choice? What effect does it create?*

**The PEA model for literary paragraphs:**
- **Point:** State the analytical claim
- **Evidence:** Quote the text (integrate with quotation marks, cite source)
- **Analysis:** Explain *how* the evidence supports the point — connect language to meaning

Example (poor): "The author uses a metaphor." (describes, no analysis)
Example (good): "The comparison of autumn to 'a slow forgetting' (p. 34) reinforces the narrator's sense of loss as something gradual and inevitable — not dramatic, but inexorable."

**Contextual reading:** A text cannot be fully understood in isolation. Ask:
- When was it written? What was happening historically?
- Who wrote it, and from what social position?
- Who was the intended reader?

**Nordic literary canon** — key authors by period:
- Romanticism: Esaias Tegnér, Erik Gustaf Geijer, Almqvist
- Realism/Naturalism: August Strindberg, Victoria Benedictsson, Selma Lagerlöf
- Modernism: Pär Lagerkvist, Eyvind Johnson, Harry Martinson
- Contemporary: Toni Kandre, P.O. Enquist, Sara Stridsberg

## Common Errors

- Summarising instead of analysing: "In the story, X happens and then Y happens" gives plot, not analysis
- Unsupported claims: every analytical claim must be backed by textual evidence; assertions without quotes are opinions
- Confusing author and narrator: the narrator is a narrative construct, not necessarily the author's voice
- Theme vs topic: topic = what the text is about (war); theme = what it says about it (war dehumanises those who fight)
- Irony: verbal irony (says one thing, means another), dramatic irony (reader knows more than character), situational irony (outcome opposite to expectations) — be precise about which type
