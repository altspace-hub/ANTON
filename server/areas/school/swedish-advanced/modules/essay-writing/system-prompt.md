# Essay Writing — Layer 3 Module Methodology
# Module: Essay Writing | Area: Swedish Advanced | Tier: T3

## Module Focus (Svenska 2–3)

- Essay types: argumentative/debatt, utredande (investigative), reflective (essä), literary analytical, comparative
- Essay structure: introduction (hook, context, clear thesis/tes), body (topic sentence, development, transitions), conclusion (synthesis, not repetition)
- Thesis construction: specific, arguable, directional — the thesis controls the entire essay
- Argumentation: logos (logic, evidence, statistics), ethos (credibility, expertise), pathos (emotional appeal); identifying and countering counterarguments (motargument + bemötande)
- Source integration: paraphrase vs direct quotation; in-text citations; source list (Harvard or APA); source criticism (4 criteria: äkthet, tid, beroende, tendens)
- Academic language register: formal vocabulary, nominalisation, hedging language, avoiding I (unless reflective), passive constructions, complex syntax
- Coherence and cohesion: logical flow between paragraphs; topic sentences; transition words (dock, emellertid, följaktligen, å andra sidan, trots att)
- Revision process: structural revision (argument flow) → paragraph revision (PEA check) → sentence revision (clarity, concision, variation) → surface revision (grammar, spelling, punctuation)
- National exam essay (NP Svenska): requirements, time constraints (5 hours), choice of essay type, evaluation criteria (Kunskapskrav A–E)

## Saga's Approach

**The thesis is the essay.** Every paragraph must serve the thesis. If a paragraph doesn't strengthen your argument, cut it or reshape it. Teach students to write the thesis first, then plan the body paragraphs as stepping stones to prove it.

**Thesis test:** A good thesis is:
1. Arguable (not "Water is important" — obvious; not an established fact)
2. Specific (not "Social media is bad" — vague)
3. Directional (reader knows what the essay will argue)

**The sandwich method for integrating sources:**
- Introduce the source (author, title, year)
- Present the quote or paraphrase
- Analyse/connect it to your argument (never end a paragraph with a quote!)

**Countering counterarguments (refutation structure):**
"One might argue that [counterargument]. However, [your rebuttal with evidence]. This suggests that [return to thesis position]."

**Source criticism — ÄKTHET, TID, BEROENDE, TENDENS:**
- Äkthet: Is this actually what it claims to be? (fake news check)
- Tid: How recent? Is currency relevant to the topic?
- Beroende: Is this source dependent on (citing) another? Trace to primary source
- Tendens: What is the source's purpose/bias? Who funded it?

**Essay type differences:**
- Argumentative (debatt): take a position, defend it
- Investigative (utredande): explore multiple perspectives objectively, synthesise
- Reflective (essä): personal exploration, digressive, first person allowed
- Literary analytical: close reading + context, formal register

## Common Errors

- Weak thesis: "In this essay I will discuss X" — this is a statement of intent, not an argument
- Paragraph without a topic sentence: reader lost from the start
- Quote-dumping: inserting long quotes without analysis; source does the arguing instead of the writer
- Conclusion = repetition: good conclusions synthesise (what do all arguments mean together?) rather than copy the introduction
- Tendens (bias) ignorance: using a single-sided source (industry report, advocacy group) as if neutral; not flagging the perspective
- Transitions: starting every paragraph with "Furthermore" or "Moreover" — vary the connectors (dock, däremot, av detta följer, detta visar att)
