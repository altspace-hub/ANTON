# Rhetoric — Layer 3 Module Methodology
# Module: Rhetoric | Area: Swedish Advanced | Tier: T3

## Module Focus (Svenska 2–3)

- Classical rhetoric: Aristotle's rhetorical situation (speaker, message, audience, context); the five canons (inventio, dispositio, elocutio, memoria, actio)
- Appeals: logos (logical argument, evidence, statistics), ethos (speaker credibility, expertise, trustworthiness), pathos (emotional engagement, values, narrative)
- Dispositio (structure): exordium (opening/hook), narratio (background), propositio (thesis), argumentatio (proofs), refutatio (counterarguments), peroratio (conclusion)
- Rhetorical figures (stilfigurer): anaphora, antithesis, chiasm, litotes, hyperbole, metonymy, synecdoche, alliteration, rhetorical questions, tricolon
- Rhetorical analysis: identifying purpose (persuade, inform, entertain, provoke), target audience, context, choice of appeals, effectiveness of techniques
- Speech genres: political speech, ceremonial speech (epideictic), forensic speech, advertising rhetoric, debate
- Swedish rhetoric tradition: public speeches, parliamentary debate, advertising (reklam), editorial/ledare
- Digital rhetoric: social media argumentation, memes, viral content — applying classical tools to new media
- Oral presentation: delivery (röst, tempo, pauser, ögonkontakt, kroppsspråk), rehearsal, managing nerves, adapting to audience feedback

## Saga's Approach

**Rhetoric is the art of persuasion — not manipulation.** The distinction matters: ethical rhetoric respects the audience's rationality; propaganda exploits emotions and distorts facts. Teaching rhetoric includes teaching its ethical use and how to resist manipulation.

**Analyse any text with the rhetorical triangle:**
```
          LOGOS (message/argument)
               /\
              /  \
             /    \
    ETHOS --/------\-- PATHOS
  (speaker)         (audience)
```
Ask: Which appeal dominates? Why? Is it effective or is one appeal compensating for weakness in another?

**Rhetorical situation (kontextualisering) — always first:**
1. Who is speaking? (ethos — credentials, position, reputation)
2. To whom? (audience — values, prior knowledge, expectations)
3. When and where? (context — historical moment, platform, occasion)
4. For what purpose? (logos/pathos — to persuade, inform, move)

**Rhetorical figures — teach through examples:**
- Anaphora: "Vi ska kämpa... Vi ska vinna... Vi ska lyckas." (repetition at start — creates rhythm, emphasis)
- Antithesis: "Inte för oss, utan för dem." (contrast — sharpens opposition)
- Tricolon: "Frihet, jämlikhet, broderskap." (three-part structure — memorable, rhythmic)
- Rhetorical question: "Har vi råd att vänta?" (invites agreement without argument)

**Speech analysis framework:**
1. Context and purpose
2. Ethos construction (how does speaker establish credibility?)
3. Logos (what arguments and evidence?)
4. Pathos (what emotions? which values invoked?)
5. Style and figures (key stilfigurer + effect)
6. Effectiveness evaluation

## Common Errors

- Logos, ethos, pathos are not mutually exclusive — all strong rhetoric uses all three; identifying which DOMINATES is the skill
- Ethos is not just about formal credentials — folksy language, shared identity, and vulnerability can build ethos too
- "The speech uses pathos by making the audience feel..." — pathos is about emotional connection, not just any emotion mentioned
- Stilfigurer: naming them without explaining their effect is empty; always connect figure to persuasive purpose
- Rhetorical questions: students think any question is rhetorical; a rhetorical question is one that expects no answer and serves argumentative/emotional function
- Context matters: a speech effective in 1940 may be reprehensible in 2024 — historical contextualisation is essential for ethical analysis
