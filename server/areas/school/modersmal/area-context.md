# Modersmål (Heritage Language) — Subject Area Context
# Area: Modersmål | Tier: T2–T3 | Curriculum: Kursplan Modersmål (Lgr22)

## What This Subject Is

Modersmål is a school subject for students with a mother tongue other than Swedish. The goal is to develop and maintain the student's heritage language alongside Swedish — not to replace one with the other. Bilingualism is a cognitive and cultural asset.

The subject covers students from Year 1–9 (Lgr22) and into Gymnasiet, typically 40–80 minutes per week in a separate group.

## Centralt Innehåll (Core Content by Tier)

### T2 — Years 7–9

**Reading and comprehension:**
- Age-appropriate texts in the heritage language (fiction, non-fiction, news, poetry)
- Literary analysis: plot, character, themes, narrative perspective
- Identifying main ideas, implicit meaning, textual evidence

**Writing:**
- Personal texts (diary, letter, personal essay)
- Factual texts (report, summary, description)
- Argumentative texts (opinion, debate)
- Orthography and grammar specific to the heritage language

**Oral skills:**
- Formal and informal register in the heritage language
- Oral presentations on personal and cultural topics
- Listening comprehension: different speakers, accents, media

**Cultural and identity content:**
- Literature, music, history and traditions from the heritage language community
- Bilingual identity — navigating between cultures
- How the heritage language relates to Swedish and other languages in the student's life

**Language awareness:**
- Comparing grammar structures between heritage language and Swedish
- Code-switching: when and why people switch languages
- Language change over time and across diaspora communities

### T3 — Gymnasiet

**Advanced literary analysis:**
- Canonical texts from the heritage language's literary tradition
- Comparative reading across cultures and time periods
- Rhetorical and stylistic analysis

**Advanced writing:**
- Academic and formal texts in the heritage language
- Literary analysis essay
- Research-based writing

**Translation and interpretation:**
- Basic principles of translation: equivalence, cultural adaptation
- Interpreting community concepts from heritage culture into Swedish context

## Pedagogical Principles for Modersmål

**Heritage language is not a problem to overcome.** The student's home language is a complete language system with its own grammar, literature, and cultural depth. The teacher never implies that Swedish is superior or that the heritage language should be replaced.

**Meet the student where they are.** Heritage language proficiency varies enormously — from near-native literacy to oral-only, from formal to informal registers. Assessment should reflect actual proficiency in the heritage language, not Swedish.

**Bilingualism as a strength frame.** Research consistently shows that students who maintain and develop their heritage language perform better academically overall. Frame the subject's value in these terms.

**Cultural bridge, not museum piece.** Heritage cultures are living, changing communities — not frozen historical artefacts. Include contemporary media, music, literature, and current events from the heritage language community.

## Common Heritage Language Groups in Swedish Schools

Arabic, Somali, Kurdish (Kurmanji and Sorani), Dari/Pashto, Spanish, Turkish, Polish, Romanian, Tigrinya, Albanian, Bosnian/Croatian/Serbian — each with distinct linguistic and cultural dimensions. The teacher adjusts to the specific language and community.
