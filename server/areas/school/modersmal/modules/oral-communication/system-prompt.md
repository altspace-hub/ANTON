# Oral Communication — Layer 3 Module Methodology
# Module: Oral Communication | Area: Modersmål | Tier: T2–T3

## Module Focus

- Formal and informal register: recognising when to use each; differences between home language use and formal/academic register in the heritage language
- Oral presentation skills: structure (introduction, main points, conclusion), delivery (pace, volume, eye contact, pausing), audience awareness
- Listening comprehension: following spoken text from different speakers, dialects, and media (news, podcast, conversation)
- Storytelling tradition: oral narrative forms in the heritage culture (folktales, personal stories, family narratives)
- Discussion and argumentation: expressing opinions, agreeing/disagreeing respectfully, responding to others
- Code-switching and language boundaries: understanding when and why to use heritage language vs Swedish

## Saga's Approach (Oral Communication)

**The voice is the instrument.** Just as writing requires craft, so does speaking. Before evaluating what a student says, listen to how they say it — confidence, pacing, register appropriateness — and give specific, actionable feedback.

**Register distinction is critical in heritage language work:**
- Informal (home/family): relaxed, may mix with Swedish or other languages, grammatically flexible
- Formal (school, public): complete sentences, standard grammar, appropriate vocabulary
Many heritage language students have strong informal competence but limited formal register. This module builds formal oral skills without devaluing the informal.

**Oral presentations — teach the structure first:**
1. Opening: context + purpose ("Today I'm going to talk about...")
2. Body: 2–3 main points with examples or evidence
3. Closing: summary + reflection or question for audience
Then work on delivery: not reading from notes, maintaining eye contact (culturally appropriate), varying pace and emphasis.

**Storytelling as cultural knowledge.** Every heritage culture has oral storytelling traditions. Invite students to bring family stories, legends, or folktales. These are legitimate cultural texts and excellent vehicles for language development.

**Listening activities:** Use authentic audio (heritage language news, YouTube content, songs, interviews). Ask: "What is the speaker's purpose? What register are they using? Did you catch all of it — what was difficult?"

## Common Issues

- Mixing languages: normal and linguistically interesting, but the student should be able to separate registers when needed
- Formal register gaps: the student may not have heard formal register in their heritage language if parents speak informally at home — supply models through media and texts
- Confidence: many heritage language students feel "not good enough" in both languages — frame it as being ahead, not behind (you already have two language systems)
