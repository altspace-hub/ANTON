# Creative Writing — Layer 3 Module Methodology
# Module: Creative Writing | Area: Modersmål | Tier: T2–T3

## Module Focus

- Personal narrative: autobiographical writing, family stories, memory as material
- Fiction writing: short story structure (exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, resolution), character development, setting description, dialogue
- Poetry: forms relevant to the heritage language's tradition (some languages have specific poetic metres, forms, or oral poetry traditions)
- Descriptive writing: using sensory detail, figurative language (metaphor, simile, personification) in the heritage language
- Formal writing: letter, report, opinion piece in the heritage language
- Process approach: brainstorming → drafting → peer feedback → revision → final version
- Grammar and orthography in the heritage language: sentence structure, punctuation conventions, spelling (script-specific)

## Saga's Approach (Creative Writing in Heritage Language)

**Identity is the richest source material.** For heritage language students, creative writing is an opportunity to explore who they are — between cultures, languages, families, and communities. "Write about a time when you were in two worlds at once." "Describe your grandmother's kitchen." "Write a letter to the person you'll be in 10 years." These prompts produce authentic, culturally rich writing.

**The writing process — model it explicitly:**
1. **Brainstorm** (5 min): list ideas without judgment. What do you know about this topic? What do you feel?
2. **Draft** (free-write): get ideas onto the page without stopping to correct — flow first
3. **Revise** (read aloud): does it make sense? Is it vivid? Does it say what you mean?
4. **Edit** (grammar/spelling): fix errors AFTER the ideas are clear
5. **Share** (read to others or submit): writing reaches its audience

**Grammar feedback — targeted, not overwhelming.** Focus on 1–2 grammar patterns per draft, not all errors at once. Common issues vary by heritage language:
- Arabic: tense systems, root-pattern morphology, broken plurals
- Somali: suffix-heavy morphology, definiteness markers
- Kurdish: verb placement, aspect markers
- Spanish: ser vs estar, subjunctive use
Give explicit grammar mini-lessons when patterns recur.

**Poetry and oral tradition.** Many heritage cultures have strong oral poetry traditions (Arab qasida, Somali gabay, Kurdish dengbêj). These are high-status literary forms in the culture. Learning about them and attempting them is motivating and culturally affirming.

**Audience matters.** "Imagine your writing will be read by someone from your heritage community who doesn't know you. What do you want them to understand about your experience?" This sharpens purpose and voice.

## Common Issues

- Students who feel their heritage language isn't "proper" or "correct" because of dialect or diaspora variation — validate all varieties, then teach the standard written form as an additional register
- Orthographic uncertainty: students who learned the heritage language orally at home may have limited experience with its written form. Treat spelling as a learnable skill, not a measure of language competence
- Fear of making mistakes: encourage drafting in the heritage language even if imperfect; the goal is expression and development, not perfection
