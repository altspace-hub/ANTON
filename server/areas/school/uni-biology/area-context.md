# Cell Biology & Genetics — University Level

## Subject Overview
University biology at the cell and molecular level investigates the architecture and function of
cells, the storage and expression of genetic information, and the mechanisms of heredity and
evolution. Students integrate biochemistry, genetics, and cell biology to understand how life
operates from the molecular scale upward. Quantitative reasoning and experimental design are
central to the discipline.

## Core Areas

### Cell Biology & Ultrastructure
- Prokaryotic vs. eukaryotic cell architecture; membrane-bound organelles and their functions
- Plasma membrane: fluid mosaic model, lipid bilayer, membrane proteins, transport mechanisms
- Cytoskeleton: actin, microtubules, intermediate filaments; motor proteins (kinesin, dynein, myosin)
- Endomembrane system: ER (rough and smooth), Golgi, vesicular trafficking, secretory pathway
- Mitochondria and chloroplasts: endosymbiotic theory, structure, genome, biogenesis

### Molecular Biology: DNA, RNA, and Protein
- DNA structure: Watson-Crick base pairing, antiparallel strands, major and minor grooves
- DNA replication: semi-conservative model, replication fork, leading/lagging strand, Okazaki fragments
- Transcription: promoters, RNA polymerase, elongation, termination; prokaryote vs. eukaryote differences
- RNA processing (eukaryotes): 5' cap, poly-A tail, splicing, alternative splicing
- Translation: ribosomes, codons, tRNA charging, elongation cycle; post-translational modifications
- Gene regulation: operons (lac, trp); enhancers, silencers, transcription factors, chromatin remodelling

### Genetics & Heredity
- Mendelian genetics: law of segregation, law of independent assortment; monohybrid and dihybrid crosses
- Extensions of Mendelism: incomplete dominance, codominance, multiple alleles, epistasis, pleiotropy
- Linkage and recombination: genetic maps; chi-square test for independence
- Sex determination and sex-linked inheritance; X-inactivation (Lyon hypothesis)
- Chromosomal aberrations: aneuploidy (Down syndrome), inversions, translocations, deletions

### Molecular Genetics & Genomics
- Mutations: point mutations (transitions, transversions), frameshift, nonsense; repair mechanisms
- Recombinant DNA technology: restriction enzymes, gel electrophoresis, cloning vectors, PCR
- DNA sequencing: Sanger method, next-generation sequencing (Illumina)
- Genomics and functional genomics: genome annotation, microarrays, RNA-seq
- Epigenetics: DNA methylation, histone modification, chromatin remodelling, imprinting

### Evolution & Population Genetics
- Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium: assumptions and deviations (selection, drift, mutation, migration)
- Natural selection: directional, stabilising, disruptive; fitness and adaptation
- Genetic drift, founder effect, bottleneck effect
- Molecular evolution: neutral theory (Kimura), molecular clocks, phylogenetics
- Speciation: allopatric, sympatric, prezygotic and postzygotic barriers

## Pedagogical Approach
Professor Lindström focuses on mechanistic understanding:
1. **Structure determines function** — always relate molecular/cellular structure to biological function
2. **Experimental evidence** — discuss the key experiments that established each concept
3. **Central dogma as a framework** — use DNA → RNA → Protein as an organising principle
4. **Quantitative reasoning** — apply Hardy-Weinberg equations, calculate map distances, analyse data
5. **Model organisms** — contextualise findings using E. coli, yeast, Drosophila, C. elegans, Arabidopsis

## Academic Standards
- Use correct gene and protein nomenclature (italics for genes, roman for proteins)
- Reference authoritative texts: Alberts (Molecular Biology of the Cell), Lewin's Genes, Griffiths (Genetics)
- Distinguish between correlation and causation in biological studies
- Connect fundamental mechanisms to biomedical relevance where appropriate
