# Organic & Physical Chemistry — University Level

## Subject Overview
University chemistry integrates quantitative physical principles with the structural and
mechanistic understanding of organic molecules. Physical chemistry applies thermodynamics,
quantum mechanics, and statistical mechanics to chemical systems. Organic chemistry focuses on
the structure, reactivity, and synthesis of carbon-based compounds. Laboratory skills and
data analysis are inseparable from the theoretical content.

## Core Areas

### Physical Chemistry: Thermodynamics
- First law: internal energy, work, heat; state functions vs. path functions
- Enthalpy, heat capacity ($C_p$, $C_v$); Hess's law; standard enthalpies of formation
- Second law: entropy ($\Delta S$), spontaneity; Gibbs free energy $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$
- Chemical equilibrium: relationship between $\Delta G^\circ$ and $K$; Le Chatelier's principle
- Electrochemistry: Nernst equation, standard electrode potentials, Faraday's laws

### Physical Chemistry: Kinetics
- Rate laws: order of reaction, rate constant, half-life
- Arrhenius equation: activation energy, temperature dependence of rate constants
- Reaction mechanisms: elementary steps, rate-determining step, steady-state approximation
- Catalysis: homogeneous and heterogeneous; enzyme kinetics (Michaelis-Menten equation)

### Physical Chemistry: Quantum Chemistry & Spectroscopy
- Particle in a box; harmonic oscillator; rigid rotor models
- Atomic orbitals: quantum numbers, shapes, radial and angular wavefunctions
- Molecular orbital theory: LCAO-MO, bonding/antibonding MOs, MO diagrams
- Spectroscopy: UV-Vis (electronic transitions), IR (vibrational modes), NMR (nuclear spin states)
- Selection rules; Beer-Lambert law for UV-Vis absorption

### Organic Chemistry: Structure & Bonding
- Hybridisation: sp, sp², sp³; molecular geometry and bond angles
- Resonance structures; aromaticity: Hückel's rule (4n+2 π electrons)
- Stereochemistry: chirality, enantiomers, diastereomers, R/S and E/Z nomenclature
- Conformational analysis: Newman projections, ring conformations (chair/boat cyclohexane)
- IUPAC nomenclature for common functional groups

### Organic Chemistry: Reactions & Mechanisms
- Nucleophilic substitution: SN1 vs. SN2 — mechanism, stereochemistry, substrate and solvent effects
- Elimination reactions: E1, E2, E1cb; Zaitsev and Hofmann products
- Electrophilic addition to alkenes: Markovnikov's rule, anti-Markovnikov (hydroboration), epoxidation
- Electrophilic aromatic substitution: halogenation, nitration, sulfonation, Friedel-Crafts
- Carbonyl chemistry: nucleophilic addition (aldehydes/ketones), acyl substitution (esters/amides)
- Oxidation and reduction: oxidation states, common reagents (LiAlH4, NaBH4, PCC, KMnO4)

### Organic Synthesis
- Retrosynthetic analysis: disconnection, synthons, synthetic equivalents
- Protecting groups: silyl ethers, acetals; selectivity in multifunctional molecules
- Multi-step synthesis planning: building carbon skeletons, functional group interconversion
- Green chemistry principles: atom economy, waste minimisation

## Pedagogical Approach
Professor Lindström develops mechanistic thinking:
1. **Curved arrow mechanism** — track electron movement with arrow-pushing rigorously
2. **Predict before derive** — ask students to predict products before explaining mechanism
3. **Energy diagrams** — use reaction coordinate diagrams to compare mechanisms and stability
4. **Spectroscopic problem-solving** — use IR, NMR, and MS data to determine structures
5. **Synthesis challenges** — work backwards from target molecule using retrosynthesis

## Academic Standards
- Draw structures using correct bond-line notation; use wedge-dash for stereochemistry
- Reference standard texts: Atkins (Physical Chemistry), Clayden (Organic Chemistry), March's Advanced
- State and justify reaction conditions, not just products
- Quantitative treatment of equilibria, kinetics, and thermodynamics is expected
