# Creative & Entertainment Production — Area Context

## Domain Overview

Creative production encompasses the professional workflows that take a creative work from initial concept through to publication, broadcast, or performance. This area serves writers, playwrights, screenwriters, translators, editors, producers, and anyone working on a creative production pipeline.

The distinguishing characteristic of this area is that it focuses on **making** creative work — the actual production pipeline — rather than marketing it. (Marketing and brand strategy for creative works is handled in the Branding & Creative area.)

## The Creative Production Pipeline

Professional creative production follows a consistent pipeline regardless of medium:

1. **Concept & Development** — Logline, treatment, concept development
2. **World-building & Structure** — Establishing the fictional world, story architecture
3. **Drafting** — First draft creation (scripts, chapters, translation)
4. **Editorial Review** — Developmental, line, copy, and sensitivity passes
5. **Testing** — Audience simulation, focus group feedback
6. **Collaboration** — Series management, continuity, multi-author coordination
7. **Pre-Publication** — Submission readiness, market assessment
8. **Distribution** — Market reach, distribution strategy, rights

## Key Professional Concepts

### Screenwriting Standards
- **Feature screenplay**: typically 90-120 pages; one page ≈ one minute of screen time
- **Formatting**: Standard screenwriting format (Final Draft conventions) — SCENE HEADINGS, action lines, character names centred above dialogue, parentheticals
- **Beat sheet**: Scene-level structural planning (Save the Cat: 15 beats; sequence approach: 8 sequences)
- **Three-act structure**: Setup (25%), Confrontation (50%), Resolution (25%)
- **Stage play format**: Samuel French formatting conventions; stage directions serve a different function than film action lines

### Dramatic Structure
- **Inciting incident**: The event that sets the story in motion (typically pp. 12-17 in a feature)
- **Midpoint**: Major shift or revelation at the structural centre
- **Dark night of the soul**: Protagonist at their lowest, before the final act
- **Character arc**: Transformation or revelation arc (what the character wants vs. needs)
- **Subtext**: What characters mean vs. what they say — the iceberg principle

### Literary Translation Principles
- **Dynamic equivalence** (Nida): Translate meaning and effect, not words
- **Foreignisation vs. domestication** (Venuti): Keep the work's cultural foreignness vs. naturalise for target audience
- **Register**: Formal/informal distinction; a character's register reveals class, education, relationship
- **Idiomatic equivalence**: A Swedish idiom requires an English equivalent that achieves the same effect, not a literal translation
- **Prosody**: In poetry and song lyrics, rhythm and sound patterns must be preserved or consciously reimagined

### Editorial Passes (Publishing Industry Standard)
- **Developmental edit**: Big picture — structure, pacing, character arcs, plot logic, thematic coherence
- **Line edit**: Sentence level — voice, rhythm, clarity, word choice, style
- **Copy edit**: Mechanical — grammar, spelling, punctuation, consistency, fact-checking
- **Proofread**: Final polish — typos, formatting, layout errors before print/publication
- **Sensitivity read**: Specialised review of representation, cultural accuracy, potential harms

### World-Building Principles (Sanderson's Laws)
- **First Law**: An author's ability to solve conflict with magic is directly proportional to how well the reader understands the magic system
- **Second Law**: Limitations are more interesting than powers
- **Third Law**: Expand what you have before adding new elements
- **Consistency**: Internal logic must be maintained; readers notice violations even if they can't name them

### Publishing & Submission Conventions
- **Query letter**: One-page letter to an agent; hook, 250-word synopsis, bio
- **Synopsis**: 1-5 page overview of the entire story including the ending
- **Partial/full**: Agent asks for first 50 pages (partial) or complete manuscript (full)
- **Comp titles**: 2-3 recently published comparable works; signals market positioning
- **Word counts by genre**: Literary fiction 80-100K; YA 60-90K; Thriller 75-90K; Fantasy 90-120K; Screenplay 90-120 pages

## Quality Standards for This Area

Creative production requires a different quality standard than analytical domains. Outputs should:
- Demonstrate genuine **craft knowledge**, not generic advice
- Reference specific techniques, schools of thought, and industry conventions
- Balance **artistic integrity** with **commercial practicality**
- Never produce generic, formulaic creative content — specificity is everything
- Understand that creative decisions have **reasons** — even unconventional choices are valid when purposeful
