## FOR ZAKAT (ISLAM)

### What Zakat Is
Zakat is the third pillar of Islam. It means giving 2.5% of qualifying wealth that has been held for one full lunar year (this year-long waiting period is called "hawl"). It is an obligation — not a donation — for Muslims who have wealth above the minimum threshold.

### The Nisab — the Minimum Threshold
Nisab is the minimum amount of wealth a person must have before zakat is required. There are two ways to calculate it:
- Gold standard: equivalent in value to 87.5 grams of gold
- Silver standard: equivalent in value to 612.36 grams of silver

The silver standard typically results in a lower threshold, meaning more people are eligible to pay. Many scholars recommend using the silver standard for the purpose of giving more to those in need. Check the current gold or silver price in your local currency to calculate today's nisab.

Example: "If gold is 8,000 Kenyan shillings per gram, the gold nisab is 87.5 x 8,000 = 700,000 shillings. If your qualifying wealth held for one year exceeds 700,000 shillings, you owe zakat."

### What Counts Towards Zakat (Zakatable Assets)
- Cash savings (held for one full lunar year)
- Business goods and inventory (at trade value)
- Gold and silver (all forms — jewellery, coins, bars)
- Livestock (cattle, sheep, camels — specific rules apply to quantity)
- Agricultural produce (paid at harvest, not after one year — different rules)
- Investments and shares (the zakatable portion — consult a scholar)

### What Does NOT Count
- Your primary home (where you live)
- Household furniture and daily-use items
- Tools and equipment you use to earn your living
- Debts you owe (deducted from zakatable assets)

### How to Calculate — Simple Method
1. Add up all your cash savings + value of gold/silver + business inventory + investments
2. Subtract any debts you owe that are due within the year
3. If the remaining amount exceeds nisab and has been held for one lunar year, pay 2.5% of the total

### Zakat al-Fitr — End of Ramadan
This is a separate, smaller zakat paid before Eid al-Fitr prayer. It is a fixed amount per person in the household (including children and elderly dependents). The amount is typically the equivalent of one meal or a set amount defined by local scholars or Zakat authorities. It is paid in food or its cash equivalent.

### Where to Pay
Pay to the eight categories of recipients defined in the Quran (the poor, the needy, those in debt, travellers in need, etc.). You may pay through:
- Official Zakat authorities: LHDN Malaysia, National Zakat Foundation (UK), Amil Zakat (Indonesia), Zakat Foundation of America
- Registered Islamic charities in your country
- Directly to individuals you know to be in need

### Complex Cases — Consult a Scholar
Business owners, those with complex investments, or those uncertain about their hawl (one lunar year) should consult a qualified Islamic scholar or their country's Zakat authority. Do not guess on obligatory worship.

## FOR CHRISTIAN TITHING

Tithing traditionally means giving 10% of income or increase to the church or to God's work. This is practised differently across denominations:
- Some churches calculate on gross income (before tax)
- Some calculate on net income (after tax)
- Some accept that tithing applies to surplus, not survival income

Your denomination's teaching on tithing is the right guide. If you are unsure, speak to your pastor or priest.

General principle: tithe should be given to your local church, which then distributes to those in need and to ministry work. Giving to registered charities may also fulfil this in many traditions.
