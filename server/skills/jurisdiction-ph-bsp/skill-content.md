# Philippines — Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP)

## Regulatory Authority

The Philippines has a multi-agency financial regulatory structure:

- **Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP)** — the central bank, responsible for monetary policy, exchange rate management, and the supervision of all banks, quasi-banks, trust entities, electronic money issuers, virtual asset service providers (VASPs), money service businesses, and remittance agents. BSP is also responsible for advancing financial inclusion under its National Strategy for Financial Inclusion.
- **Securities and Exchange Commission Philippines (SEC Philippines)** — distinct from the US SEC; regulates corporations, securities dealers, investment houses, financing companies, and lending companies. Issues AML/CFT rules for SEC-supervised entities.
- **Insurance Commission (IC)** — regulates insurance companies, mutual benefit associations, and pre-need companies.
- **Anti-Money Laundering Council (AMLC)** — a uniquely powerful body in the Philippine regulatory architecture. AMLC is not merely an FIU but also has investigative and prosecutorial powers. AMLC is composed of the BSP Governor (Chairman), SEC Chairperson, and Insurance Commission Commissioner. Its Secretariat acts as the operational arm. AMLC can file AML cases in court, apply for asset freezing orders, and enter into international mutual legal assistance agreements.

## Key Regulations

- **Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA), Republic Act 9160**, as amended by RA 9194, RA 10167, RA 10365, and most significantly **RA 11521 (2021)** — RA 11521 was a landmark amendment that expanded the list of covered persons and covered transactions, added real estate developers and brokers, dealers in precious metals and stones, offshore gaming operators, and enhanced AMLC's enforcement powers. The 2021 amendment was part of the Philippines' successful exit from the FATF Grey List.
- **BSP Circular on Electronic Money (BSP Circular 649 and subsequent)** — governs e-money issuers including GCash, Maya, and other electronic wallets.
- **BSP Digital Bank Licensing Framework (BSP Circular 1105, 2020)** — establishes a separate digital bank category with specific prudential requirements and capital thresholds.
- **BSP Circular on Virtual Asset Service Providers (BSP Circular 1108, 2021)** — governs crypto exchanges and virtual asset service providers operating in the Philippines, requiring BSP registration and AML/CFT compliance.
- **AMLC Regulatory Issuances** — AMLC regularly issues implementing rules and guidelines on covered transactions, suspicious transaction reporting, customer due diligence, and targeted financial sanctions.

## AML/CFT Framework (Critical: FATF Grey List History)

The Philippines' AML/CFT framework has undergone substantial reform following FATF scrutiny:

- **AMLC (Anti-Money Laundering Council)** serves simultaneously as the FIU, AML/CFT policy body, and enforcement/prosecution authority — a uniquely integrated structure. AMLC can independently file money laundering cases in court without referral to the Department of Justice, apply for court orders to freeze accounts, and request mutual legal assistance from foreign jurisdictions.
- **CTR (Covered Transaction Report):** Cash and monetary instrument transactions equal to or exceeding **PHP 500,000** (approximately USD 8,800) in a single banking day must be reported to AMLC. This is called a "Covered Transaction Report" in Philippine terminology.
- **STR (Suspicious Transaction Report):** Any transaction, regardless of amount, that gives rise to suspicion of ML/TF must be reported to AMLC within five working days of determination of suspicion.
- **FATF Grey List (2000–2005, 2021):** The Philippines was on the FATF Grey List twice. The most recent grey listing (June 2021) followed deficiencies in real estate AML, casino supervision, and prosecution effectiveness. The Philippines successfully completed its FATF action plan and was removed from the Grey List in June 2021 — one of the fastest grey list exits on record, achieved through RA 11521 and extensive enforcement actions. International banks may still apply enhanced correspondent banking scrutiny to Philippine-originating transactions.
- **Covered Persons:** Following RA 11521, covered persons now include: all banks and quasi-banks, offshore banking units, insurance companies, securities dealers, money service businesses, remittance agents, pawnshops, casino operators, real estate developers and brokers, dealers in precious metals, precious stones and jewelry, and designated offshore gaming operators (DOGOs/POGOs).

## OFW Remittances (Critical Section)

The Philippines is one of the world's largest remittance-receiving countries:

- **Volume:** The Philippines receives over **USD 37 billion** annually in personal remittances (2023 data), representing approximately 8–9% of GDP. This makes remittances the single largest source of foreign exchange for the country.
- **Major Corridors:** United States (largest), Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Singapore, Hong Kong, Japan, and Italy. Significant OFW populations in the Middle East, East Asia, and Europe.
- **Remittance Channels:** Traditional money transfer operators (Western Union, MoneyGram), bank-to-bank transfers, Philippine bank foreign branches and tie-ups, informal channels (Padala), and increasingly digital wallets (GCash GFunds international transfer, Maya).
- **AML Implications:** The scale of remittance flows makes the Philippines a significant node in international AML/CFT monitoring. Trade-based money laundering through import/export declaration manipulation and structuring of remittances are documented typologies. BSP and AMLC have issued specific guidance for money service businesses handling OFW remittances.

## Digital Finance

The Philippines has a rapidly growing digital financial sector:

- **GCash (Mynt, jointly owned by Globe Telecom and Ant Group)** — the dominant mobile wallet with over 76 million registered users (2023), exceeding the adult population of many countries. GCash processes billions of pesos daily in peer-to-peer transfers, bill payments, insurance, and investments.
- **Maya (formerly PayMaya, owned by PLDT/Smart)** — the major competitor to GCash, also operating Maya Bank (digital bank licence).
- **InstaPay** — BSP-operated real-time payment system for retail transactions below PHP 50,000, operating 24/7. Key interoperability infrastructure.
- **PESONet** — batch electronic fund transfer system for higher-value payments, settling three times daily.
- **Digital Bank Licences (BSP Circular 1105):** Six digital bank licences were awarded: Tonik Bank (first digital bank, Singapore-backed), GoTyme Bank (Gokongwei-Tyme Group), Maya Bank (PLDT), OwnBank (Robinson's Retail), Overseas Filipino Bank (Land Bank subsidiary for OFWs), and UNO Digital Bank (Fintech consortium).

## Key Terminology

- **OFW (Overseas Filipino Worker)** — the term for Filipino migrant workers. OFW remittances are a national economic cornerstone. "OFW banking" is a distinct segment with tailored products from Philippine banks, including foreign branch presence in key OFW destinations.
- **Padala** — the Filipino word for "send" or "remittance." "Padala centres" are informal and formal remittance agents widely used in both origin and destination countries.
- **Barangay** — the smallest administrative unit in the Philippines (roughly equivalent to a village or urban ward). Relevant for BSP financial inclusion mapping — access to formal financial services at the barangay level is a key policy metric. BSP publishes financial inclusion maps by barangay.
- **PhilSys (Philippine Identification System)** — the national digital ID system launched in 2021 under Republic Act 11055. PhilSys Number (PSN) and PhilSys Card are being introduced as the primary identity document for all Filipino citizens and resident aliens. BSP has recognised PhilSys as valid KYC documentation. Adoption has been slower than planned but coverage is expanding rapidly.
- **BancNet** — the Philippine interbank ATM and payment network, connecting all ATMs and POS terminals nationally.
- **PHP** — Philippine Peso, the national currency. AML thresholds are set in PHP; compliance teams should monitor exchange rates for operational planning.
