# Singapore — Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS)

## Regulatory Authority

Singapore employs a distinctive unified regulatory model:

- **Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS)** — functions simultaneously as the central bank, monetary authority, banking supervisor, capital markets regulator, and financial conduct authority. This integrated structure is relatively rare globally and enables coordinated cross-sectoral supervision. MAS has a well-deserved reputation for rigorous, risk-based supervision and substantive enforcement.
- **Commercial Affairs Department (CAD)** — the financial crime investigation branch of the Singapore Police Force. CAD investigates complex commercial crime including money laundering, corruption, and market manipulation. CAD operates the Suspicious Transaction Reporting Office (STRO), which receives STRs from all reporting entities.
- **Accounting and Corporate Regulatory Authority (ACRA)** — registers companies and maintains beneficial ownership information. Relevant for AML/CFT because corporate vehicles are a key ML risk.

MAS and CAD collaborate closely on financial crime enforcement. MAS focuses on regulatory action against financial institutions (fines, licence revocations); CAD/STRO handles criminal investigation and prosecution.

## Key Regulations

- **Corruption, Drug Trafficking and Other Serious Crimes (Confiscation of Benefits) Act (CDSA)** — Singapore's primary AML statute. ML offences are defined with reference to a broad list of "serious offences" including tax evasion, corruption, fraud, and criminal breach of trust. Notably, Singapore has no de minimis threshold for ML prosecution — the proceeds can be of any value.
- **MAS Notices on Prevention of Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism** — sector-specific binding notices issued by MAS to each regulated entity class: Notice 626 (banks), Notice SFA 04-N02 (capital markets services licensees), Notice 314 (insurers), Notice PSN01 and PSN02 (payment service licensees), among others. These notices set out detailed CDD, record-keeping, and reporting obligations.
- **Payment Services Act 2019 (PS Act)** — landmark legislation that modernised Singapore's payment regulation. The PS Act covers: account issuance services, domestic/cross-border money transfers, merchant acquisition, e-money issuance, digital payment token (crypto) services, and money-changing. Introduced a tiered licensing regime: money-changing licence, standard payment institution licence, and major payment institution licence.
- **Securities and Futures Act (SFA)** — governs capital markets, including securities dealing, fund management, and trading in futures and derivative contracts.
- **Insurance Act** — governs insurance companies, brokers, and intermediaries, with MAS as the prudential and conduct supervisor.

## AML/CFT Framework

Singapore's AML/CFT framework is comprehensive, frequently updated, and vigorously enforced:

- **STRO (Suspicious Transaction Reporting Office)** — within the CAD. All persons (not just financial institutions) must file STRs when they have knowledge or suspicion of ML/TF. Singapore's STR obligations extend to any person, making it broader than many jurisdictions' reporting requirements.
- **CTR Threshold:** Singapore does not have a mandatory currency transaction reporting threshold equivalent to the US FinCEN CTR. However, MAS Notices require financial institutions to apply enhanced scrutiny to large cash transactions.
- **FATF Membership:** Singapore is a full FATF member. Singapore regularly receives strong mutual evaluation results, particularly for its technical compliance and effectiveness in financial crime investigation and prosecution.

**Enforcement Track Record:** MAS has established a global reputation for significant financial penalties against major banks for AML/CFT failures. Landmark cases include:
- **BSI Bank (2016)** — first bank in Singapore to have its merchant bank licence revoked in 32 years; fined SGD 13.3 million for 41 separate breaches related to 1MDB transactions.
- **UBS AG (2017)** — fined SGD 15 million for AML control failures related to 1MDB.
- **Goldman Sachs (2020)** — fined SGD 122 million for 1MDB-related AML failures, the largest AML penalty in Singapore history at the time.
- **DBS, OCBC, Citibank, UOB (2023)** — collectively fined for AML control failures related to a major scam syndicate money laundering network uncovered in 2023 (the largest ML bust in Singapore's history, involving assets worth over SGD 2.8 billion seized).

These enforcement actions have profoundly shaped Singapore banks' AML investment posture.

## Digital Finance and Fintech

Singapore is Asia's leading fintech hub with sophisticated digital financial regulation:

- **MAS Regulatory Sandbox** — widely recognised as a global benchmark for financial innovation sandboxes. Enables fintech firms to test solutions with actual customers under time-limited regulatory relief, with streamlined approval timelines.
- **Digital Full Bank Licences** — MAS awarded two Digital Full Bank licences and three Digital Wholesale Bank licences in 2020. Recipients include GrabFin/Singtel (GXS Bank), Sea Group (Maribank), and Ant Group (ANEXT Bank).
- **PayNow** — Singapore's national real-time payment system, operated by the Association of Banks in Singapore. Enables instant fund transfers using NRIC, mobile number, or UEN (Unique Entity Number for businesses). PayNow is linked to Thailand's PromptPay and India's UPI for cross-border instant payment.
- **FAST (Fast And Secure Transfers)** — the underlying real-time gross settlement payment rail for bank-to-bank transfers.
- **SGFinDex** — Singapore's open banking data sharing initiative, enabling individuals to consolidate financial data from multiple banks and government agencies (CPF, HDB) through a single secure platform.
- **Digital Payment Tokens (DPTs)** — MAS's regulatory term for cryptocurrencies and digital assets. Payment Service licensees for DPT services must comply with MAS anti-money laundering requirements for crypto, which are among the strictest globally, including Travel Rule implementation.

## Key Terminology

- **Prescribed Financial Institution (PFI)** — the MAS term for all entities subject to MAS AML/CFT notices: banks, merchant banks, finance companies, insurers, capital markets services licensees, and payment service licensees.
- **Designated Payment Token (DPT)** — Singapore's regulatory classification for cryptocurrencies and other digital payment tokens. DPT service providers require a Major Payment Institution licence under the PS Act.
- **1MDB Scandal** — the 1Malaysia Development Berhad sovereign wealth fund fraud involved the misappropriation of approximately USD 4.5 billion. Singapore was a key node in the global ML network. The scandal fundamentally reshaped Singapore's AML/CFT enforcement approach and caused MAS to significantly upgrade its supervisory intensity.
- **Travel Rule** — MAS requires payment service licensees to collect and transmit originator and beneficiary information for virtual asset transfers above SGD 1,500, aligned with FATF Recommendation 16.
- **CAMS/ACAMS** — Certified Anti-Money Laundering Specialist qualification; widely required for AML compliance staff at Singapore financial institutions.
- **UEN (Unique Entity Number)** — Singapore's business identification number, issued by ACRA to all registered companies and businesses. Used for corporate KYC, payment identification, and government services.
