# Malaysia — Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM)

## Regulatory Authority

Malaysia's financial regulatory architecture is sophisticated and globally respected, particularly in Islamic finance:

- **Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM)** — the central bank, responsible for monetary policy, financial stability, and the supervision of licensed banks, insurance companies, development financial institutions, money service businesses, and payment system operators. BNM is widely regarded as one of the world's most capable central banks, particularly for its integrated approach to Islamic finance regulation.
- **Securities Commission Malaysia (SC)** — regulates capital markets, fund management, unit trust schemes, stockbrokers, and the Islamic capital market (sukuk issuance, Islamic REITs, Islamic funds). SC and BNM have a memorandum of understanding for coordination on cross-sectoral issues.
- **Labuan Financial Services Authority (Labuan FSA)** — regulates offshore financial institutions and structures domiciled in Labuan (Malaysia's international business and financial centre), including Labuan banks, Labuan Islamic banks, and Labuan insurance.

## Key Regulations

- **Anti-Money Laundering, Anti-Terrorism Financing and Proceeds of Unlawful Activities Act 2001 (AMLA)** — Malaysia's primary AML/CFT statute. AMLA covers all reporting institutions including banks, insurance companies, securities firms, money changers, remittance businesses, lawyers, accountants, real estate agents, casinos, and dealers in precious metals and stones. AMLA has been amended several times; the most recent substantial amendments strengthened beneficial ownership requirements and the National Coordination Committee to Counter Money Laundering (NCC).
- **Financial Services Act 2013 (FSA)** — the primary prudential and conduct statute for conventional banks, insurance companies, and investment banks. Replaces the earlier Banking and Financial Institutions Act 1989 (BAFIA).
- **Islamic Financial Services Act 2013 (IFSA)** — the dedicated statute for Islamic banks, takaful operators, Islamic investment banks, and Islamic financial holding companies. A landmark piece of legislation globally, IFSA explicitly anchors the legal treatment of Islamic finance products in Sharia contracts, making it distinct from mere "conventional finance with Islamic labelling."
- **Development Financial Institutions Act 2002 (DFIA)** — governs specialised development financial institutions (DFIs) such as Bank Simpanan Nasional, EXIM Bank, Agro Bank, and SME Bank.
- **BNM e-KYC Policy Document (2020)** — provides a comprehensive framework for electronic Know Your Customer processes, including facial recognition, document verification, and liveness detection. BNM's e-KYC framework is one of the most mature in ASEAN and is widely referenced by other regulators.
- **Digital Bank Licensing Framework (2020)** — BNM's framework for licensing digital banks in Malaysia. Five licences were awarded in 2022 to GXS Bank (Grab-Singtel), Boost Bank (RHB-Boost), AEON Bank (AEON Financial-MoneyLion), KAF Digital Bank, and YTL Digital Capital.

## Islamic Finance (Critical Section)

Malaysia is the global reference jurisdiction for Islamic finance regulation. Several unique features define the Malaysian framework:

**Sharia Advisory Council (SAC) of BNM:** The SAC is the highest Sharia authority in Malaysia for Islamic financial business. Its rulings are binding on all Islamic financial institutions, courts, and arbitration bodies when hearing Islamic finance cases. This centralised Sharia governance structure eliminates the fragmentation seen in other jurisdictions where each institution has its own Sharia board with potentially conflicting rulings. Individual institutions maintain their own internal Sharia Committees, but these must not contradict the SAC.

**Islamic Banking Market Share:** As of 2024, Islamic banking assets account for approximately 43% of total banking system assets in Malaysia — among the highest proportions globally. The government has set targets for continued expansion, including increasing takaful (Islamic insurance) market share.

**Islamic Capital Market:** Malaysia consistently ranks as the world's largest sukuk market by outstanding issuance volume. Key features:
- Bursa Malaysia Suq Al-Sila — a commodity Murabaha trading platform providing Sharia-compliant liquidity management instruments, widely used by Islamic banks across the region.
- SC-regulated Islamic REITs, Islamic ETFs, and Islamic private equity.
- Malaysia's sukuk structuring conventions (particularly for sovereign sukuk) differ in some respects from GCC/AAOIFI standards — Malaysian structures are generally more pragmatic about IFRS integration.

**Malaysia vs. AAOIFI Standards:** Unlike GCC countries that mandate AAOIFI standards, Malaysia uses its own Sharia rulings (issued by the SAC) and accounting standards based on MFRS (Malaysian Financial Reporting Standards, which are IFRS-aligned). The SAC has occasionally reached different conclusions from AAOIFI on product permissibility. Compliance professionals working across GCC and Malaysia must be alert to these divergences.

**Key Islamic Finance Products:** Diminishing Musharakah (home financing — dominant product), Commodity Murabaha (working capital, interbank liquidity), Ijarah Thumma Al-Bai (hire-purchase, formerly dominant for vehicles), Bai Bithaman Ajil (deferred payment sale, being phased out), Wakalah (agency contracts, widely used in takaful), Mudarabah (profit-sharing investment accounts).

## AML/CFT Framework

- **Financial Intelligence and Enforcement Department (FIED)** — the division within BNM responsible for AML/CFT supervision, financial intelligence, and enforcement. FIED acts as Malaysia's FIU function within the central bank structure.
- **Joint Financial Intelligence Unit (JFIU)** — a collaboration between BNM and the Royal Malaysia Police (PDRM) for receiving, analysing, and disseminating STRs and CTRs. The JFIU portal (goAML) is used for all suspicious transaction reporting.
- **STR Threshold:** Any suspicion — no minimum amount. Reporting institutions must file whenever there are reasonable grounds to suspect ML/TF.
- **CTR Threshold:** Cash transactions exceeding **MYR 50,000** (approximately USD 11,000) must be reported to JFIU.
- **BNM AML/CFT and Targeted Financial Sanctions (TFS) Policy Document** — the comprehensive regulatory document setting out all AML/CFT obligations for reporting institutions; periodically updated.
- **FATF Membership:** Malaysia is an FATF member via the Asia/Pacific Group on Money Laundering (APG). Malaysia underwent a mutual evaluation in 2014–2015; an updated assessment reflects significant improvements. Compliance professionals should reference the most current APG/FATF mutual evaluation report.

## Digital Finance and Fintech

Malaysia has a mature and fast-evolving digital financial ecosystem:

- **DuitNow** — BNM's national instant payment and QR payment system, enabling real-time transfers using mobile number, NRIC number, or QR code. DuitNow has achieved widespread merchant adoption and is central to Malaysia's cashless payment strategy.
- **Touch 'n Go eWallet** — Malaysia's largest e-money operator with over 18 million users, operated by TNG Digital (Alliance Bank-Ant Group joint venture). Widely used for transport, retail, and bill payments.
- **PayNet (Payments Network Malaysia)** — national payment system operator owned by BNM and the banking sector, operating DuitNow, FPX (online banking), MyDebit (national debit scheme), and JomPAY.
- **BNM Regulatory Sandbox** — one of ASEAN's most established regulatory sandboxes, enabling fintech firms to test innovative solutions under a time-limited regulatory exemption.
- **Open API Framework** — BNM's phased open banking framework requiring banks to publish APIs for third-party payment initiation and account information services.

## Key Terminology

- **SAC (Sharia Advisory Council)** — BNM's supreme Sharia authority. SAC rulings are legally binding. Always check SAC resolutions when advising on Islamic finance compliance in Malaysia.
- **Ar-Rahnu** — Islamic pawnbroking. Customers pledge gold in exchange for a Qard (benevolent loan); the institution charges a fee for safekeeping the collateral (ujrah), not interest. Widely offered by Islamic banks and Agrobank.
- **Hibah** — gift. In Islamic banking deposits, banks may voluntarily give customers a "hibah" rather than paying guaranteed interest, aligning with Sharia prohibition on riba. The gift rate is typically disclosed but not contractually guaranteed.
- **Mudarabah** — profit-sharing. Islamic investment accounts are structured as Mudarabah contracts where the bank is the entrepreneur (mudarib) and the depositor provides capital (rabb al-mal). Profits are shared; losses borne by depositor (except in cases of bank negligence).
- **Principal Dealer** — a bank licensed by BNM and SC to act as a primary dealer in Malaysian Government Securities (MGS) and Government Investment Issues (GII — the Islamic equivalent). Principal dealers are the backbone of both conventional and Islamic sovereign debt markets.
- **NRIC (National Registration Identity Card)** — the primary KYC document for Malaysian citizens. Foreign nationals use their passport. BNM's e-KYC framework enables remote identity verification using NRIC + MyKad chip data.
