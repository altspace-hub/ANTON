# Pakistan — State Bank of Pakistan (SBP)

## Regulatory Authority

Pakistan's financial sector is overseen by three principal regulators:

- **State Bank of Pakistan (SBP)** — the central bank, responsible for monetary policy, foreign exchange management, and supervision of all banks, development finance institutions, microfinance banks, and payment service providers. SBP issues binding prudential regulations and AML/CFT directions.
- **Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan (SECP)** — regulates capital markets, insurance companies, non-bank financial companies (NBFCs), leasing companies, and modarabas (Islamic investment vehicles). SECP enforces AML/CFT for its regulated entities under separate SECP AML/CFT Regulations.
- **Federal Insurance Ombudsman / Insurance Division** — insurance sector oversight sits within SECP's remit; a dedicated Insurance Act 1938 (and Companies Act 2017) governs the sector.

Pakistan also has the **Financial Monitoring Unit (FMU)**, established under the AML Act, which serves as the country's Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU). The FMU receives, analyses, and disseminates Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs) and Currency Transaction Reports (CTRs), and cooperates with foreign FIUs through the Egmont Group.

## Key Regulations

- **Anti-Money Laundering Act 2010 (AMLA), as amended 2020** — primary AML/CFT statute covering banks, exchange companies, securities brokers, DNFBPs, and designated non-financial businesses. The 2020 amendment significantly expanded coverage and strengthened enforcement powers in response to FATF requirements.
- **SBP AML/CFT Regulations for Banks and DFIs** — mandatory risk-based customer due diligence, beneficial ownership identification, enhanced due diligence for PEPs, correspondent banking controls, and STR filing obligations.
- **Islamic Banking Policy (IBP)** — SBP framework for full-fledged Islamic banks and Islamic Banking Windows (IBWs) operated by conventional banks. Covers Sharia governance, product approval, and profit-sharing investment account (PSIA) treatment.
- **Branchless Banking Regulations (BBR)** — governs mobile and agent banking. Defines Level 0, 1, 2, and 3 account tiers with progressively relaxed CDD requirements to promote financial inclusion. JazzCash and Easypaisa operate under BBR.
- **Microfinance Institutions Ordinance 2001 (MFO)** — establishes licensing requirements for microfinance banks (MFBs) supervised by SBP.
- **Zakat and Ushr Ordinance 1980** — mandates automatic deduction of Zakat (2.5%) from savings and investment accounts on the 1st day of Ramadan each year. Non-Muslim account holders may file a declaration of exemption (Sahib-e-Nisab). This has direct operational implications for banks managing customer notifications, exemption processing, and reconciliation.

## AML/CFT Framework

The **Financial Monitoring Unit (FMU)** is Pakistan's FIU, operating under the Ministry of Finance. Key operational thresholds and obligations:

- **STR (Suspicious Transaction Report):** Mandatory for all covered entities upon suspicion of money laundering or terrorist financing. No threshold — suspicion alone triggers the obligation.
- **CTR (Currency Transaction Report):** Required for cash transactions equal to or exceeding **PKR 2.5 million** (approximately USD 8,800 at current rates) in a single transaction or series of linked transactions.
- **Risk-based CDD:** Institutions must apply simplified, standard, or enhanced due diligence based on a documented customer and product risk assessment. High-risk categories include PEPs, non-resident customers, money service businesses, and customers from FATF high-risk jurisdictions.

**FATF History:** Pakistan was placed on the FATF Grey List in June 2018 following deficiencies in its AML/CFT regime, particularly around terrorist financing prosecutions, designated non-financial businesses, and real estate controls. Pakistan completed its FATF Action Plan and was removed from the Grey List in October 2022. Compliance professionals should be aware that enhanced scrutiny from correspondent banks regarding Pakistan-related transactions remains common in international practice even post-delisting.

## Islamic Banking

Pakistan has one of the world's largest Islamic banking sectors by proportion. As of 2024, Islamic banking assets represent approximately **20% of total banking system assets**, with continued strong growth:

- **Full-fledged Islamic banks:** Meezan Bank (market leader), BankIslami, Dubai Islamic Bank Pakistan, Al Baraka Bank, Bank Al Falah (Islamic subsidiary).
- **Islamic Banking Windows (IBWs):** Most major conventional banks operate IBWs. SBP has set targets for conversion to full Islamic banking.
- **SBP Sharia Board:** A central bank-level Sharia board provides authoritative rulings applicable to all Islamic banks. Individual institutions maintain their own Sharia Supervisory Committees.
- **Products:** Murabaha (commodity finance), Diminishing Musharakah (home finance), Ijarah (leasing), Musharakah (equity participation), and Bai' Salam (agricultural advance purchase).
- **Sukuk Market:** Government of Pakistan issues sovereign sukuk (Ijara sukuk backed by motorway/infrastructure assets) regularly. Corporate sukuk market is growing. The State Bank itself issues short-term Sharia-compliant instruments (GoP Ijara Sukuk) for Islamic bank liquidity management.

## Digital Finance and Fintech

Pakistan's digital finance landscape has expanded rapidly:

- **JazzCash** (Mobilink Microfinance Bank) and **Easypaisa** (Telenor Microfinance Bank) dominate the mobile money market with tens of millions of active wallets.
- **Raast** — Pakistan's national instant payment system launched by SBP in 2021, described as Pakistan's equivalent of India's UPI. Person-to-person (P2P), person-to-merchant (P2M), and bulk payment functionalities rolled out in phases.
- **Digital Bank Licensing Framework (2022)** — SBP issued guidelines for the licensing of digital banks. Five initial licences awarded to entities including EasyPaisa Digital Bank, Hugo Bank, KayMaal, Mashreq (Pakistani subsidiary), and Raqami.
- **1LINK** — the national ATM switching and interbank payment network, critical infrastructure linking all bank ATMs and POS terminals.
- **Open Banking** — SBP has issued a framework for Open Banking APIs, supporting third-party payment initiation and account information services.

## Key Terminology

- **CNIC (Computerised National Identity Card)** — the primary KYC document for Pakistani citizens, issued by NADRA (National Database and Registration Authority). Account opening and financial transactions are tied to CNIC verification via the NADRA Verisys system.
- **NADRA Verisys** — real-time biometric and CNIC verification system used by banks for remote KYC.
- **Ehsaas / Benazir Income Support Programme (BISP)** — government social protection programmes delivering digital payments to millions of beneficiaries, often the first formal financial account for low-income women.
- **Kamyab Pakistan / Rozgaar Scheme** — government-backed subsidised financing programmes channelled through commercial and microfinance banks; relevant for understanding directed lending and associated AML considerations.
- **Ameen** — SBP's branding for Islamic banking window operations (also used informally); not a universal term.
- **Foreign Exchange Regulation Act (FERA) / Foreign Exchange Manual** — governs cross-border payments and remittances, relevant for trade-based AML and hawala/hundi monitoring.
