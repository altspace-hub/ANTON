# Hong Kong — Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA)

## Regulatory Authority

Hong Kong operates a sectoral regulatory model with three principal financial regulators:

- **Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA)** — established in 1993, the HKMA serves as Hong Kong's de facto central bank and banking regulator. The HKMA is responsible for monetary policy (maintaining the Linked Exchange Rate System pegging HKD to USD), managing the Exchange Fund, supervising all Authorised Institutions (AIs), and overseeing the payment and settlement system. HKMA is not technically a central bank by statute — it is an administrative unit of the government — but functions as one in all material respects.
- **Securities and Futures Commission (SFC)** — regulates securities markets, fund managers, stockbrokers, and, since 2023, Virtual Asset Trading Platforms (VATPs). SFC and HKMA share oversight of banks that conduct securities and investment activities.
- **Insurance Authority (IA)** — regulates insurance companies, insurance agents, and insurance brokers. The IA assumed direct regulation of the insurance sector from the insurance industry self-regulatory body in 2019.
- **Joint Financial Intelligence Unit (JFIU)** — operated jointly by the Hong Kong Police Force and the Customs and Excise Department, the JFIU is Hong Kong's FIU. All STRs are filed with the JFIU.

## Key Regulations

- **Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (AMLO, Cap. 615)** — Hong Kong's comprehensive AML/CFT statute. AMLO imposes customer due diligence (CDD), record-keeping, and suspicious transaction reporting obligations on: Authorised Institutions (banks), licensed corporations (securities firms), insurance companies, money service operators, trust or company service providers, and (from 2023) real estate agents and dealers in precious metals and stones.
- **Banking Ordinance (Cap. 155)** — the foundational statute governing the authorisation and supervision of all banking institutions in Hong Kong. Banks are classified as: Licensed Banks, Restricted Licence Banks, and Deposit-Taking Companies.
- **Securities and Futures Ordinance (SFO, Cap. 571)** — governs capital markets, including all regulated activities such as securities dealing, advising on securities, asset management, and clearing.
- **Insurance Ordinance (Cap. 41)** — governs insurance companies and intermediaries.
- **HKMA Supervisory Policy Manual (SPM)** — a comprehensive suite of guidance documents issued by HKMA covering all aspects of banking supervision. The AML/CFT-related SPM modules (particularly AML-1: Guideline on Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Financing of Terrorism) provide the most detailed operational guidance for banks and are regularly updated.

## AML/CFT Framework

- **JFIU (Joint Financial Intelligence Unit)** — Hong Kong's FIU, operated jointly by the Hong Kong Police Force and the Customs and Excise Department. All covered entities must file STRs with JFIU. JFIU also disseminates typology reports and AML/CFT guidance.
- **STR Threshold:** No minimum amount. Any knowledge or suspicion of ML/TF triggers an STR obligation. Drug trafficking proceeds are covered by the Drug Trafficking (Recovery of Proceeds) Ordinance; other serious crimes by the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance.
- **Police Financial Investigation Division** — within the Organised Crime and Triad Bureau of the Hong Kong Police Force; investigates complex ML cases.
- **FATF Membership:** Hong Kong is a full FATF member. Hong Kong's most recent mutual evaluation was conducted in 2023–2024.
- **Enforcement:** HKMA conducts regular thematic AML/CFT reviews of the banking sector and has issued publicly disclosed remediation requirements to multiple banks. Major international banks with significant Hong Kong operations have received supervisory actions. HKMA's approach is risk-based and increasingly uses data analytics for supervisory monitoring.
- **Common Reporting Standard (CRS)** — Hong Kong implemented CRS (the OECD's automatic exchange of financial account information standard) fully. Financial institutions must identify tax-resident account holders from CRS-participating jurisdictions and report annually to the Inland Revenue Department.

## Virtual Asset Regulation

Hong Kong has moved aggressively to regulate and attract virtual asset business:

- **SFC Virtual Asset Trading Platform (VATP) Licensing (2023)** — the SFC introduced mandatory licensing for centralised virtual asset trading platforms operating in or marketing to Hong Kong investors. VATPs must hold a Type 1 (securities dealing) and Type 7 (automated trading) licence. AML/CFT requirements, including Travel Rule compliance, are mandatory.
- **HKMA Stablecoin Framework** — HKMA is developing a licensing regime for fiat-referenced stablecoin issuers, with a consultation process underway. Hong Kong-dollar backed stablecoins are a target use case.
- **Crypto Hub Competition** — Hong Kong is actively competing with Singapore for crypto industry business, offering a potentially more permissive licensing environment while maintaining robust AML/CFT requirements.

## Digital Banking

- **8 Virtual Bank Licences** — HKMA awarded 8 virtual bank licences in 2019–2020. Operational virtual banks include: ZA Bank (ZhongAn-backed), Mox Bank (Standard Chartered + HKT + PCCW), PAOB (Ant Group), Livi Bank (BOC + JD.com), Fusion Bank (Tencent + ICBC), Airstar Bank (Ant Group + Xiaomi), WeLab Bank (WeLab), and Ping An OneConnect Bank. These are full banks with no physical branches.
- **FPS (Faster Payment System)** — operated by HKMA's Hong Kong Interbank Clearing Limited (HKICL), FPS enables real-time transfers in both HKD and RMB using mobile number or email address. This dual-currency capability is unique given Hong Kong's role as an offshore RMB hub.
- **HKMA Open API Framework** — a phased framework requiring banks to publish APIs for product information, then account information, then payment initiation services.

## Key Terminology

- **Authorised Institution (AI)** — the HKMA's statutory classification for all HKMA-regulated banks. AIs include Licensed Banks, Restricted Licence Banks, and Deposit-Taking Companies. All AIs are subject to AMLO obligations.
- **Exchange Fund** — Hong Kong's foreign reserves, managed by HKMA. The Exchange Fund backs the HKD under the Currency Board arrangement and serves as HKMA's primary investment portfolio.
- **Linked Exchange Rate System** — Hong Kong's currency board arrangement that has pegged the HKD to the USD within a band of 7.75–7.85 since 1983. A defining feature of Hong Kong's monetary system, maintained even through significant political and economic developments.
- **HKIMR** — Hong Kong Institute for Monetary and Financial Research, HKMA's research arm; publishes working papers and reports on financial stability and fintech.
- **RMB Offshore Hub** — Hong Kong is the world's largest offshore RMB (Renminbi) clearing and settlement centre. Cross-border RMB flows between mainland China and Hong Kong require understanding of CIPS (China Interbank Payment System) connectivity and PBoC regulations, in addition to HKMA rules.

## Note on Hong Kong and China

The HKMA is entirely separate from the People's Bank of China (PBoC). Hong Kong maintains its own legal system (common law, derived from English law) and financial regulatory framework under "One Country, Two Systems." Hong Kong's AML/CFT framework is distinct from China's. However:
- Cross-border flows between Hong Kong and mainland China have increased significantly and require careful monitoring.
- PEP assessments for Hong Kong-connected individuals should consider mainland Chinese political exposure.
- Stock connect, bond connect, and wealth management connect programs link Hong Kong and mainland capital markets.
