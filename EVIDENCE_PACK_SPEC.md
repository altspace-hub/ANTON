## 1. Purpose and Strategic Framing

### 1.1 What this module is

The Evidence Pack module is ANTON's answer to one question a regulator, auditor, client, or internal compliance function will eventually ask every company that uses AI for professional work:

> *"Show me exactly how this output was produced, what the AI thought, what humans decided, what rules were applied, what went into it, and prove nothing has been tampered with since."*

Today, ANTON captures all the raw material needed to answer that question — `audit_log`, `thinking_content`, `output_versions`, `workflow_runs`, `workflow_steps`, `canvas_changes`, `rule_violations`, `override_log`, reviewer attribution, model/seed metadata, knowledge sources, persona and skill records. But the raw material sits in sixteen different database groups and thirty-something tables. A regulator does not want a database dump. They want a bound, searchable, tamper-evident binder scoped to one project, one mission, one workflow run, one date range — and they want to be able to read it without a platform login.

The Evidence Pack module is that binder. It is a new assembly layer that walks ANTON's existing audit surface for a defined scope, collects every related artifact, writes a cryptographically signed manifest, maps the contents to EU AI Act Annex IV and AMLR auditability requirements, and exports the result as a self-contained `.anton` bundle, a consolidated PDF, a machine-readable JSONL stream, or a password-protected read-only web view.

### 1.2 Why this matters strategically

The EU AI Act enters full application on 2 August 2026 for high-risk AI systems. AMLR applies from 10 July 2027. Deployers of AI systems in regulated industries need demonstrable audit trails — not just in principle, but as an actual deliverable that can be handed to a supervisor during an on-site visit.

Today, every AI deployment in regulated Europe faces the same question and has no good answer: "how do I show the regulator what the AI did?" ANTON should be able to answer that question in one click. This is a direct commercial moat — "regulator-ready by construction" is a line no cloud AI tool can credibly offer, because they do not persist the artifacts to produce it.

Positioning: this is not a compliance feature bolted on. This is ANTON's process-based trust philosophy made physical. You do not trust any individual output. You trust the auditable process that produced it.

### 1.3 Three audiences

The module serves three distinct audiences with different needs:

- **Regulators and external auditors** need a complete, tamper-evident, read-only view scoped to a matter they are investigating. They do not have an ANTON account. They access through a share link, optionally password-protected and time-limited.
- **Internal compliance, legal, and audit functions** need to prepare the above before the regulator arrives. They build, review, redact where legally required (privileged material), and release the pack.
- **Professional users defending their own work** need to be able to say "here is how I produced this analysis" — to a client, a board, a court, an insurer, or a future version of themselves six months later when memory has faded.

All three are served by the same underlying assembly. The UI surface differs by role.

## 2. Regulatory Framing

The spec is organised around three regulatory anchors. Every design decision should be traceable back to at least one of them.

### 2.1 EU AI Act — high-risk system obligations

Key articles the Evidence Pack must address:

- **Article 12 (Record-keeping)** — high-risk AI systems must automatically record events throughout the system's lifecycle. Logs must enable traceability, monitoring of operation, and post-market surveillance.
- **Article 13 (Transparency and provision of information)** — deployers must be provided with information that enables them to interpret the system's output and use it appropriately. The Evidence Pack surfaces this interpretation trail per session.
- **Article 14 (Human oversight)** — natural persons must be able to oversee the AI system. Evidence of who oversaw what, when, and what they decided must be recoverable. Every `override_log`, `canvas_change`, and reviewer decision is Article 14 evidence.
- **Article 15 (Accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity)** — levels of accuracy must be declared and logs must support monitoring. Quality scores, rule violations, and seeds for reproducibility carry this.
- **Article 26 (Obligations of deployers)** — deployers in regulated sectors must keep logs generated by the system for at least six months. The retention policy of the Evidence Pack must respect this.
- **Article 50 (Transparency obligations for certain AI systems)** — disclosure that content is AI-generated. Should be embedded as metadata in every exported artifact.
- **Annex IV (Technical documentation)** — nine-point structure of mandatory technical documentation. The Evidence Pack's compliance mapping document must follow this structure explicitly.

### 2.2 AMLR — auditability dimension

AMLR (Regulation (EU) 2024/1624) and the broader AML package require that AML controls are auditable. The Advisense AMLR presentation materials identified auditability as one of the five AML data quality dimensions — *"Can you demonstrate to a regulator when data was captured, changed, and by whom? Full lineage and traceability are essential under AMLR."* The Evidence Pack is the mechanism that answers this question for any AI-assisted AML workflow — gap analyses, risk assessments, STR drafts, policy updates, EDD files.

### 2.3 GDPR — Article 22 and Article 30

- **Article 22 (Automated individual decision-making)** — where AI output contributes to decisions about natural persons, the data subject has a right to meaningful information about the logic involved. The Evidence Pack's transparency trail is directly responsive.
- **Article 30 (Records of processing activities)** — the pack's manifest provides the provenance information needed for Article 30 records where AI processing is involved.

### 2.4 Sector-specific adaptations (Phase 3)

The compliance mapping layer must be extensible. Near-term targets after the EU AI Act + AMLR baseline: DORA (ICT risk), MiFID II (investment advice records), MiCA (crypto-asset compliance), CSRD (sustainability assurance), EU Pay Transparency Directive (recruitment and pay decisions — ties into the Talent Discovery module).

## 4. Data Model

### 4.1 New tables

Four new tables. All other data is referenced, not copied.

```sql
-- The pack itself
CREATE TABLE evidence_packs (
  id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,                    -- EP-YYYYMMDD-XXXXXX
  title TEXT NOT NULL,
  purpose TEXT,                           -- free text: "AMLR gap analysis audit — Nordea Q2 2026"
  scope_type TEXT NOT NULL,               -- project, mission, workflow_run, session, canvas, date_range, custom
  scope_ref TEXT,                         -- JSON: {project_id, mission_id, session_ids[], start_date, end_date...}
  scope_label TEXT,                       -- human-readable scope summary
  created_by TEXT NOT NULL,               -- user_id
  created_at TEXT NOT NULL,
  finalised_at TEXT,                      -- once finalised, content is immutable
  status TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'draft',   -- draft, finalised, shared, archived, superseded
  hash_manifest TEXT,                     -- SHA-256 of the full manifest, computed at finalise
  signature TEXT,                         -- Ed25519 signature over hash_manifest
  signer_public_key TEXT,                 -- for verification without platform access
  item_count INTEGER DEFAULT 0,
  size_bytes INTEGER DEFAULT 0,
  retention_until TEXT,                   -- minimum retention date (Art 26 = +6 months by default)
  legal_hold INTEGER DEFAULT 0,           -- if 1, cannot be deleted regardless of retention
  supersedes TEXT,                        -- id of previous pack this replaces (if regenerated)
  compliance_frameworks TEXT,             -- JSON array: ["eu_ai_act", "amlr", "gdpr", "dora"]
  notes TEXT,
  FOREIGN KEY (created_by) REFERENCES users(id)
);

-- What's in the pack (polymorphic references into existing tables)
CREATE TABLE evidence_pack_items (
  id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  pack_id TEXT NOT NULL,
  item_type TEXT NOT NULL,                -- session, message, output_version, workflow_run, workflow_step,
                                          -- canvas_change, canvas_comment, rule_violation, rule_exemption,
                                          -- override_event, reviewer_decision, knowledge_source,
                                          -- persona_snapshot, skill_snapshot, model_config, prompt_template
  item_table TEXT NOT NULL,               -- source table name
  item_id TEXT NOT NULL,                  -- primary key in source table
  item_hash TEXT NOT NULL,                -- SHA-256 of canonicalised item content at time of assembly
  included_at TEXT NOT NULL,
  item_order INTEGER DEFAULT 0,           -- for deterministic ordering in the manifest
  redaction_status TEXT DEFAULT 'none',   -- none, partial, full (legal-privilege redactions)
  redaction_reason TEXT,
  regulatory_relevance TEXT,              -- JSON: which articles/dimensions this item evidences
  FOREIGN KEY (pack_id) REFERENCES evidence_packs(id) ON DELETE CASCADE
);

CREATE INDEX idx_pack_items_pack ON evidence_pack_items(pack_id);
CREATE INDEX idx_pack_items_type ON evidence_pack_items(item_type);

-- Regulator share links
CREATE TABLE evidence_pack_shares (
  id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  pack_id TEXT NOT NULL,
  access_token TEXT NOT NULL UNIQUE,      -- cryptographically random, URL-safe
  password_hash TEXT,                     -- bcrypt, optional
  recipient_name TEXT NOT NULL,           -- "Finansinspektionen — Jan Johansson"
  recipient_organisation TEXT NOT NULL,
  recipient_contact TEXT,                 -- email, for access notifications
  purpose TEXT NOT NULL,                  -- "On-site inspection, ref FI-2026-0123"
  created_by TEXT NOT NULL,
  created_at TEXT NOT NULL,
  expires_at TEXT NOT NULL,               -- default 30 days, max 365
  revoked_at TEXT,
  revoked_reason TEXT,
  allow_download INTEGER DEFAULT 1,       -- 0 = view only
  allow_search INTEGER DEFAULT 1,
  watermark_text TEXT,                    -- "CONFIDENTIAL — Finansinspektionen only"
  FOREIGN KEY (pack_id) REFERENCES evidence_packs(id),
  FOREIGN KEY (created_by) REFERENCES users(id)
);

CREATE INDEX idx_pack_shares_token ON evidence_pack_shares(access_token);
CREATE INDEX idx_pack_shares_pack ON evidence_pack_shares(pack_id);

-- Chain-of-custody log for regulator access (every view is logged)
CREATE TABLE evidence_pack_access_log (
  id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,
  share_id TEXT,                          -- null if accessed by internal user
  pack_id TEXT NOT NULL,
  accessed_at TEXT NOT NULL,
  accessor_type TEXT NOT NULL,            -- internal_user, external_auditor, system
  accessor_id TEXT,                       -- user_id for internal, share_id for external
  ip_address_hash TEXT,                   -- hashed, for privacy
  user_agent_hash TEXT,
  action TEXT NOT NULL,                   -- view_index, view_item, search, export, download, verify_signature
  item_accessed TEXT,                     -- specific item viewed, if applicable
  search_query_hash TEXT,                 -- hashed search query, not stored in clear
  success INTEGER DEFAULT 1,
  error_reason TEXT,
  FOREIGN KEY (share_id) REFERENCES evidence_pack_shares(id),
  FOREIGN KEY (pack_id) REFERENCES evidence_packs(id)
);

CREATE INDEX idx_pack_access_pack ON evidence_pack_access_log(pack_id);
CREATE INDEX idx_pack_access_date ON evidence_pack_access_log(accessed_at);
```

### 4.2 No schema changes required to existing tables

This is a hard constraint. The Evidence Pack is an assembly layer. Every item it references must already be recoverable from the existing schema. If the collector cannot reconstruct an item because the source table does not store enough information, **fix the source table in a separate spec** — do not paper over gaps in the Evidence Pack layer.

### 4.3 Retention and legal hold

- Default retention: 6 months after `finalised_at`, matching EU AI Act Article 26(6). Configurable per pack up to 10 years.
- Legal hold flag overrides retention — a pack under legal hold cannot be deleted by any user, including admins, until the hold is released with reason and timestamp logged.
- Superseded packs are never auto-deleted — only marked `superseded` and linked forward.

## 5. Services Architecture

Six new services in `server/services/evidence-pack/`. Each has a single responsibility.

### 5.1 `evidence-pack-collector.ts`

Given a scope definition, walks the database and returns the set of items that belong in the pack.

**Key function:**
```typescript
async function collectForScope(scope: ScopeDefinition): Promise<CollectedItems>
```

The collector must handle every scope type:

- **`project`** — all sessions, missions, workflow_runs, canvases linked to the project, plus all derived items (messages, versions, violations, overrides).
- **`mission`** — the mission record, all tasks, all sessions spawned by the mission, all actions taken by the Action Layer, all Service Pack invocations, all Playwright session traces.
- **`workflow_run`** — the run record, all steps, all outputs of each step, all approvals, all webhook calls made, all downstream sessions triggered.
- **`session`** — the session, all messages (with thinking), all output versions, all rule checks run against it, all canvas activity on its outputs.
- **`canvas`** — the canvas, all participants, all comments, all changes, all underlying sessions that fed it.
- **`date_range`** — all of the above, filtered by `created_at` within range, for a specified user or team.
- **`custom`** — explicit list of item IDs (for pack reassembly or targeted regulator requests).

For every collected item the collector computes:

1. A canonical JSON representation (stable key order, no timestamps that drift between runs of the same content).
2. SHA-256 of the canonical representation.
3. A regulatory relevance tag set (see §7).

**Critical:** the collector walks the graph transitively. A session pulls in its messages pulls in its referenced knowledge sources pulls in the persona and skill snapshots active at session time. A workflow_run pulls in every session it spawned. This is where the existing per-session audit log becomes a project-scope evidence chain.

### 5.2 `evidence-pack-assembler.ts`

Takes collected items and produces the pack structure.

**Responsibilities:**

- Deduplicate items referenced through multiple paths (a session that feeds both a canvas and a workflow_run appears once).
- Apply ordering rules (chronological within item type, then alphabetical by ID).
- Build the manifest JSON with the full structure of the pack.
- Insert rows into `evidence_packs` and `evidence_pack_items`.
- Compute the manifest hash.

**Output manifest structure** (summary):

```json
{
  "pack_id": "EP-20260501-A3F9B2",
  "schema_version": "1.0",
  "anton_version": "0.6.0",
  "title": "...",
  "purpose": "...",
  "scope": {...},
  "created": {"by": "...", "at": "...", "organisation": "..."},
  "compliance_frameworks": ["eu_ai_act", "amlr"],
  "item_count": 487,
  "items_by_type": {...},
  "manifest_hash": "sha256:...",
  "signature": "ed25519:...",
  "signer_public_key": "...",
  "items": [
    {
      "order": 1,
      "type": "session",
      "id": "sess_abc123",
      "hash": "sha256:...",
      "regulatory_relevance": ["eu_ai_act.art_12", "amlr.auditability"],
      "summary": "AMLR Gap Analysis — Nordea Q2 review",
      "content_ref": "items/session_sess_abc123.json"
    },
    ...
  ]
}
```

### 5.3 `evidence-pack-signer.ts`

Wraps `identity.ts`. Signs the manifest hash with the finalising user's Ed25519 key. Includes the signer's public key in the manifest so signature verification does not require platform access.

The signer also produces a **verifier mini-tool**: a standalone HTML file (with embedded JS, no network calls) bundled into every export, which accepts the manifest file and displays "signature valid / invalid / manifest tampered". A regulator can verify authenticity without trusting ANTON.

### 5.4 `evidence-pack-exporter.ts`

Four export formats, all reusing existing export services where possible.

- **`.anton` bundle** (primary). Extends `anton-bundler.ts` with new bundle type `evidence-pack`. ZIP containing `manifest.json`, `signature.txt`, `verifier.html`, `items/*.json`, `assets/*` (attached documents, images referenced in sessions), `compliance/eu_ai_act_annex_iv.md`, `compliance/amlr_auditability.md`, `README.md`.
- **PDF** (human-readable binder). Extends `export-pdf.ts` with an evidence-pack layout. Cover page, table of contents, compliance mapping summary, per-item sections with metadata headers, full thinking content, appendix with cryptographic proofs. Page numbering, consistent headers, watermark if shared externally.
- **JSONL** (machine-readable). One item per line, streaming-friendly. For regulators with their own ingestion systems.
- **Self-contained HTML** (for archival, browsable offline). Single `.html` file with embedded CSS and JS, searchable locally, no network calls.

All exports include the same signed manifest; the signature verifies every format consistently.

### 5.5 `evidence-pack-regulator-view.ts`

Serves the read-only web view for external auditors accessing via share link.

- Validates share token, checks expiry and revocation.
- If password-protected, handles password challenge.
- Enforces view-only or view-and-download per share configuration.
- Logs every access to `evidence_pack_access_log` before rendering.
- Serves a minimal, professional UI — no ANTON navigation, no upsell, no branding noise. Just the pack, a search box, a download button (if allowed), and a "verify signature" link.
- Watermarks every page with the share's `watermark_text`.

### 5.6 `evidence-pack-compliance-mapper.ts`

Produces the compliance mapping documents included in every pack.

- `eu_ai_act_annex_iv.md` — Annex IV nine-point structure filled in for this pack's scope. For each point, either the evidence items that satisfy it, or an explicit "not applicable" with reasoning, or a gap notice directing the user to fix before finalising.
- `amlr_auditability.md` — the five AML data quality dimensions with coverage per the pack's AML workflows, plus AMLR Article 21 record-keeping mapping.
- Extensible to GDPR Article 30, DORA ICT record-keeping, MiCA Article 68, etc., in Phase 3.

The mapper also produces a **pre-finalise compliance check**: before a pack can be finalised, the mapper runs and flags any Annex IV or AMLR requirements that are not evidenced in the pack. The user is shown a "complete the pack" checklist and must either add evidence, justify the absence, or explicitly accept the gap with rationale. Finalising a pack with accepted gaps is allowed but the gaps appear on the cover page — regulators see exactly what is and is not documented.

## 7. Regulatory Relevance Tagging

Every item collected into a pack is tagged with the regulatory touchpoints it evidences. This is what makes the compliance mapper work and what lets regulators filter the pack by article.

Relevance tags are computed during collection based on item type and content:

- **Every `message` with `thinking_content`** → `eu_ai_act.art_13` (transparency)
- **Every `override_log` entry** → `eu_ai_act.art_14` (human oversight)
- **Every `canvas_change` or reviewer decision** → `eu_ai_act.art_14` (human oversight)
- **Every `output_version`** → `eu_ai_act.art_12` (record-keeping), `amlr.auditability`
- **Every `rule_violation`** → `eu_ai_act.art_15` (accuracy/robustness), `eu_ai_act.art_9` (risk management)
- **Every `session` with `seed` recorded** → `eu_ai_act.art_15` (reproducibility)
- **Every `model_config`** → `eu_ai_act.annex_iv.2a` (system description)
- **Every AML-domain session** (detected via area tagging) → `amlr.art_21` (record-keeping), `amlr.auditability`
- **Every recruitment-domain session** (Talent module) → `eu_ai_act.annex_iii.4` (high-risk: employment), `eu_pay_transparency`
- **Every GDPR-flagged session** → `gdpr.art_22`, `gdpr.art_30`

The tag vocabulary lives in a seeded table `compliance_relevance_tags` (new, small reference table) so tags can be added without code changes.

## 9. Cryptographic Integrity

### 9.1 Signing model

- Every finalised pack is signed by the finalising user's Ed25519 private key.
- The public key is embedded in the manifest so verification is self-contained.
- Each user's keypair is stored encrypted at rest in the existing `identity.ts` keystore. Users do not handle raw keys; they authenticate with their normal credentials and the signer service uses the unlocked key.
- For organisations that require corporate signing (not individual), Phase 2 adds organisation-level signing keys managed by admin role.

### 9.2 Verification model

Three ways to verify a pack's integrity, all without platform access:

1. **Standalone HTML verifier** bundled into every export — drag in the manifest, see "valid" or "tampered".
2. **CLI verifier** — a small Node.js script included in the `.anton` bundle for power users.
3. **Web verifier** at `https://anton.openexpert.io/verify` — a static page, no login, drag in manifest, see result. Hosted on Anthropic-independent infrastructure for trust.

### 9.3 Tamper detection

Any modification to any item in the pack changes that item's hash, which changes the manifest hash, which invalidates the signature. Regulators do not need to trust ANTON's claims; they verify mathematically.

### 9.4 What signing does and does not prove

- **Does prove:** this pack's contents existed in this exact form at the moment of finalisation, and were finalised by this authenticated user.
- **Does not prove:** the contents reflect reality outside ANTON. The pack proves the platform's internal record; the underlying professional work being documented must be independently assessed.

This distinction must appear on every pack cover page, verbatim. It is essential for honest regulator communication and protects the user from overclaiming.

## 13. Acceptance Criteria (cumulative across phases)

A successful implementation satisfies all of the following:

1. A user with appropriate permissions can create an evidence pack from any supported scope in three clicks or fewer.
2. Every AI-generated output within the scope appears in the pack with its full thinking content, model configuration, seed, knowledge sources, and human review record.
3. Every human decision within the scope (reviewer approvals, canvas comments, apprentice overrides) is preserved with actor attribution and timestamp.
4. The pack's manifest hash is stable: the same scope assembled twice produces the same hash if no underlying data has changed.
5. A modification to any included item invalidates the pack signature, and the standalone verifier detects this.
6. An external regulator with only the share URL can view, search, and download the pack without creating an ANTON account.
7. Every regulator access is logged in the chain-of-custody log and is visible to the pack owner.
8. The pack cover page truthfully shows the pack's compliance coverage, including gaps and their justifications.
9. A pack under legal hold cannot be deleted, including by admins.
10. No existing ANTON functionality regresses.

## 14. Open Questions for Daniel

Four decisions needed before Phase 1 implementation begins:

1. **Default retention period.** EU AI Act Article 26 says minimum six months. Advisense clients in banking will often need seven or ten years. Proposal: default six months, configurable per pack, configurable minimum per organisation.
2. **Who can finalise a pack?** Proposal: any authenticated user can create drafts; only users with `evidence_pack.finalise` permission (default: admin + analyst roles) can finalise. Tighter by default, loosenable per organisation.
3. **Share link expiry maximum.** Proposal: 30 days default, 365 days maximum. Longer than a year requires pack re-issuance.
4. **Organisation-wide signing keys — Phase 2 or Phase 4?** Individual signing is simpler for Phase 2; corporate signing is what regulated clients will actually use. Proposal: individual in Phase 2, corporate in Phase 4 as planned, with a clear migration path.

## 15. Out of Scope (Explicit Exclusions)

- **Automatic redaction of personal data.** Redaction is a human decision with legal weight. The module surfaces a redaction workflow; it does not auto-redact.
- **Cross-ANTON federation.** Packs produced on Instance A cannot be merged into packs on Instance B. A regulator wanting evidence from multiple ANTON instances receives multiple packs. Merging is an AAP/Network (Layer 3) concern for a later spec.
- **Continuous regulator access to live data.** Packs are finalised snapshots. "Live" regulator dashboards are a separate product concern and would require a fundamentally different trust model.
- **Translating thinking content into regulator-local languages.** Translation is valuable but adds interpretation risk that could invalidate the "exact record" claim. Packs are served in the language the work was done in.

## 16. Final Note to Claude Code

This spec is designed to be executed in the order it is written. Do not skip the investigation protocol. Do not build in parallel what already exists. Extend, do not duplicate.

The test at every stage: *would a Swedish Finansinspektionen inspector, a Maltese FIAU examiner, or an EBA auditor be able to use this pack effectively without training?* If the answer is "maybe with help", keep iterating. The deliverable must feel like a professional evidence binder, not a database export.

Investigation before implementation. Extend before inventing. Truth over polish.
