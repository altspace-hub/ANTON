The Markets pillar — financial research with a public scorecard

Markets is ANTON's research pillar, instrumented for learning. It does not just answer questions about companies and indexes — every prediction it makes is recorded with a deadline and scored against what actually happened, and the scorecard is public, including when it is wrong. It is the most ambitious pillar, and the most honest one about how it is doing.

1What the Markets pillar is

Most AI tools give an answer and move on; nothing ever tells them whether the answer was right. Markets is built to break that loop. Every prediction it makes — a price move, an index direction, a thesis outcome — is recorded with a deadline. When the deadline arrives, ANTON checks the prediction against what actually happened and feeds the verdict back into how it weighs evidence next time.

That feedback loop is the whole point. Markets is the testbed for Layer 2 of ANTON's vision — "Intelligent ANTON": knowledge atoms, pattern detection, predictions and calibration. If the loop proves itself here — and §4 below is candid about where that proof stands — the same machinery can make any pillar self-improving.

2How to think about a learning system

🧭The mental model

Think of Markets as an analyst keeping a scored notebook, not an oracle. An oracle expects to be believed. An analyst writes down a view, dates it, and later marks it right or wrong — and gets better because of that habit. Markets' value is not any single prediction; it is the discipline of checking. When you read a Markets output, read it the way you would read a colleague's note: a reasoned view, with a track record you can inspect, not a fact.

Two ideas make the rest of the pillar make sense:

  • A prediction is a commitment. It comes with a horizon and a measurable claim. That is what makes it scorable — and what makes the calibration honest. A vague view cannot be marked.
  • Calibration is the product. When Markets says it is "70% confident", that number is only meaningful if, historically, claims at 70% came true about 70% of the time. The pillar's job is to make that true over time — and to show you when it isn't yet.
💡Read the why-chain, not just the verdict

Markets always exposes the causal reasoning — the why-chain — behind a conclusion. That is the most useful part. A prediction you disagree with but whose reasoning is laid out plainly is far more valuable than a confident number with no chain. Inspect the links; that is where you decide whether to trust the view.

The split is deliberate, and it mirrors the Risk Atlas philosophy: deterministic engine plus LLM rationale. That separation is what makes the calibration trustworthy — the scorekeeper is not the same component as the storyteller.

How it works The Markets service stack and migrations +

Markets is built across migrations 049_markets_pillar.sql through 062_markets_anton100_indexes.sql — the pillar foundation, thesis entities, patterns & indexes, the learning tables, why-chains (v1 and v2), workflow seeds, PostgreSQL partitioning and optimisations, a cross-pillar reference table, the event calendar, data source seeds and the ANTON 100 index enhancements.

The logic lives in roughly three dozen services under server/services/market-*.ts. Key ones: market-thesis-service.ts and market-thesis-lifecycle-service.ts manage theses; market-prediction-verifier.ts scores predictions against outcomes; market-conditional-accuracy-service.ts and market-pattern-weight-feedback-service.ts handle calibration; market-computation-service.ts runs the Python templates; market-index-service.ts and market-index-rebalance-service.ts drive the ANTON 100; market-why-chains-service.ts builds the causal chains.

4An honest word — the effectiveness audit

A learning pillar should be judged by whether it is actually learning. In April 2026 a closed-loop audit looked hard at exactly that — and the answer was sobering.

⚠What the April 2026 audit found

Prediction accuracy was around 21% — well below where a useful learning loop should sit. Calibration was inverted: higher-confidence predictions were not more reliable. The thesis lifecycle was thin, and only one index rebalance had ever run. In short, the machinery was built and wired, but the loop was not yet measurably making ANTON better. That is the honest status — see Build status for where every pillar stands.

Because of that audit, two environment flags exist so an operator can pause the expensive parts of Markets while the effectiveness work continues:

  • MARKETS_THINKING_DISABLED=true — pauses every LLM-spending Markets phase. Free phases (NAV, prices, prediction checkpoints, event triggers, materialised-view refreshes) keep running.
  • MARKETS_FETCH_DISABLED=true — pauses every external Markets data fetch (market data, news, RSS).

If your Markets screens look quiet, an operator may have set these. That is intentional restraint, not a bug. We are telling you this because a learning system that hides its scorecard is not learning — it is just performing. Markets shows you its real numbers, including the unflattering ones, and that candour is the feature.

5Working with a thesis, step by step

A thesis is the central object in Markets: a view about an entity or theme, with reasoning attached and an outcome to be checked. Here is the lifecycle.

  1. Open Markets and pick a subject

    An ANTON 100 index, a constituent entity, or a theme. The pillar gives you the current picture and recent why-chains.

  2. Form a thesis

    State a view — "this entity is mispriced because…". ANTON builds a why-chain: the explicit causal links behind the claim.

  3. Attach a prediction

    Turn the view into something scorable: a measurable claim, a horizon, a confidence level. Without these it cannot be marked.

  4. Let the loop run

    The prediction is logged. At its deadline the verifier checks it against real outcomes — no LLM, no editing.

  5. Read the verdict and the recalibration

    Right or wrong, the result updates pattern weights and your calibration record. Review the thesis lifecycle to see how the view held up.

📌Example — a thesis through the loop

You think a constituent of an ANTON 100 index is undervalued. You write that as a thesis; ANTON builds a four-link why-chain — earnings momentum, a sector tailwind, a depressed peer multiple, a catalyst on the calendar. You attach a prediction: +8% within 90 days, confidence 62%. The prediction is logged. Ninety days later the verifier checks the actual move — say it returned +3%. The claim is marked a partial miss; the pattern weights behind "sector tailwind" are nudged down a little, and your 62%-confidence band gets one more data point. No single run made ANTON smarter — the habit of marking it did. And, candidly, today that habit is still early: see §4.

How it works Predictions, verification and calibration +

A thesis is stored via market-thesis-service.ts; its progression through draft → active → resolved is handled by market-thesis-lifecycle-service.ts. Predictions are recorded against the thesis with a horizon and confidence.

At the deadline, market-prediction-verifier.ts compares the claim with real market data (subject to MARKETS_FETCH_DISABLED), producing a deterministic verdict. market-conditional-accuracy-service.ts rolls verdicts into calibration curves, and market-pattern-weight-feedback-service.ts adjusts the weights of the patterns that fed the why-chain. market-backtest-runner.ts replays history to test the loop offline. All of this is what the April 2026 audit measured.

6Best practices

✓ Do
  • Read the why-chain before you read the prediction — the reasoning is the real output.
  • Treat every prediction as a dated, scorable commitment, not advice.
  • Check the calibration record — does ANTON's confidence track reality yet?
  • Use the ANTON 100 indexes as a stable frame to compare against.
  • Keep theses around long enough to be marked; the loop only learns from resolved ones.
✕ Don't
  • Don't treat a Markets prediction as financial advice — it is a scored analyst view.
  • Don't trust a confidence number without checking the calibration behind it.
  • Don't assume the loop is mature — the April 2026 audit found ~21% accuracy.
  • Don't be alarmed by quiet screens — an operator may have set the pause flags.
  • Don't skip the why-chain just because the headline answer looks confident.
★The Markets habit

Use Markets the way a disciplined analyst uses a journal: write the view, date it, and come back to see if it held. The pillar's promise is not perfect predictions — it is an honest, improving record. Judge it, and use it, on that.
