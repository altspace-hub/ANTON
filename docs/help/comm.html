ANTON Comm — standalone communication & money

ANTON Comm is a peer-to-peer communication app that needs no ANTON Local instance behind it. It bootstraps its own identity, talks to other people end-to-end encrypted, and carries a full FutureChain wallet with a jurisdiction-aware tax engine. It is the most self-contained app in the suite.

For: anyone — no ANTON Local needed Android + PWA · Capacitor 8.3 Navy theme E2E messaging + wallet

1What ANTON Comm is

ANTON Comm lives at src/comm/ and ships as a Capacitor Android app and an installable PWA. It is a standalone product: install it, and you have an encrypted messenger, an ephemeral social feed, a local calendar, a portal browser and a cryptocurrency wallet — with nothing else required.

Unlike ANTON Companion, which is a window onto a desktop instance, Comm is the whole thing. There is no server holding your data and no instance to pair with. Your identity is created on the phone, your messages are encrypted end-to-end, and your wallet ledger lives only on the device.

2How to think about it

🧭The mental model

Think of ANTON Comm as two apps that happen to share a phone: a private messenger and a private wallet. They sit on the same Ed25519 identity and the same relay connection, but neither needs a server that knows about you. The relay routes ciphertext it cannot read; the wallet ledger never leaves the device. Comm's promise is that your conversations and your money are both yours alone — no account, no cloud copy.

Two ideas explain almost every screen:

  • Your identity is a keypair, not an account. On first run Comm generates an Ed25519 identity and derives a shareable contact hash — ANTON-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX. There is no sign-up, no recovery email. If you lose the phone without a backup, that identity is gone.
  • The relay is a dumb pipe. Every message is encrypted before it touches the network. The ANTON Mesh relay matches connections and forwards opaque bytes; it cannot decrypt, edit or impersonate. It is a post office that only ever handles sealed envelopes.
⚠Comm currently ships only English & Swedish

Comm includes locale catalogues for English and Swedish only. A user whose phone is set to any other language will see the entire app in English. This is a known, deliberate gap — translating Comm into the other 36 suite languages is a separate effort, tracked on Build status. It does not affect functionality, only the interface language.

3First run — bootstrapping an identity

Because there is no account, onboarding's real job is to create the keypair that is you, and to connect to the relay.

  1. Open the app

    The Welcome screen explains, in a sentence, that Comm is a private messenger with a wallet, and that everything stays on the phone.

  2. Create your identity

    Comm generates an Ed25519 keypair and writes the private key into the phone's secure storage. From it, it derives your contact hash ANTON-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX — the address you share.

  3. Pick a display name

    A name your contacts will see. It is local cosmetic metadata, not part of the cryptographic identity.

  4. Connect to the relay

    Comm opens a WebSocket to wss://relay.futurechain.eu and announces itself by contact hash so peers can reach you.

  5. Add your first contact

    Exchange contact hashes — by QR in person, or by pasting a hash — and the encrypted channel between you two is live.

How it works Identity, crypto & the relay client +

src/comm/services/identity.ts mints the Ed25519 identity and derives the contact hash; the private key is held by secure-store.ts. Message encryption is in crypto.ts — HPKE / X25519 sealing, so each message is end-to-end encrypted to the recipient's key.

relay-client.ts speaks a compact 5-byte-framed WebSocket protocol to wss://relay.futurechain.eu — HELLO_COMM to announce, DELIVER_COMM to route a ciphertext frame. A frame-type byte lets the same relay carry Comm traffic and ANTON instance traffic on one connection without confusing them.

4Messaging, Wassup, Events & Portals

Comm's communication side is four features layered on the same encrypted channel to your contacts.

E2E chat

One-to-one encrypted conversations with reactions, message edits and deletes, polls, voice notes, media and location sharing, read receipts, and disappearing messages with a per-thread timer. Edits and poll votes are queued so they survive an offline peer.

Wassup — the ephemeral feed

A status feed: short text or an image, visible for 24 hours by default, with likes and comments. It is fanned out over the same chat layer to your contacts only — there is no public timeline and no algorithm.

Events

A local calendar. Create an event, invite contacts, and collect RSVPs — all carried as ordinary E2E chat messages. No event server ever sees who is going where.

Portals

Browse ANTON portals discovered through the relay's registry, then invoke a portal's capabilities directly on the owner's instance. Descriptors and pages are cached so a flaky network falls back to the last good copy.

💡Why "ephemeral" is a feature, not a limitation

Wassup posts expiring after 24 hours, and disappearing messages clearing on a timer, are not Comm forgetting to save your data. They are Comm choosing not to. A status that vanishes can't be screenshotted from a years-old archive; a conversation that clears can't be subpoenaed from a backup that doesn't exist. Treat the timers as a privacy tool you reach for on purpose.

How it works The IndexedDB v7 schema +

All Comm data sits in one IndexedDB database, anton-comm, defined in a single place — src/comm/services/db.ts. There is one openDb() and one schema; feature modules import it rather than opening the database themselves, which avoids the version-race that once hung the chat list on "Loading…".

The schema has grown by version: v1 contacts, then messages and events, the wassup_posts / wassup_interactions stores, v5 an inline_outbox queue for edits and poll-votes that must survive an offline peer, v6 a portal_cache, and v7 a wallet_txs ledger store. Per the tax rules the wallet ledger must stay on-device — it is never synced to a server.

5The FutureChain wallet & tax engine

Comm carries a full FutureChain wallet — the second "app" inside the app. It can send, receive and swap FutureChain (FTC), and it keeps every transaction in an on-device ledger that a tax engine reads.

Wallet & ledger

A FutureChain secp256k1 wallet. Every send, receive, swap and refund is written to the local transaction ledger — the wallet_txs store — which is the single source of truth for both your balance history and the tax engine. It never leaves the phone.

The jurisdiction-aware tax engine

Built on @futurechain/sdk/tax. It computes a per-transaction cost basis, applies holding-period relief and loss offset, and produces position and annual reports — exportable as a Swedish K4 form or generic CSV.

Two more pieces support payments:

  • Money profile — an optional, self-declared baseline: date of birth, nationality, occupation, source of funds, expected monthly spend and a typical payment size. It is private to the phone.
  • The light fraud engine — the same 9-rule deterministic check that ANTON Pay §5 describes in full. Byte-identical code, run before every payment is confirmed.
🧭How to think about the tax engine

The tax engine is a bookkeeper, not a tax adviser. It applies the rules for your declared jurisdiction to the ledger it can see, and produces a report you can hand to an accountant or paste into a filing. It does not know about transactions you made in another wallet, and it does not file anything for you. Treat its K4 / CSV output as a well-prepared draft — accurate for what Comm knows, and a starting point for the rest.

⚠Honest status — the payment rail is partial

The SDK tax engine is in progress (Swedish rules done, other jurisdictions in draft), the wallet module's signing is partial, and the ISO 20022 PACS.008 builder is stubbed pending the FutureChain Rust core. Comm assembles and shows PACS.008 drafts today; cryptographic signing switches on when the SDK unstubs. See Build status for the full honest map.

6Sending a payment, step by step

Paying a merchant in Comm works exactly like ANTON Pay — the fraud check is the same engine. See The payment flow for the end-to-end picture.

  1. Open Wallet, tap Scan

    The camera reads a merchant's futurechain:pay QR. A manual paste fallback exists if the camera can't be used.

  2. Comm decodes & validates

    It checks the scheme and the expiry. An expired code is rejected with a clear message rather than a quiet failure.

  3. Read the Review screen

    FTC amount and an estimated value in your currency, the merchant, the purpose, the live expiry countdown, and an expandable ISO 20022 detail panel — every value lifted from the QR.

  4. Check the fraud panel

    The 9-rule engine compares the payment to your money profile and your ledger history. A warning arms a two-step confirm.

  5. Confirm & send

    The transaction is written to the on-device ledger with a PACS.008 draft, and the tax engine picks it up for cost-basis tracking.

📌Example — paying for a chair

Erik scans the QR at a design shop. Review shows 24.00 FTC ≈ 264 SEK · Form & Function AB · Retail, 13 minutes on the countdown. One signal fires — above typical, because Erik set 10 FTC as his typical payment and this is a one-off furniture purchase. The story makes sense, so he reads it, confirms once, and the payment lands in his ledger. Months later, the tax engine uses this exact ledger row to compute the cost basis on the FTC he spent.

How it works The Comm payment services +

The payment path is a small set of single-responsibility services in src/comm/services/. fraud-engine.ts exports the pure assessPayment(pending, money, behavior) function — no I/O, no network, no LLM — byte-identical to ANTON Pay's. money-profile.ts holds the self-declared baseline; behavior-profile.ts derives the empirical one from the ledger.

transactions.ts reads and writes the wallet_txs ledger; pacs008-draft.ts assembles the ISO 20022 draft; payment-identity.ts supplies the debtor party. tax-bridge.ts connects the ledger to @futurechain/sdk/tax, and tax-residency.ts / ftc-classification.ts feed the jurisdiction logic. wallet.ts is the wallet itself.

7Best practices

✓ Do
  • Exchange contact hashes in person by QR when you can — it's the strongest verification.
  • Fill in the money profile — it makes the fraud engine far smarter.
  • Use disappearing messages and Wassup's 24h expiry as deliberate privacy tools.
  • Export a K4 / CSV from the tax engine before filing season and give it to your accountant.
  • Back up before resetting the app — the identity and the wallet are irreplaceable.
✕ Don't
  • Don't expect to recover an identity by email — there is no account and no recovery.
  • Don't treat the tax report as filed advice; it's an accurate draft of what Comm knows.
  • Don't ignore a stack of fraud signals because each one alone seems minor.
  • Don't assume a non-English phone shows a translated app — only en/sv ship today.
  • Don't expect signed settlement yet — PACS.008 drafts populate; signing waits on the SDK.
★Treat the phone as the vault

In Comm there is no cloud copy of anything — not your messages, not your identity, not your wallet. That is the whole point, and it puts one habit at the centre: a real backup, kept somewhere safe, made before you ever need it. A standalone app that owes you nothing also can't rescue you. The phone is the vault; back the vault up.
