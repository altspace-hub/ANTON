ANTON Pay — the customer payments app

ANTON Pay is a focused, phone-only app for private people who just want to pay merchants — scan a code, check it's safe, confirm. It is the simplest app in the ANTON suite, and a good place to learn the ideas the other payment apps share.

For: customers / private people Android + PWA 38 languages No backend — all on-device

1What ANTON Pay is for

ANTON Pay does one job well: it lets a person pay a merchant in FutureChain (FTC) by scanning a QR code. It is the counterpart to ANTON Business, which is the app the merchant uses to show that code.

It exists because not everyone wants the full ANTON Comm experience — messaging, social feed, events. Some people only want to pay. ANTON Pay is that: a wallet, a scanner, a payment-review screen, and a quiet on-device fraud check. Nothing else.

2How to think about it

🧭The mental model

Think of ANTON Pay as a careful wallet, not a bank app. It never moves money on its own and it never talks to a server about you. When you scan a code, the app reads exactly what the merchant put in it, shows it to you in plain language, and compares it against your own normal pattern — so a payment that looks wrong stands out before you tap Confirm.

Two ideas are worth holding onto, because they explain almost every screen:

  • The QR is a contract. Everything you see on the review screen — amount, merchant, purpose, expiry — came out of the QR code. The app adds nothing. If a detail looks wrong, the code is wrong.
  • The fraud engine is advisory, never a gate. It is there to make you pause and think, not to block you. You are always allowed to pay. It simply makes sure a strange payment is a decision, not an accident.
💡Good to know

ANTON Pay has no login and no account. Your identity is a wallet key created on first run and held in the phone's secure storage. If you reset the app or lose the phone without a backup, that wallet is gone — treat it like cash.

3Setting up — first run

Onboarding is deliberately short. A payments app is useless without a wallet, so ANTON Pay creates one for you up front rather than hiding it in settings.

  1. Open the app

    The Welcome screen explains, in one sentence, what ANTON Pay does.

  2. Create your wallet

    Tap Create my wallet. An Ed25519 key pair is generated on the phone and locked into secure storage. This is instant and needs no network.

  3. Add your details (optional)

    You can enter your name and address now or later. They are only used to fill in payment records — see §6.

  4. You're ready

    The Done screen shows your wallet address. From here on, the Home screen's big Scan to pay button is all you need.

4Making a payment, step by step

This is the core flow. It is the same every time.

  1. Tap "Scan to pay"

    The camera opens. If the camera is unavailable, use Enter code manually and paste the futurechain:pay link.

  2. Point at the merchant's QR

    The app decodes and checks the code — is it a real ANTON payment code, and has it expired? An expired code is rejected with a clear message.

  3. Read the Review screen

    Amount in FTC (and an estimated value in your currency), the merchant, the purpose, and a live expiry countdown. Tap ISO 20022 payment details to see the full structured record.

  4. Check the fraud panel

    If anything looks unusual, a coloured panel lists why — see §5. At warning level you confirm twice.

  5. Confirm & pay

    The payment is recorded on your phone with a full PACS.008 draft. The merchant settles it on their side through Safello.

📌Example — paying for lunch

You scan the QR on the café counter. Review shows 12.50 FTC ≈ 138 SEK · Café Lindgren · Restaurant. The amount matches the menu, the purpose makes sense, the countdown says 14 minutes left. One signal appears — "You've never paid this merchant before" — which is true and harmless for a café you're visiting for the first time. You tap Confirm & pay. Done.

How it works Decoding the QR & building the PACS.008 draft +

The QR carries a futurechain:pay URI. payment.ts decodes it with the SDK's reference.decode() (ADR-004 v1), validating the scheme, parameters and the exp expiry timestamp.

On confirm, pacs008-draft.ts assembles an ISO 20022 PACS.008 draft: the debtor is your saved identity (or a bare wallet address if you skipped it), the creditor is the merchant party extracted from the QR's cn/cc/cct/cst/cpc fields. The ADR-004 purpose is mapped to an ISO external purpose code — RETAIL→GDDS, RESTAURANT/SERVICE→SCVE, EVENT→OTHR, REFUND→REFUND.

The draft is stored on the payment record but not cryptographically signed — real signing is blocked on the FutureChain Rust core (iso20022_pacs008.rs) being vendored. See Build status.

5The fraud engine — reading the signals

Before you confirm, ANTON Pay quietly compares the payment against two pictures of "normal for you": what you declared in your money profile, and what your actual payment history shows. If something doesn't fit, it raises a signal. Nine rules can fire.

Dormancy breakA large first payment after a long quiet gapcaution
🧭How to read a signal

A signal is not an accusation — it is a fact about how this payment compares to your past. "New merchant" at a café you're visiting for the first time is expected. The same signal combined with "5× your typical amount" and "expiring QR" is a pattern worth stopping for. Read the signals together, and ask: does the story make sense?

Signals are weighted — info 8, caution 22, warning 45 — summed and capped at 100. The total sets the panel's level:

  • Clear — no panel, or a quiet "good to know".
  • Worth a look — a caution-toned panel; read it, then proceed.
  • This looks unusual — a warning-toned panel; the Pay anyway button requires a second, deliberate tap.
⚠If you see a warning

Stop. Re-read the amount and the merchant name. Ask the person you're paying to confirm them out loud. The "expiring QR" signal in particular is a known scam pattern — a fraudster wants you to rush. A real merchant will happily show you a fresh code.

How it works The fraud engine is pure and deterministic +

fraud-engine.ts exports a single pure function — assessPayment(pending, money, behavior) — with no I/O, no network and no LLM call. Given the same inputs it always returns the same { score, level, signals[] }, which makes it fully unit-testable (fraud-engine.test.ts).

The two baselines: money-profile.ts holds your self-declared figures; behavior-profile.ts derives the empirical picture — median and max amount, known counterparties, active hours, 24h and 30d velocity — from your local payment history. The same engine, byte-identical, also runs inside ANTON Comm.

6Money profile & payment identity

Two optional screens in Settings make ANTON Pay work better. Both are private to the phone — the app never sends them anywhere.

Payment details (your identity)

Your name and address. These fill the debtor side of the ISO 20022 record on every payment. Optional — without them the record falls back to your bare wallet address.

Money profile (your baseline)

Date of birth, nationality, occupation, where your money comes from, your expected monthly spend and a typical payment size. This is the baseline the fraud engine measures against.

💡Why filling in the money profile helps

The fraud engine works with whatever it has. With no money profile it can still use your payment history — but it can't catch a first-ever payment that's far above what you'd ever intend to spend, because it has nothing to compare to. Two honest numbers — typical payment size and monthly budget — make the "above typical" and "monthly cap" signals possible. It is a five-minute setup that pays off the first time something is wrong.

7History & activity review

Every confirmed payment is kept on the phone as a receipt. Two screens let you look back.

  • Payment history — every payment, newest first. Tap a row to expand the full detail, including the PACS.008 draft and any fraud signals that fired at the time.
  • Activity review — a dashboard of your pattern: how many payments you've made, your typical and largest amounts, which merchants you've paid, and a list of every payment that was flagged. It's the after-the-fact view that helps you notice a problem you missed in the moment.
★A habit worth keeping

Open Activity review once a week. You're not auditing — you're keeping a feel for your own normal. The better you know your pattern, the faster a wrong payment will jump out at you, in the app or out of it.

8Best practices

✓ Do
  • Fill in your money profile — it makes the fraud check far smarter.
  • Read the whole Review screen before confirming, not just the amount.
  • Treat a warning as a real stop sign — verify out loud.
  • Check Activity review periodically to stay close to your pattern.
  • Back up before resetting the app or changing phones — the wallet is irreplaceable.
✕ Don't
  • Don't let anyone rush you because a QR is "about to expire".
  • Don't ignore a stack of signals just because each one alone seems minor.
  • Don't assume the app blocked anything — it never does; the decision is yours.
  • Don't pay a code whose merchant name you can't verify.
  • Don't treat the estimated currency value as exact — it's a live guide, the FTC amount is what's paid.
📌Example — the engine doing its job

A message asks you to pay a "delivery fee" and includes a payment link. You paste it. Review shows 340 FTC to a merchant you've never seen, the panel is red — "This looks unusual" — listing new counterparty, large, above typical (you set 10 FTC as typical) and expiring QR. The story doesn't make sense for a delivery fee. You don't tap the second confirm. The engine didn't block you — it just made the strangeness impossible to miss.
