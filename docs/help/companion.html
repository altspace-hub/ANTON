ANTON Companion — your instance, in your pocket

ANTON Companion is the mobile surface of your own ANTON Local instance. It is not a separate product with its own data — it is a window. You pair a phone to a desktop instance once, and from then on the phone shows that instance's work, lets you approve its decisions, and lets you ask it things from anywhere.

1What ANTON Companion is

ANTON Companion lives at src/app/ and ships as a Capacitor Android app, an installable PWA, and an iOS scaffold ready to overlay onto a Mac-generated project. Whatever the wrapper, the job is the same: be the trusted phone client of a specific ANTON Local instance.

It exists for the professional who can't always be at the desk but still needs to act — approve an action a Mission is waiting on, answer a question, photograph a document into a workspace, dictate a quick research request. The instance keeps all the intelligence and all the data; the phone is a careful, well-secured remote.

2How to think about it

🧭The mental model

Companion is a window onto an instance, not a standalone app. It holds almost no state of its own — just the device keypair and the list of instances it has been paired to. Every list, every Mission, every approval you see was computed on the desktop and sent down. If the instance is off, the window shows nothing. That is the design: the brain stays on your machine; the phone is the glass you look through.

Two consequences are worth holding onto, because they explain the whole app:

  • Trust is per-device, not per-account. Companion has no username and no shared login. A phone earns access through the pairing ritual, and the instance can revoke that one device's certificate without touching any other. Losing the phone is contained.
  • The phone never holds the keys to the kingdom. Push payloads carry only an event id and a severity — never the confidential content. Approvals are signed on the phone but decided against data the instance shows you at read time. A stolen, locked phone leaks nothing.
💡Good to know

Because Companion is a window, it is genuinely fine to pair several phones to one instance, or one phone to several instances. There is no "primary device." Each pairing is an independent certificate. See §4.

3Pairing a device, step by step

Pairing is the one-time ritual that turns a fresh phone into a trusted client. It is an Ed25519 enrollment exchange with no central authority and no password — the full mechanics are covered on Pairing & enrollment; here is the walkthrough as you experience it in the app.

  1. On the desktop — "Connect a device"

    An admin opens the pairing screen in ANTON Local. The instance issues a 60-second enrollment package — its public keys, a self-binding signature, relay endpoints, a one-time nonce, and an optional 6-digit out-of-band code — and renders it as a QR.

  2. On the phone — open Join

    Open Companion's Join screen. The phone generates a fresh Ed25519 keypair and writes the private key into the biometric-protected Keychain / Keystore. It never leaves the device.

  3. Scan the QR

    Point the camera at the desktop QR. Companion parses the package and, if the desktop showed a 6-digit code, asks you to type it — a defence against scanning the wrong screen.

  4. Sign the challenge

    The phone signs ${token}.${nonce}.${publicKey} and POSTs it to /api/app/enrollment/complete. The server verifies the signature and code.

  5. Receive the device certificate

    The instance issues a device certificate and a session token. Companion biometric-locks the credentials. The phone is now a paired client.

📌Example — a consultant on a site visit

Maria runs ANTON Local on her office workstation. Before leaving she opens "Connect a device", and on her phone taps Join and scans the QR — confirming the 6-digit code 428137 shown on the desktop. Thirty seconds later her phone holds a device certificate. On the client site she gets a push: "Mission checkpoint — high". She opens Companion, reads the proposed action, holds her thumb to the fingerprint sensor, and approves it. The Mission continues without her touching the desktop.

How it works The enrollment ritual in code +

On the phone, src/app/services/identity.ts mints the Ed25519 keypair (via @noble/ed25519) and stores the private key through a tier-aware secure store — @aparajita/capacitor-secure-storage on a device, a fallback in the PWA. pairing-url.ts parses the QR package; enrollment.ts drives the complete POST.

Server side, server/services/app-enrollment-service.ts issues the 60-second-TTL package, verifies the returned signature and confirmation code, and writes the device certificate. The instance's own Ed25519 private key is AES-256-GCM encrypted in instance_identity.privkey_encrypted when INSTANCE_KEY_ENCRYPTION_KEY is set — without it the service stores plaintext and logs a one-time warning.

Tables (migrations 094, 130, 131): app_devices holds the Ed25519-paired phones, app_enrollment_tokens carries the live token plus its confirmation_code, and app_session_tokens holds the issued session.

4Multiple instances & transports

One phone can be paired to several ANTON Local instances — your own workstation, a team server, a client's hosted instance. Companion makes the active instance unambiguous so you never act on the wrong one.

The instance switcher

A Wallet-card-style bottom sheet lists every paired instance. Switching is race-free — a half-finished switch can never leave you talking to one instance with another's credentials. An InstanceTopBar always shows which instance is in focus.

Two transports

Direct HTTPS when the instance is reachable on the LAN or at a public URL. Mesh relay when it is not — Noise-IK ciphertext is bounced through the ANTON Mesh relay, which matches you by instance id and can never read the traffic.

💡Which transport am I on?

You usually don't need to care — Companion prefers a direct connection and falls back to the relay automatically. The relay only ever sees encrypted bytes and connection metadata, so "I'm on the relay" is not a privacy downgrade for your message contents. It is a downgrade only in that the relay could observe that your phone connected, and could be a denial-of-service point.

5The approvals inbox — the enterprise wedge

The approvals inbox is the single feature that makes Companion worth deploying across an organisation. ANTON Local runs Missions and agents that pause at checkpoints — "I'm about to send this email", "I'm about to spend against this budget". Those pending decisions surface in Companion as a severity-sorted inbox, and your reply is cryptographically signed.

How an approval behaves depends on its severity:

  • Critical / High — opening the item, and confirming it, requires a biometric re-confirm (fingerprint or face). Any item explicitly marked requires_biometric behaves the same way regardless of severity.
  • Normal / Low — a single deliberate tap.

When this phone holds a paired keypair, the reply is sent as a signed envelope: an Ed25519 signature over the decision plus a replay-protected nonce. The instance can prove which device approved what, and the same reply can't be replayed later.

The Approvals tab carries a live badge with the pending count, so an urgent decision is visible without opening the app.

⚠An approval is a real decision

A critical checkpoint usually means money moves, a message goes out, or something irreversible happens. The biometric prompt is there to make sure it is you and that it is deliberate. Read the full item — Mission name, the proposed action, the data behind it — before you place your thumb. Don't clear the inbox on autopilot.

How it works Checkpoints & signed-envelope replies +

The instance writes pending decisions to the app_checkpoints table; server/services/app-checkpoint-service.ts exposes them and decides the biometric requirement from severity. The app pulls them through /api/app/checkpoints/*.

On the phone, src/app/services/checkpoints.ts is the approvals client. When a device keypair exists it wraps the response in a signed envelope — identity.ts produces the Ed25519 signature, and the server checks the nonce against app_signed_envelope_nonces so a captured reply cannot be replayed. The ApprovalsScreen.tsx is a primary tab with the live pending badge.

6Push, voice & capture

Three features turn Companion from a viewer into something you can act through.

Push

APNs, FCM and web-push. A notification carries only an event id, a severity and an opaque title plus a deep link — never the confidential content. Tapping it opens the right screen, where the real data is fetched over the authenticated channel.

Voice

A full-screen, Telegram-style hold-to-talk overlay with live captions, an on-device speech fallback, platform text-to-speech for the reply, and immediate barge-in when you tap. Good for a quick research question with your hands full.

Capture

Camera, photo library, or the system share-sheet. The image is resized to 2048px at ~70% quality before it is POSTed to the instance — so a document photo becomes workspace input without a huge upload.

💡Why push carries so little

It is a deliberate privacy stance. Push notifications pass through Apple's and Google's servers. If the payload contained the actual approval text or a document summary, that text would sit on a third-party server. By carrying only an id and a severity, ANTON keeps every confidential word on your instance and your phone — the notification is just a doorbell.

How it works The capture and push pipelines +

On the phone src/app/services/capture.ts wraps the Capacitor camera and share-target APIs and does the resize before upload; tts.ts, haptics.ts and biometric.ts are the other thin Capacitor wrappers, and push.ts registers the device token. The VoiceMode overlay and CapturePage.tsx are the surfaces.

Server side, server/services/app-push-service.ts dispatches to APNs / FCM / web-push and reads device tokens from the app_push_tokens table. Real dispatch needs APP_GATEWAY_PUSH=true plus provider keys — until those are set the dispatcher is a stub. See Build status for where push currently stands.

7Pro mode vs Standard mode

Companion mirrors the desktop's breadth, which is a lot of surface for a phone. It offers two shapes for that surface.

ModeShapeBuilt for
Pro mode35+ screens mirroring the desktop pillars — Chat, Tasks, Schedule, Search, Markets, Radar, Missions, My Work, Community, Portals, Wallet, Work modules, School.Power users who use the full ANTON Local workspace and want all of it on the phone.
Standard modeA simplified 4-tab layout — the essentials only, with plain-language labels.NGO deployments, occasional users, and anyone who only needs Chat, Approvals and a couple of core surfaces.
🧭Choosing a mode for a deployment

The instinct is to give everyone Pro mode "just in case." Resist it. A 4-tab Standard layout is not a lesser app — for someone whose whole job on the phone is "approve the things that need approving," it is a better app: nothing to get lost in. Match the mode to what the person actually does, not to what the app can do.

8Best practices

✓ Do
  • Confirm the 6-digit code during pairing — it stops a wrong-screen scan.
  • Set INSTANCE_KEY_ENCRYPTION_KEY on the instance so its key is encrypted at rest.
  • Read the whole approval — Mission, action, data — before the biometric tap.
  • Revoke a lost phone's device certificate from the instance immediately.
  • Pick Standard mode for users who only ever need to approve and chat.
✕ Don't
  • Don't treat Companion as a data store — it is a window; the instance holds everything.
  • Don't clear the approvals inbox on autopilot; each critical item is a real decision.
  • Don't expect Companion to work with the instance offline — no instance, no window.
  • Don't assume push tells the whole story — it carries only an id; open the app for detail.
  • Don't share one pairing across phones; pair each device for its own revocable certificate.
★The habit that makes Companion safe

Treat the device certificate like a key to your office, because that is what it is. When a phone is retired, sold or lost, the very first action is to revoke its certificate on the instance — not to "remember to do it later." Pairing is cheap and per-device by design precisely so that revocation is clean and total.
