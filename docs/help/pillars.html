The 12 pillars — modes of intelligence

A pillar is a whole way of working that ANTON can switch into. There are twelve, from the expert consulting of Work to the self-learning intelligence of Markets. This page explains what each one is for, how to switch between them, and — most usefully — how to decide which pillar a task belongs in.

1What a pillar is

ANTON Local is not one tool — it is twelve, sharing one window. Each of those twelve is a pillar: a distinct mode of intelligence, with its own user interface, its own back-end services and its own data model. The Work pillar is a consulting workspace of expert modules. Markets is a self-learning financial-intelligence engine. Missions is a multi-step automation runner. They look and behave differently because they are different — they simply live under one roof.

You are always inside exactly one pillar. The whole left sidebar, the available modules, the workspace shape — all of it reflects the pillar you have selected. Switching pillars is the biggest move you make in ANTON.

2How to think about pillars

🧭The mental model

Think of the pillars as rooms in a workshop, not tabs in an app. Each room is set up for a different kind of work — one has the consulting desk, one is the trading floor, one is the automation bay. You do not carry a welding torch into the accounting room. When you start a task, the first and most important question is not "which module?" but "which room am I doing this in?". Choose the room — the pillar — and the right tools are already laid out on the bench.

Two ideas follow from that and are worth keeping:

  • Pillar before module. A module only exists inside its pillar. If you cannot find a module, you are very often in the wrong pillar — switch first, search second.
  • Pillars do not share state. Because each has its own data model, a Markets thesis is not visible from Grow, and a Mission is not a Work session. They interconnect deliberately (a Mission can run Work modules), but by default a pillar is its own world.

4Switching pillars

You change pillar with the App Mode toggle. It is the most important navigation control in ANTON — and switching is instant, with no page reload.

   THE APP MODE TOGGLE  —  one click changes everything below it

   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  Work ▾   ◀── you are here                                │
   ├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │  Work · School · Life · Pathfinder · Markets · Community  │
   │  Procure · Civic · Grow · Payments · Portals · Missions   │

                          │  pick a pillar
                          ▼
   ┌──────────────┐   the SIDEBAR, the MODULE LIST, the
   │   SIDEBAR    │   WORKSPACE SHAPE and the DATA MODEL all
   │  re-renders  │   swap to match the chosen pillar
   └──────────────┘
  1. Find the App Mode toggle

    It sits at the top of the interface, showing the name of the current pillar — Work by default.

  2. Open it and pick a pillar

    The toggle expands to the full list of twelve. Click the one you want.

  3. The whole pillar loads

    The sidebar, module list and workspace re-render for the new pillar instantly. Your previous pillar's state is preserved — switching back returns you to where you were.

💡Switching loses nothing

Because each pillar keeps its own state, moving from Work to Markets and back does not discard your Work session. The toggle is safe to use freely — treat it like walking between rooms, not like closing a document.

How it works Where the active pillar lives in code +

The active pillar is a single value — appMode — held in the Zustand store useSettingsStore (src/stores/). The App Mode toggle writes to it; pillar-aware components read from it to decide what to render.

Each pillar's pages live in their own folder under src/pages/ — src/pages/markets/, src/pages/portals/, src/pages/missions/, src/pages/school/, src/pages/life/, src/pages/community/ and so on — all lazy-loaded via routes in src/App.tsx. Server-side, each pillar has dedicated services and route files (server/services/market-*.ts, grow-service.ts, procure-service.ts, civic-service.ts, the server/services/portals/* stack) and its own migrations — for example Procure at migration 091, Civic at 092, Grow at 093, Portals at 145–148.

5Choosing the right pillar

The fastest way to choose is to ask what shape your task has, not what topic it is about.

Ask: what am I producing?

  • A document or analysis → Work
  • A market view backed by tracked predictions → Markets
  • A repeatable, multi-step automated job → Missions
  • A web space other ANTONs can use → Portals
  • A vendor decision with scoring → Procure
  • A sales pipeline view → Grow

Ask: who is it for?

  • A student or teacher → School
  • Your own personal finances or rights → Life
  • A citizen dealing with an authority → Civic
  • Another ANTON user you want to message → Community
  • Yourself, not sure where to start → Pathfinder
  • A payment or wallet action → Payments
📌Example — same topic, three different pillars

The topic is "a new supplier". If you need a memo weighing whether to sign them, that is a document — go to Work and open a Decision Memo. If you need to run a structured RFQ and score three bidders, that is a procurement cycle — go to Procure. If you want ANTON to monitor that supplier's news and flag anything material every week, that is a recurring automated job — go to Missions. One topic, three task shapes, three pillars. Match the shape, not the subject.

⚠If a module seems missing

Almost always you are in the wrong pillar. A module is only listed inside its own pillar's sidebar. Before assuming a capability does not exist, open the App Mode toggle and check whether another pillar is the right room — and check Build status for anything that is still partial.

6The two showcase pillars

Two pillars are more than features — they are ANTON's proofs of where the whole project is heading. They are worth understanding even if you never use them daily.

Markets — proof of self-learning intelligence

Markets is ANTON's evidence that the workspace can learn. Every day, real market outcomes are compared against the predictions ANTON made — validating or refuting both the forecast and the reasoning behind it, and recalibrating future confidence. It is the canonical example of an "intelligent pillar": a deterministic data loop wrapped around LLM reasoning. Any future learning pillar is built to this template.

Portals — proof of interoperability

Portals is ANTON's evidence that instances can talk to each other. Every portal is at once a human-facing web space and a machine-readable endpoint other ANTON instances can discover and invoke through the Agent Protocol. It proves the suite is not a set of islands but a network — the foundation for the collaborative-intelligence layer of ANTON's vision.

8Best practices

✓ Do
  • Choose the pillar by the shape of the task — what you are producing.
  • Switch pillars freely — each keeps its own state, nothing is lost.
  • Start in Pathfinder if you genuinely do not know where a task belongs.
  • Use Work as your home base — it is the mature core and most tasks live there.
  • Read the deep help page for any pillar you are about to lean on heavily.
✕ Don't
  • Don't hunt for a module before checking you are in the right pillar.
  • Don't expect data to follow you between pillars — they are separate worlds by design.
  • Don't assume every pillar is as mature as Work — check Build status.
  • Don't pick a pillar by topic alone — "a supplier" can belong in three different ones.
  • Don't avoid the App Mode toggle — switching is cheap and safe.
★The habit to build

Every task begins with one question: which room? Open the App Mode toggle, pick the pillar whose shape matches the work, and let its sidebar hand you the right tools. Mode before module — get that reflex, and all twelve pillars become one coherent workspace instead of twelve separate apps.
