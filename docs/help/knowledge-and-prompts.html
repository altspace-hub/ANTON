Knowledge sources & the prompt that gets built

Before any module runs, ANTON does two things you control: it decides where the AI gets reference material, and it assembles a layered system prompt that turns a frontier model into a domain expert. Get both right and a click on "Run" produces excellent output.

1What this page covers

Two linked systems sit behind every module run in ANTON Local.

(A) Knowledge sources answer the question "what is the AI allowed to look at?" — only its own training, web pages you name, files on your machine, or a blend. (B) The seven-layer prompt answers "who is the AI being, and how carefully is it thinking?" — it stacks system rules, your domain area, your organisation's context, the module's expertise, an optional persona, skills, the knowledge sources, and a transparency layer into one instruction set.

You do not have to understand the internals to get good results — the defaults are tuned. But knowing what the panels do lets you steer a run that the defaults would not nail.

2How to think about it

🧭The mental model

Think of a module run as briefing a consultant. The knowledge source is the folder of materials you hand them — nothing, a stack of web printouts, your own files, or a mix. The seven-layer prompt is the job description and house style — what kind of expert they are, who they work for, how formal to be, how hard to think. ANTON writes that briefing for you; the panels are where you adjust it.

Two ideas make the choices easy:

  • Knowledge source is about truth, prompt layers are about behaviour. If the AI needs facts it cannot have memorised — your contract, this week's news — change the knowledge source. If the output is factually fine but the wrong shape or tone, change the prompt (persona, thinking level, output format).
  • More is not better. Pointing at ten local folders or asking for maximum reasoning on a simple task costs time and money for no gain. Match the setting to the task.
💡The defaults are a real answer

Every module ships with a knowledge mode and thinking level already chosen by the people who built it. "Start with the problem, not the solution" means the first thing to try is just clicking Run. Tune the panels when the default output tells you something specific is missing — not pre-emptively.

3The four knowledge source modes

Every module has a Knowledge Source Panel with four modes. They differ only in where the AI's reference material comes from.

ModeWhere facts come fromBest for
Claude's KnowledgeThe model's built-in training, plus an optional live web searchGeneral expertise, well-established topics, anything before the knowledge cutoff
Online ReferencesURLs you paste — the server fetches each page and extracts the textA specific regulation, a named report, current pages you can point to
Local FoldersDirectories on your machine, indexed and word-countedYour own contracts, policies, evidence — material that must never leave the machine
CombinedTwo or more of the above merged under a priority ruleGrounding broad AI expertise in your specific documents

Combined mode — the priority rule

Combined mode is the powerful one, and the priority rule decides what wins when sources disagree:

  • Local-first — your documents are authoritative; AI knowledge fills gaps. Use for compliance work where your own policy is the truth.
  • AI-first — the model leads; your files are supporting context. Use for analysis where you want broad expertise, lightly grounded.
  • Merged — both weighed together with no built-in winner. Use for research where you genuinely want the synthesis.
⚠Local Folders are path-whitelisted

ANTON will only read directories listed in the ALLOWED_FOLDER_PATHS environment variable. This is a deliberate safety boundary — it stops a module (or a connector) reaching anywhere else on the disk. If a folder you expect is not selectable, it has not been added to that whitelist. See Install & first run.

📌Example — choosing a source for a gap assessment

You are assessing your firm's AML policy against the EU AMLR. The regulation text is stable and public — but your policy is private and specific. The right choice is Combined, local-first: point Local Folders at the directory holding your policy documents, let Claude's Knowledge supply the AMLR framework. The AI compares your real text against the regulation without your policy ever leaving the machine.

How it works Knowledge resolution at request time +

When a module runs, the selected mode is resolved into actual reference text by resolveKnowledgeSources in server/services/knowledge-resolver.ts. Online References are fetched server-side and stripped to plain text; Local Folders are indexed and word-counted with every path checked against ALLOWED_FOLDER_PATHS via path.resolve prefix matching. The resolved material becomes Layer 6 of the prompt (see §4). Web search, when enabled, is passed through as the Anthropic web_search tool rather than pre-fetched.

Personas & audience proxies

Layer 4 lets you stack a persona on top of the module. There are 8+ named expert roles — a sceptical auditor, a regulator, a board chair, a seasoned consultant — and 10+ audience proxies that make the AI write for a reader: explain it as if to a board, a journalist, a new joiner. A persona changes voice and emphasis without changing the facts.

How it works prompt-builder.ts and the layers +

server/services/prompt-builder.ts documents the structure in its own header: "ANTON's system prompt is composed in seven KINDS of context. Sub-layers extend Layers 2 and 4 — twelve assembly points in total but seven conceptual layers." Layer 1 is the CREATIVITY_INSTRUCTIONS map (strict / balanced / creative) plus the plan-first and structure-reference instructions. Layer 2 reads server/areas/{area}/area-context.md; buildOrgContextLayer and buildKnowledgePackLayer add 2a and 2b. Layer 3 is the module's system-prompt.md. Layer 4 is getExpertRoleInstruction with buildResumeContextLayer for 4a. Layer 6 calls resolveKnowledgeSources plus buildAtomLayer. The full visual map lives at /docs/architecture/11-seven-layer-prompt-builder.md.

5Thinking levels & choosing a model

Layer 7 carries the thinking level — how much deliberation the model does before answering. Five levels trade speed and cost against depth.

LevelUse it forOn Claude Opus 4.7
quickLookups, simple drafts, format conversionseffort: low
thinkThe everyday default — most module runseffort: medium
think_hardAnalysis with trade-offs, gap assessmentseffort: high
investigateHard, multi-step reasoning where being right matters mosteffort: max
plan_firstBig tasks — the model plans, then executes the planeffort: max
💡Don't reach for "investigate" by reflex

Higher thinking levels are slower and cost more, and on a straightforward task they rarely improve the answer. Use think for most work; step up to think_hard or investigate only when the task genuinely has competing considerations the model must weigh. plan_first shines on long, structured deliverables.

6Configuring a run, step by step

Putting it together — the order you actually touch the panels.

  1. Open the module & read the problem statement

    Every module opens with a problem statement and pre-tuned behaviour. Read it — it tells you what the defaults assume.

  2. Decide if the AI needs your facts

    If the task depends on documents the model can't have memorised, set the Knowledge Source Panel — usually Combined, local-first. If not, leave it on Claude's Knowledge.

  3. Pick a thinking level

    Leave it at think unless the task has real trade-offs to weigh, in which case step up to think_hard.

  4. Add a persona, if it helps

    For a board memo, layer the "board chair" audience proxy. For a hard review, the "sceptical auditor" persona. Optional — skip if unsure.

  5. Choose the output format

    Select the format(s) you want before running — see Output & export.

  6. Run, then read the reasoning trail

    The transparency layer surfaces how the model reasoned. If the output misses, the trail usually shows which layer to adjust.

📌Example — a fully configured run

Task: draft a board memo on a new regulation's impact on your firm. You set Knowledge Source to Combined, AI-first (broad regulatory knowledge, lightly grounded in your org profile), thinking level to think_hard (real trade-offs to weigh), layer the "board chair" audience proxy so it writes for a non-specialist reader, and pick the Decision Memo output format. One click, and the seven layers plus your knowledge produce a memo in the right shape, tone and depth.

7Best practices

✓ Do
  • Try the defaults first — they are a real, tuned answer.
  • Switch to Combined, local-first whenever your own documents are the truth.
  • Use think for most work; reserve higher levels for genuine trade-offs.
  • Layer an audience proxy when the reader is non-specialist.
  • Read the reasoning trail when output misses — it points at the layer to fix.
✕ Don't
  • Don't point at folders that aren't on the ALLOWED_FOLDER_PATHS whitelist and expect them to load.
  • Don't crank thinking to maximum by reflex — it costs time and money for no gain on simple tasks.
  • Don't fix a tone problem by changing the knowledge source, or a facts problem with a persona.
  • Don't paste a dozen URLs when two will do — more sources dilute focus.
  • Don't assume a non-default model is better; Claude is the tuned baseline.
★Diagnose by layer

When a run disappoints, name the problem before you touch a panel. Wrong facts → knowledge source. Wrong shape → output format. Wrong voice → persona. Not deep enough → thinking level. The seven-layer model is not just architecture — it is a checklist for fixing a run in one move instead of five.
