ANTON-to-ANTON — two instances working together

One ANTON Local instance can talk to another: discover its agents, hand it a piece of a mission to run, and — in the near future — pay it for the work. This page is how that works today, the rules that govern it, and what is still on the way.

1What ANTON-to-ANTON means

Every ANTON Local instance is normally a private, local-first workspace. But two instances — yours and a colleague's, a client's, a partner firm's — can also be connected, so that work and knowledge can flow between them under the owners' control.

There are three things one ANTON can do with another:

🔎

Discover & ask

Find a Specialized Agent on a peer instance and put a question to it.

📨

Delegate a mission

Hand a mission task to a peer ANTON to run, and get a signed result back.

💸

Pay for it

Near-future. Attach an FTC amount to delegated work and settle on completion.

2How to think about it

🧭The mental model

Think of two ANTONs less like two computers on a network and more like two professionals who have exchanged business cards. Nothing happens until they've been introduced. After that, every message between them is signed — like initialled paperwork — and the receiving person always decides whether to act on it. ANTON never silently runs a stranger's work.

Under the surface there are three transport layers — worth knowing which is which:

LayerCarriesStatus
Community P2P — signed messages via a queueMission delegations, results — the workhorse todayBUILT
Remote agents — HTTPS calls to a peer's public agent endpointsDiscover & query a peer's Specialized AgentsBUILT
AAP — the ANTON Agent Protocol, real-time encrypted bundlesThe "proper" protocol — see Agent ProtocolSPEC + STUB

The important takeaway: the polished real-time protocol (AAP) is still being wired up, but a working path already exists — signed messages over the Community P2P queue. That is what mission delegation runs on.

3The guidelines — the trust model

Anything crossing between two ANTONs obeys four firm rules. They are not settings you can switch off — they are the design.

① Introduced first, always

Two instances must be connected as community contacts before anything flows. Each holds the other's contact hash (ANTON-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX, derived from an Ed25519 key) and public key. An un-introduced peer is rejected outright.

② Everything is signed

Every delegation and every result is Ed25519-signed by the sender and verified on arrival. If a signature does not verify, the item is marked failed and cannot be acted on — no override.

③ The receiving human decides

Nothing auto-runs on the peer. A delegation lands in an inbox; the peer's owner explicitly accepts or declines. Accepted work runs as a normal mission with its usual human checkpoints.

④ Both sides keep the receipts

Every step — created, sent, received, accepted, completed, approved — is written to a signed audit log on both instances. Who did what, when, is always reconstructable.

⚠A signature failure is final

If an incoming delegation or result fails signature verification, that is treated as a security event, not a glitch. The item is dead — you re-establish the connection and the sender re-sends. Do not look for a "force accept" button; there isn't one, by design.

4Connecting two ANTONs

This is the one-time introduction. Do it once per pair of instances.

  1. Open the Community pillar on both instances

    Each ANTON has a contact hash — its public, shareable address. Find it in Community.

  2. Exchange contact hashes

    Instance A adds instance B's ANTON-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX hash as a contact, and B adds A's. Share the hashes over a channel you trust — they are safe to show, but you want to be sure whose hash it is.

  3. Accept the connection

    Each side accepts the other. Both instances now hold the peer's contact hash, public key and endpoint in their contact list — the introduction is complete.

  4. You're connected

    From here, A can discover B's agents, and either side can delegate a mission task to the other.

How it works The connection record +

A connected peer is a row in community_connections — it holds the peer's contact_hash, public_key (Ed25519, used to verify every signature from that peer), endpoint (HTTPS URL) and a preferred_transport. The status must be accepted or active for any A2A operation to proceed.

Identity itself lives in instance_identity — the instance's own Ed25519 private key, AES-256-GCM encrypted at rest. The contact hash is the first 16 bytes of SHA-256(public key), so a hash can always be checked against the key that signs with it. See Pairing & enrollment for the identity primitives.

5Delegating a mission to a peer

This is the heart of ANTON-to-ANTON, and it is built and working. One instance hands a mission task to a connected peer, the peer runs it, and a signed result comes back.

  1. Create a mission and decompose it

    On the originating ANTON, build a mission in the Missions pillar and let it break the objective into tasks.

  2. Delegate a task

    On a task, choose Delegate, pick the connected peer, and write the brief: a title, the objective, any context, the modules the peer should use, the expected output, a deadline, and — optionally — an FTC payment amount. This creates a draft delegation.

  3. Send it

    Sending signs the brief with your instance's Ed25519 key and queues it for delivery to the peer. Its status moves to sent.

  4. Wait for the result

    The peer accepts, runs the work, and submits a signed result. It arrives in your Outbound delegations tab.

  5. Verify and approve

    Your ANTON checks the result's signature against the peer's public key. You review the result and Approve or Reject it.

📌Example — splitting an AMLR programme

A consultancy runs the AMLR-readiness mission for a client. The independent audit brief task should be done by a different firm for independence. The consultancy delegates just that task to the audit firm's ANTON, with the brief and a deadline. The audit firm's ANTON runs it as its own sub-mission, with its own checkpoints, and sends back a signed deliverable. The consultancy's audit trail records exactly who produced it and when.

💡One task at a time, for now

Today you delegate a single task. Handing a peer a whole multi-task sub-graph as one unit, and being notified the moment they accept (not only when results return), are known near-term improvements — see the docs/A2A_ROADMAP.md roadmap.

6On the receiving side

When a peer delegates to you, here is what you see and do.

  1. It appears in your Mission Inbox

    The signed delegation lands in the Missions inbox. ANTON has already verified the signature against the sender's public key.

  2. Accept or decline

    You read the brief and decide. Declining notifies the sender with an optional reason. Accepting creates a local sub-mission — linked back to the delegation — that you own.

  3. Run the sub-mission

    It behaves like any mission you created yourself: decompose, approve the plan, advance through tasks, clear checkpoints. It starts at check-in autonomy, so you stay in control.

  4. Submit the result

    When it's done, submit the result. Your ANTON signs it and sends it back to the originator.

How it works Mission delegation internals +

The delegation flow lives in server/services/missions/mission-delegation.ts and server/routes/mission-delegation.ts; the data model is migration 120_missions_delegation.sql — tables mission_delegations and mission_delegation_log.

Transport is not AAP yet — it rides the existing Community P2P queue: the signed brief is written to community_mail with message_type = 'mission_delegation' and queued in community_message_queue. The peer's /api/p2p/receive hands it to /missions/p2p/receive, which verifies the Ed25519 signature before recording it. Accepting writes a new missions.missions row with an origin_delegation_id back-reference. The whole protocol is BUILT; the only stub is a "peer has accepted" notification back to the originator.

7Paying for the work — the near future

Delegating naturally raises the question: can the peer be paid for the work? The honest answer today is not yet — but the groundwork is laid.

What's already there

  • The delegation brief already carries an optional payment_amount_ftc field.
  • Delegated tasks support quality-linked terms (full pay at high quality, partial lower down).
  • A full payment pipeline exists — propose → approve → execute — with budgets and a separation-of-duties rule.
  • The ISO 20022 PACS.008 record is correctly assembled for every payment.

What's missing

  • The FutureChain rail runs in stub mode — wallet addresses and transaction IDs are placeholders; no money actually moves.
  • The blocker is the FutureChain Rust core (wallet.rs, iso20022_pacs008.rs, the RPC client) — not yet vendored.
  • Pay-on-approval is now wired (Phase D0): approving a delegated result routes the payment through the mission payment pipeline — but against the stub gateway, with no settlement poller yet.
⚠Honest status

You can attach an FTC amount to a delegation today and the pipeline will record it — but settlement is a placeholder. Real ANTON-to-ANTON payment switches on when the FutureChain Rust core lands and the RPC client is implemented. The full plan is in docs/A2A_ROADMAP.md; the cross-cutting blocker is on the Build status page.

8Best practices

✓ Do
  • Verify a peer's contact hash through a trusted channel before accepting the connection.
  • Write delegation briefs the way you'd brief a colleague — objective, context, expected output, deadline.
  • Treat an accepted sub-mission like your own work: keep its checkpoints, review its output.
  • Use the audit log on both sides if there's ever a question about who did what.
  • Decline a delegation you can't do well — a clear decline beats a poor result.
✕ Don't
  • Don't act on anything from an un-introduced or signature-failed peer.
  • Don't expect a delegated task to run unattended — the peer's human gates it.
  • Don't rely on A2A payment yet — the amount is recorded but not settled.
  • Don't delegate work whose data the peer shouldn't see — the brief travels to them.
  • Don't assume real-time delivery — Community P2P is a signed queue, not a live channel.
★The simple rule

Delegate to another ANTON exactly what you would be comfortable handing a trusted external colleague: a clear brief, data they're allowed to see, and a result you will review before you rely on it.
