# Stage 7 — Risk Appetite (Socratic script)

This is the only stage where you have to make a real judgement call. The
calculator can tell you the residual; only you can say what's acceptable.

For each threat path with a residual position the pack flagged, I'll ask:

**1. Do you accept the residual position?**
- Within: monitor; you're done with this path
- Boundary: act when cost-effective; you're done with this path with a watch flag
- Outside: act now; we need a remediation plan
- Unacceptable: stop trading on this path until it's fixed, OR formally accept it as tolerated non-compliance with a named owner, end-state, and timeline

**2. If "act":**
- What's the action? (be specific — "upgrade the control" isn't enough; name the control and the upgrade)
- Who owns it? (name a person, not a role)
- By when? (a real date, not "Q3")
- How much will it cost? (rough estimate is fine)

**3. Sign-off.**
- For a small business: the owner signs off. For a board-governed business: the board signs off (or delegates to a committee with named members).
- The sign-off is captured with a date and a name. It does not require ANTON to generate it — but ANTON will produce a board-ready document for the meeting.

---

After this stage:

- A **company-wide appetite statement** is generated — one page, board-ready.
- Any path outside or unacceptable becomes a **remediation programme item**
  with owner, target date, and budget.
- The **escalation triggers** are confirmed (the pack ships defaults; you can add).
- The Atlas moves from `draft` to `active` and the maintenance cycle starts.

The Atlas isn't a one-off document. It's a living workspace. The next time
something changes — a new regulation, a new contract, an incident — the
Atlas updates and so does the appetite statement. That's the point.
